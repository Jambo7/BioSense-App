# SCIENTIFIC POSITION STATEMENT

BioSense is a premium wellness and preventative health-intelligence platform. It is not a medical device.
It does not diagnose disease, and it does not replace healthcare professionals.

Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase is best understood as **a marker of hepatobiliary stress and enzyme induction** — a
sensitive but non-specific enzyme, concentrated in the liver and bile ducts and involved in maintaining
glutathione (the body's main antioxidant), that rises when the hepatobiliary system is challenged by alcohol,
fatty liver, bile-flow problems, or enzyme-inducing medications, often **before** other liver enzymes or
symptoms appear — **not** a standalone verdict and **not** a diagnosis. Its meaning is inseparable from the
wider **liver-enzyme pattern** and from context: an isolated GGT rise often reflects alcohol, a medication, or
early metabolic (fatty-liver) change; GGT raised **with ALT** points to a hepatocellular pattern; GGT raised
**with ALP** (and near-normal ALT) points to a biliary pattern; and GGT is what tells you a raised **ALP** is
coming from the liver rather than bone. GGT is, as clinicians put it, a *corroborating witness, not the verdict*
— it does not measure liver function and does not name the cause.

So BioSense reads GGT **with its companions** (ALT, AST, and the future ALP and Bilirubin), **never in
isolation**, begins with biological context — alcohol, medications, obesity, metabolic health, smoking, recent
illness, exercise, and sex/age/hormonal state all move GGT — ranks the plausible explanations for an elevated
value rather than asserting one, shows where reference ranges genuinely differ rather than splitting them, and
names no condition. It is careful with alcohol in particular: GGT is sensitive to alcohol but **cannot** prove
or quantify drinking or certify a "safe" amount.

The BioSense Wellness Interpretation Bands here are **consumer wellness classifications, not diagnostic
criteria.** Every BioSense interpretation is version controlled, transparent, and designed to evolve as the
evidence evolves.

# 1. Biomarker Overview

Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT, also gamma-GT or GGTP) is an enzyme found mainly in the liver and the
epithelial cells lining the bile ducts. <cite index="1-1">GGT helps the liver metabolise drugs and toxins. If the liver is damaged, GGT levels may rise as it leaks into the bloodstream.</cite> It also has an antioxidant role — <cite index="5-1">GGT is an enzyme that helps shuttle amino acids and maintain glutathione, the body's primary antioxidant.</cite> **[A][E1]**

The defining feature for interpretation is that GGT is **sensitive but non-specific** and rises **early**.
<cite index="5-1">It is one of the earliest liver markers to rise when the hepatobiliary system is challenged</cite>, reacting to alcohol, fatty liver, bile obstruction, or enzyme-inducing medications — often before ALT/AST or symptoms. But <cite index="1-1">a GGT result cannot diagnose the specific cause of liver problems, so it is normally carried out alongside other liver markers, such as alkaline phosphatase (ALP), or a scan.</cite> This is exactly why GGT is read **with** the wider liver-enzyme pattern (ALT, AST, and the future ALP and Bilirubin), never alone — it is, as clinicians put it, a *corroborating witness, not the verdict*. **[A][E2][E16]**

GGT is also strongly **context-dependent**: <cite index="1-1">abnormal results are most pronounced in chronic heavy drinkers, but even small amounts of alcohol can cause elevated GGT levels. A normal value does not necessarily rule out alcohol misuse, either.</cite> Alcohol, medications, metabolic health, and sex/age/hormonal state all move it. So BioSense reads GGT **with its companions**, in context, and names no condition. **[B][B2]**

# 2. Physiological Function

GGT sits on the membranes of liver cells and bile-duct epithelium, where it participates in the gamma-glutamyl
cycle — transferring glutamyl groups and helping recover the amino acids needed to synthesise **glutathione**,
the body's principal intracellular antioxidant. **[A][E1]** Because it is membrane-bound and inducible, GGT
production **increases** when the hepatobiliary system is challenged — by alcohol, by bile-flow obstruction, and
by drugs that induce hepatic metabolising enzymes — and it also leaks into the blood when liver cells are
stressed. **[A][E2][E12]**

Two features define interpretation **[A]**:
- **GGT is inducible and early, but non-specific.** It rises before ALT/AST in alcohol, biliary, and drug-
  induction settings — a sensitive early signal — but many things raise it, so it localises poorly on its own. **[A][E2][E12]**
- **Its meaning is pattern-dependent.** Whether ALT/AST are also raised (hepatocellular), whether ALP is raised
  (biliary), and whether a medication or alcohol is in play determine what a raised GGT means — so the LFT
  pattern and context are essential. **[A][E13][E14]**

---

# 3. Scientific Background

Three scientific themes shape how BioSense represents GGT. **[A]**

**First, GGT means little in isolation.** It is <cite index="16-1">a non-specific but highly sensitive marker of liver damage and cholestasis</cite>, and its role is to **corroborate and localise**: <cite index="16-1">ALP and GGT are interpreted together to localise the source of raised ALP in the blood: an ALP rise with normal GGT suggests bone disease</cite>, whereas an ALP rise with a raised GGT points to the hepatobiliary system. A practical framework is that <cite index="12-1">isolated GGT elevation often indicates alcohol use, medication induction, or early metabolic liver disease; GGT elevated alongside ALT indicates hepatocellular injury; GGT elevated alongside ALP (with normal ALT) points toward biliary/cholestatic disease.</cite> BioSense therefore treats GGT **plus the LFT pattern and context** as the unit of interpretation. **[A][E13][E14][E16]**

**Second, the reference ranges genuinely differ.** They are sex-specific and vary widely by lab and country —
<cite index="10-1">for adult males the reference range is 8 to 61 U/L and for adult females 5 to 36 U/L</cite> at one laboratory, while others cite men 10–71 and women 6–42, and internationally <cite index="9-1">men ≤ 50 U/L and women ≤ 40 U/L in Canada and Finland; men ≤ 28 U/L and women ≤ 18 U/L in Australia; men and women ≤ 50 U/L in Korea; men and women 12–58 U/L in Japan</cite>. Wellness sources add an "optimal" target below ~20–30 U/L. BioSense **presents these frameworks side by side and never averages them.** **[A][E3][E5][E8][E6]**

**Third, GGT is easily confounded and cannot stand alone as evidence.** It <cite index="13-1">can rise within days of enzyme-inducing drugs such as phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, and occasionally antibiotics, and can also stay elevated for weeks after alcohol intake falls, so a one-off value cannot reliably calculate alcohol consumption or prove alcohol-related disease</cite>. And crucially, <cite index="11-1">an isolated non-progressive elevation of GGT seldom reflects significant liver disease and usually does not require specialist referral.</cite> A single value is best read with the panel and, if borderline, repeated after an alcohol/medication review. **[A][E12][E10][E17]**

**The wellness reading — [B]:** GGT is a context-first, LFT-pattern-paired, high-dominant hepatobiliary-stress
and enzyme-induction screen — read with ALT/AST/future ALP/Bilirubin, with sex/age/hormonal overlays, plausible
causes ranked rather than one asserted, reference disagreement shown honestly, borderline values repeated (after
an alcohol/medication review) before they count, and no condition named. It is deliberately careful with
alcohol.

**An honest boundary — [E]:** ranges are contested and sex/lab/country-dependent, GGT is non-specific and
confounded by alcohol/medications/metabolic state, and it does not measure liver function — so BioSense leans on
the LFT pattern and context and is explicit about confidence. **[E][E8][E16]**

# 4. Why GGT Matters

**1. It is an early, sensitive hepatobiliary-stress signal. [A][E2]** GGT often rises before other liver
enzymes in alcohol, biliary, and drug-induction settings — a useful early screen of liver-wellness. **[A]**

**2. Read with the panel, it localises the pattern. [A][E13][E14]** Alongside ALT/AST it distinguishes
hepatocellular from biliary patterns, and alongside ALP it tells you whether a raised ALP is coming from liver
or bone — the difference between a bare number and an interpretation. **[A]**

**3. Interpreted in context, it reflects metabolic and lifestyle burden. [A][E11][E23]** Isolated GGT often
tracks alcohol, medication induction, or early metabolic/fatty-liver change (oxidative stress), and persistently
raised GGT is associated in cohorts with metabolic and cardiovascular risk — useful wellness context. **[A][E21]**

**Why BioSense measures it — [C]:** GGT is a high-value, sensitive, early, but non-specific screen whose meaning
is multi-marker — the ideal case for Context-First interpretation, cross-biomarker intelligence, confidence
inheritance, sex/age-aware banding, ranked explanations, and guideline-disagreement handling, all while never
diagnosing liver disease, alcohol dependence, a biliary obstruction, hepatitis, or cancer.

# 5. Laboratory Measurement

GGT is measured on an automated analyser (part of the liver-function panel), reported in **U/L (≡ IU/L)** as an
**enzyme activity**. **[A][E1][E9]**

- **Units.** U/L is canonical; U/L ≡ IU/L (activity, not mass) — the same enzyme-activity unit as ALT/AST; no
  lipid/glucose/vitamin/creatinine/thyroid/haemoglobin factor applies. **[A][E9]**
- **Read with the panel.** GGT is interpreted with ALT, AST, and (where available) ALP and Bilirubin — never in
  isolation — and it localises a raised ALP (liver vs bone). **[A][E13][E14]**
- **Context matters.** Sex-specific ranges; age and hormonal state (postmenopausal/OCP higher; young/pregnant
  lower); alcohol; enzyme-inducing medications; metabolic health. **[A][E20][E12]**
- **Timing/trend.** GGT can lag acute biliary obstruction by ~24 h and stays raised for weeks after alcohol
  falls; the trend and the date a medication began can matter more than a single peak. **[A][E25]**
- **Companion panel.** Read with **ALT** (hepatocellular), **AST** (hepatocellular / AST:ALT), the future
  **ALP** (biliary localisation) and **Bilirubin**, and **hs-CRP/ferritin** (inflammation/metabolic context). **[A][E13]**

# 8. Measurement Limitations & the Never-In-Isolation Principle  *(Context-First basis — reused SCL-010)*

GGT's defining limitation is that **a value does not, on its own, define liver-health status** — it is a
sensitive but non-specific "corroborating witness" — which is why the Context-First gate (§0.2), the LFT-pattern
layer (§0.5), and the ranked-confound output apply. **[A][B2]**

## 8.1 GGT needs the panel — [A]
A raised value is a starting point; ALT/AST/ALP/Bilirubin localise the pattern (hepatocellular vs biliary), and
GGT localises a raised ALP (liver vs bone). GGT is never interpreted in isolation. **[A][E13][E14]**

## 8.2 It is non-specific and confounded — [A]
Alcohol, enzyme-inducing medications, metabolic state, and hormonal status all raise GGT; it stays elevated for
weeks after alcohol falls and can lag biliary obstruction; context and repeat are essential. **[A][E10][E12][E25]**

## 8.3 Ranges are contested — [A]
Sex-specific reference ranges differ by lab and country; wellness-optimal targets are evolving — shown as
frameworks, never averaged. **[A][E3][E8]**

## 8.4 It does not measure function — [A]
GGT reflects hepatobiliary **stress/induction**, not liver **function**; an isolated non-progressive elevation
seldom reflects significant disease. **[A][E16][E17]**

**How BioSense uses this — [C][D]:** the Context-First gate runs first; GGT is banded high-dominant and
sex-specific by ×ULN with age/hormonal overlays; the LFT pattern is emitted only with companions (else screen-
level + limited confidence); plausible causes are **ranked, not asserted**; the alcohol-caution, medication-
induction, and isolated-non-progressive-elevation nuances are surfaced; missing companions/context sets Context-
Required/Reduced confidence; and no condition is ever named.

# 9. Relationships With Other Biomarkers  *(cross-biomarker intelligence — reused SCL-010; pattern inheritance via SCL-016/017/018/019)*

GGT consumes its companion and context markers where available. **[A][C]**

- **ALT (SCL-014) — the hepatocellular companion. [A]** GGT raised **with** ALT points to a hepatocellular
  pattern; GGT raised with **normal** ALT (and raised ALP) points biliary. **[A][E13]**
- **AST (SCL-015) — hepatocellular / alcohol companion. [A]** An AST:ALT ratio >2:1 alongside a raised GGT is a
  classic alcohol-pattern hint (read with the De Ritis ratio, SCL-015). **[A][E13]**
- **Future ALP (SCL-022) — the biliary-localisation companion. [A]** GGT and ALP are interpreted **together**:
  ALP↑ with GGT↑ → hepatobiliary; ALP↑ with **normal** GGT → bone. Combined GGT+ALP is the biliary pattern. **[A][E14][E15]**
- **Future Bilirubin (SCL-023). [A]** Adds to the cholestatic picture (jaundice) when GGT/ALP are raised —
  context, routed, not diagnosed. **[A][E15]**
- **hs-CRP (SCL-006) / Ferritin (SCL-010). [A]** Inflammation/metabolic context (ferritin also an acute-phase
  reactant); support a metabolic/inflammatory-burden read alongside an isolated GGT rise. **[A][E23]**
- **(Context) alcohol, medications, obesity, metabolic health, smoking, recent illness, exercise. [A]** These
  are interpretation context that move GGT, never something BioSense advises changing beyond general wellness
  (and never a prompt to stop a prescribed medication). **[A][E10][E12]**

**Cross-biomarker rule [C] (reused R4/R9):** where these are **available**, BioSense consumes them (with the
LFT-pattern and confound caveats) to sharpen the read and confidence; where **unavailable** — especially ALT/AST
and the future ALP (without which only a screen-level statement is possible) — it records a **confidence
limitation** and names what would clarify, never inventing certainty. **[C][R4][R9]**

- **Alcohol context.** "GGT is sensitive to alcohol, but it can't measure how much someone drinks or prove
  alcohol as the cause on its own — and it tends to fall within a few weeks of drinking less." Never infer
  dependence (CAV8). **[B][D][E10]**
- **Enzyme-inducing medication (normal ALT/AST).** "Some medicines raise GGT simply by revving up liver enzymes
  rather than by harming the liver — that context matters here." Reassure + context (CAV9). **[B][D][E12]**

**LFT-pattern modifier:** where companions are available, present the pattern (isolated / hepatocellular /
biliary / alcohol / induction / ALP-localisation) as **context**; where companions are absent, give a
**screen-level** statement and name that the liver panel completes the picture (CAV3). The pattern confidence
**inherits the lower** input (§0.6). **[D][R4][R9]**

**Ranked-confounds modifier (reused):** on any elevated GGT with ≥2 plausible causes, present **Possible
Explanation A/B/C** ordered by evidence + context (alcohol, NAFLD/metabolic, medication-induction, biliary,
other) — never a single certain cause, never a named condition. **[D][R3]**

**Sex/age/hormonal overlay modifier:** apply the sex-specific ULN and the age/hormonal context; never a unisex
cut-off where sex-specific evidence exists (CAV10). **[D][E20]**

**Context-unavailable modifier:** where **companions** (or sex/age/hormonal/alcohol/medication context) are
missing, state the confidence limitation and name what would clarify (CAV3/CAV4); never invent certainty (S8).
**[D][R4]**

Every interpretation pairs the band and pattern with context guidance (§17) and the mandatory caveats (§0.10).
**None diagnoses liver disease, alcohol dependence, a biliary obstruction, hepatitis, or cancer, none asserts a
single cause, and none treats a BioSense band or pattern as a medical boundary.** **[D]**

# 15. Special Populations & Abstention

BioSense **abstains or requires context** where its bands don't apply or the picture is too uncertain. **[C]/[D]/[E]**

- **15.1 Context-required (common for GGT).** Elevated GGT with **no companions** (no LFT pattern) or an
  unexcluded confound (recent alcohol / new medication / acute illness) → screen-level + request companions/
  repeat; state what's needed (§13, CAV3/CAV6). **[D][R2]**
- **15.2 Alcohol.** GGT is sensitive to alcohol but **cannot** quantify or prove use; never infer dependence;
  educate and, where relevant, note it falls ~3–4 weeks after reducing intake. **[D][E10][E19]**
- **15.3 Enzyme-inducing medications.** Phenytoin/carbamazepine/phenobarbital/rifampin/certain statins raise GGT
  by induction; with normal ALT/AST this is typically induction, not injury — context, never a prompt to stop a
  prescribed medicine. **[D][E12]**
- **15.4 Suspected biliary pattern (GGT+ALP).** Combined GGT+ALP elevation → biliary-pattern context that
  warrants professional review/imaging; route, don't diagnose. **[D][E15]**
- **15.5 Metabolic / fatty-liver context.** Isolated GGT with obesity/insulin resistance/metabolic syndrome →
  metabolic (oxidative-stress) context, read with hs-CRP/ferritin; a wellness read, not a disease label. **[D][E11][E23]**
- **15.6 Sex, age & hormonal state.** Sex-specific ULN; higher postmenopausal/on OCP; lower young/pregnant;
  slight rise with age — interpret with these, don't over-flag. **[D][E20]**
- **15.7 Pregnancy.** GGT tends to be lower in pregnancy; a raised value in pregnancy (or a suspected biliary
  pattern such as intrahepatic cholestasis) is a professional matter — route. **[D][E20]**
- **15.8 Red flags.** Markedly elevated / rising GGT; combined GGT+ALP (biliary pattern); a hepatocellular
  pattern with high ALT/AST; GGT with jaundice or systemic symptoms → calm prompt healthcare review regardless
  of band. **[D][E13][E15]**

**Abstention and Context-Required are first-class, non-error outputs**, always explained. **[D]**

# 16. Trend & Longitudinal Behaviour

- **Trend beats a single value. [A]** GGT is non-specific and persists for weeks after alcohol falls, so a
  within-person **trend** (read with the panel) is more informative than one reading. **[E10][E25]**
- **Repeat borderline values with the panel. [A]** A new borderline value is repeated **with** ALT/AST/ALP,
  after an alcohol/medication review, before it means anything; an isolated non-progressive elevation is common
  and often benign. **[E17][E19]**
- **Withdrawal-trial logic. [A]** Normalisation after 1–3 months off alcohol or a suspected medication
  **implicates** that substance — a powerful, non-diagnostic interpretive tool. **[E19]**
- **Timing matters. [A]** GGT can **lag** acute biliary obstruction by ~24 h, and the **date a medication began**
  can be more revealing than a single peak. **[E25]**
- **Context/abstained points. [C]** Recent alcohol, a new enzyme-inducer, acute illness, and context-required
  points are tagged so they don't create a false trend.

# 17. Lifestyle & Context Guidance

For GGT, the first tier is **context and the liver panel** (ALT/AST/future ALP above all), then context-
appropriate lifestyle. **[A]/[B]**

## 17.1 Panel & context first [A][E13][E14]
Where GGT is elevated, the clarifying steps are the **liver panel** (ALT, AST, and future ALP/Bilirubin), the
**context review** (alcohol, enzyme-inducing medications, metabolic health, smoking, recent illness), and — for
a new borderline value — a **repeat with the panel** after an alcohol/medication review. **[A]**

## 17.2 Liver-wellness context [A][E10][E11]
General liver-wellness — moderating **alcohol**, a **healthy weight**, managing **metabolic health**, and **not
smoking** — is relevant context; GGT tends to fall within a few weeks of reducing alcohol, and isolated
elevation is often benign. Framed as **education, not treatment**. **[A]**

## 17.3 Confound & exposure context [A][E12][E20]
Alcohol, enzyme-inducing medications, metabolic state, and hormonal status (postmenopausal/OCP/pregnancy) are
recognised context/confounds for a raised GGT — useful for interpretation, **never** a prompt to stop or change
a prescribed medication without professional advice. **[A]**

## 17.4 Framing rules [B][D]
Panel and context first (repeat for new borderline, after alcohol/medication review); **no specific treatments,
medication changes, or doses** (S9); reference-range disagreement shown, never averaged; alcohol handled with
care (sensitive but cannot quantify/prove — never infer dependence); calm, evidence-informed language; never a
diagnosis; the panel-paired (CAV2), screen-only (CAV3), alcohol (CAV8), induction (CAV9), and reference (CAV10)
caveats attached where relevant.

# 19. Safety Considerations

- **Not a diagnosis; named conditions never diagnosed.** Every output carries CAV1; BioSense describes patterns,
  never names liver disease/alcohol dependence/biliary obstruction/hepatitis/cancer (S1). **[D][R7]**

  one (S4, CAV6). **[D][R3]**

  with age (S5, CAV10). **[D][E20]**
- **Repeat-first + isolated-elevation reassurance.** New borderline → repeat with the panel after an alcohol/
  medication review; isolated non-progressive elevation seldom reflects significant disease (S7, CAV5). **[D][E17]**
- **Calm red-flag routing.** Markedly elevated/rising, GGT+ALP biliary pattern, hepatocellular pattern with high
  ALT/AST, or jaundice/systemic symptoms → prompt, unhurried review; never emergency-diagnose (S10, CAV7). **[D][E15]**
- **No treatment/medication guidance.** Never advise stopping/changing a prescribed medicine or an alcohol-
  cessation drug; educate + refer (S9). **[D]**
- **Missing companions/context stated, not invented.** (S8). **[D][R4]**
- **Correct unit handling.** U/L ≡ IU/L enzyme activity (no mass factor; not a count); LFT pattern requires

# 22. Medication, Alcohol & Exposure Context (educational only)

Educational context only; BioSense does not instruct on treatment, dose, or medication changes (S9). **[D]**
- **Alcohol:** the most sensitive routine marker of alcohol-related liver stress, but it **cannot** quantify
  intake or prove alcohol as the cause; falls ~3–4 weeks after reducing intake — context, never a dependence
  inference. **[A][E10][E19]**
- **Enzyme-inducing medications** (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, rifampin, certain statins,
  barbiturates): raise GGT by hepatic-enzyme **induction** (often with normal ALT/AST) — context, never a prompt
  to stop a prescribed medicine. **[A][E12]**

  via oxidative stress/steatosis — wellness context, read with hs-CRP/ferritin. **[A][E11][E23]**
- **Other:** heart failure, hyperthyroidism, pancreatitis, viral hepatitis, biliary conditions — clinical
  contexts mentioned only as possibilities, never diagnosed. **[A][E18]**
- Any medication, alcohol, or exposure question → educational context + suggestion to speak with a healthcare
  professional; BioSense never advises starting, stopping, or changing a medication. **[D]**

# 23. Open Questions & Areas of Uncertainty [E]

1. **GGT needs the panel. [E]** Alone it is a screen — a corroborating witness; the LFT pattern and confidence
   inheritance handle this. **[E13][E16]**
2. **Reference ranges are contested. [E]** Sex-specific; differ across labs/countries; shown, never averaged. **[E3][E8]**
3. **Wellness-optimal targets are evolving. [E]** <20 / <25–30 U/L shown as targets, not cut-offs. **[E5][E6]**
4. **GGT is non-specific and confounded. [E]** Alcohol, medications, metabolic state, hormones all raise it;
   context and repeat mitigate. **[E10][E12]**
5. **It does not measure liver function. [E]** Reflects stress/induction; isolated non-progressive elevation is
   often benign. **[E16][E17]**
6. **Risk associations are epidemiological. [E]** Metabolic/CVD/mortality/bone associations are cohort-level,
   shown as context, not individual prediction. **[E21][E22]**
7. **Companion availability is data-dependent. [E]** Without the panel (esp. ALP), only a screen-level statement
   is possible; the pattern degrades to a confidence limitation, not certainty. **[R4][R9]**

9. **Lamkin Clinic (GGT: Optimal Levels & Interpretation).** — *biliary (PBC/PSC/choledocholithiasis/strictures)
   → GGT+ALP with normal/mild ALT/AST; enzyme-inducers induce P450 alongside GGT via a common nuclear-receptor
   pathway → elevated GGT with normal ALT/AST is induction not injury; metabolic syndrome/insulin resistance
   elevate GGT via oxidative stress/steatosis; pancreatitis; most sensitive marker for alcohol (E12, E15, E23).*
10. **BC Guidelines (Abnormal Liver Chemistry) & StatPearls (Liver Function Tests, NBK482489).** — *obstructed/
    damaged bile ducts induce ALP & GGT; acute biliary obstruction elevation may lag ~24 h; an isolated non-
    progressive GGT elevation seldom reflects significant liver disease and usually doesn't need referral;
    normalisation after 1–3 months off alcohol/medication implicates that substance; cholestatic pattern = ALP &
    GGT ↑ (E17, E19, E25, E15).*
11. **PMC7644547 (GGT prognostic value, IPAH) & PMC9385606 (GGT & hip fracture) & PMC3151286 (KORA-F4 liver
    enzymes & glucose).** — *sex/hormonal variation (higher men; ↓ young/pregnant; ↑ postmenopausal & on OCP);
    international reference variation (Canada/Finland ≤50/≤40; Australia ≤28/≤18; Korea ≤50; Japan 12–58; China
    ≤38); elevated GGT associated with hip-fracture risk in postmenopausal women independent of alcohol/liver
    disease; sex-specific enzyme reference values (E20, E8, E22, E21).*

> **Category B (BioSense Wellness Interpretation Bands)** are a synthesis of references 1–11; they are BioSense
> Version 1 classifications, high-dominant and sex-specific by ×ULN with age/hormonal overlays, not attributable
> to any single reference as a diagnostic threshold, and **do not restate diagnostic labels.** The differing
> sex-specific reference ranges, international cut-offs, and evolving wellness-optimal targets are shown
> separately and **never averaged**; GGT is presented as a panel-paired screen (a corroborating witness), never
> in isolation, never a diagnosis of liver disease/alcohol dependence/biliary obstruction/hepatitis/cancer, and
> alcohol is never quantified or used to infer dependence; the LFT pattern is a hint whose confidence inherits
> the lower input, never a standalone verdict.
