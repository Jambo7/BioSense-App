Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define how BioSense learns from an individual's long-term interactions, behaviours and preferences in order to personalise the platform while preserving constitutional integrity, scientific accuracy and user autonomy. Unlike the upstream intelligence engines, which develop an understanding of the individual's health, the Personalisation & Adaptive Learning Engine develops an understanding of the individual as a person. Its responsibility is to identify stable patterns that improve how BioSense communicates, educates, prioritises and supports behaviour over time. The engine shall never alter the underlying health intelligence established elsewhere within the BioSense platform.

Constitutional Principle
Learn about the person without changing the truth about their health.

1.2 Scope
This specification governs:
long-term personalisation, 
communication preferences, 
behavioural preferences, 
educational progression, 
intervention adherence, 
coaching preferences, 
routine recognition, 
habit recognition, 
engagement patterns, 
notification preferences, 
report preferences, 
adaptive personalisation, 
longitudinal user learning. 
It deliberately excludes:
Health Belief formation (ENG-001), 
observation processing (ENG-002), 
confidence evaluation (ENG-003), 
Decision Object generation (ENG-004), 
communication delivery (ENG-005). 
Its responsibility begins only after trusted health intelligence has already been established.

1.3 Authority
The Personalisation & Adaptive Learning Engine consumes outputs from all preceding constitutional engines.
It may consume:
Health Beliefs, 
Behavioural Identity, 
User Goals, 
Decision Objects, 
Structured Explanations, 
Conversation history, 
intervention outcomes, 
educational interactions, 
user preferences, 
behavioural engagement signals. 
It shall not modify upstream intelligence. Instead, it produces governed Personalisation Objects that may be consumed by other engines.

Engineering Rule
The Personalisation Engine is a read-only consumer of health intelligence and a producer of personalisation intelligence.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
No two individuals respond identically to the same health guidance.
Over time, BioSense should become increasingly effective at understanding:
how an individual learns, 
what motivates them, 
how they prefer information, 
which interventions they adopt, 
which educational approaches improve understanding, 
how their routines influence success. 
Learning exists to improve the user's experience of BioSense. It shall never distort scientific conclusions.

Founder Principle
Personalisation should improve relevance without compromising objectivity.

1.5 Constitutional Definition
For engineering purposes:
The Personalisation & Adaptive Learning Engine is the constitutional system responsible for developing governed, explainable and longitudinal models of an individual's behavioural and communication preferences without altering the underlying health intelligence of the BioSense platform.
The engine learns about the individual. It does not redefine the individual's health.

Constitutional Principle
Health intelligence and personalisation intelligence are separate constitutional domains.

1.6 Personalisation Is Not Reasoning
A strict architectural boundary exists between reasoning and personalisation.
Reasoning occurs within:
ENG-001 
ENG-002 
ENG-003 
ENG-004 
Communication occurs within:
ENG-005 
Personalisation occurs within:
ENG-006 
The Personalisation Engine shall never:
create Health Beliefs, 
modify observations, 
alter confidence, 
reprioritise Decision Objects, 
reinterpret evidence. 
It supplies personalisation signals to other engines. Those engines remain responsible for constitutional reasoning.

Engineering Rule
Personalisation informs behaviour.
It does not determine truth.

1.7 Personalisation Objects
The primary output of this engine shall be governed Personalisation Objects.
Examples include:
Communication Preference Object 
Coaching Preference Object 
Educational Progress Object 
Behavioural Adherence Object 
Routine Profile Object 
Notification Preference Object 
Habit Stability Object 
Engagement Pattern Object 
Each Personalisation Object shall possess:
provenance, 
confidence, 
version, 
update history, 
explainability, 
audit history. 

Founder Principle
Personalisation should be engineered as structured intelligence, not hidden behaviour.

1.8 Explainability
Every Personalisation Object shall remain explainable.
BioSense shall always be capable of explaining:
what has been learned, 
why it has been learned, 
which evidence supports it, 
how confident the platform is, 
which engines consume it, 
how it influences user experience. 
If BioSense cannot explain a learned preference, that preference is constitutionally incomplete.

Constitutional Principle
Every learned preference should itself be explainable.

1.9 Human Autonomy
The purpose of personalisation is to improve understanding and usability.
It shall never:
manipulate behaviour, 
reduce user autonomy, 
optimise for engagement, 
create dependency, 
prioritise commercial outcomes. 
Users shall remain free to override personalisation where appropriate. Personalisation exists to support the individual, not control them.

Founder Principle
Adapt to the individual without attempting to direct the individual.

1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ The Personalisation Engine is clearly separated from all reasoning engines.
✓ Personalisation Objects are established as first-class constitutional objects.
✓ Health intelligence and personalisation intelligence remain independent.
✓ Personalisation is explicitly prohibited from altering scientific conclusions.
✓ Human autonomy is preserved.
✓ Every Personalisation Object remains explainable and auditable.
✓ Architectural ownership boundaries remain unambiguous.
Chapter 2
Adaptive Learning Architecture

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the constitutional architecture through which BioSense develops, validates and maintains long-term personalisation knowledge. Learning within BioSense is a governed process. The platform shall not immediately convert individual interactions into permanent user preferences.
Instead, learning shall occur progressively through repeated observation, validation and confidence assessment. The objective is to develop stable personalisation intelligence that improves the user experience while remaining transparent, explainable and reversible.

Constitutional Principle
Learning should emerge from consistent evidence rather than isolated behaviour.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Every interaction between the individual and BioSense may provide evidence about personal preferences. However, individual interactions are often noisy.
A single behaviour may reflect:
curiosity, 
circumstance, 
experimentation, 
temporary need, 
or genuine long-term preference. 
The Adaptive Learning Architecture shall therefore distinguish between:
observed behaviour, 
emerging patterns, 
validated preferences. 
Only validated preferences shall influence future personalisation.

Founder Principle
Observe first. Learn second. Personalise third.

2.3 Learning Sources
The Personalisation Engine may learn from multiple categories of governed evidence.
Examples include:
Communication Behaviour
preferred explanation depth, 
request for additional detail, 
summary selection, 
reading completion. 

Educational Behaviour
completed educational content, 
repeated learning topics, 
requested scientific depth, 
educational progression. 

Behavioural Outcomes
intervention adherence, 
habit consistency, 
routine stability, 
goal persistence. 

Interaction Behaviour
conversation frequency, 
clarification requests, 
preferred delivery channels, 
feature utilisation. 

User Configuration
explicitly selected preferences, 
notification settings, 
language preferences, 
accessibility preferences. 
Each learning source contributes evidence.
None independently establish permanent preferences.

Engineering Rule
Every learning source contributes probabilistically rather than deterministically.

2.4 Learning Pipeline
Every learned preference shall progress through the same constitutional pipeline.
Behaviour

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Pattern Recognition

↓

Preference Candidate

↓

Confidence Assessment

↓

Personalisation Object

↓

Consumed by Other Engines
Learning shall never bypass this process.

Constitutional Principle
Personalisation should be earned through evidence.

2.5 Preference Candidates
Potential preferences shall first exist as Preference Candidates.
Examples include:
"This individual may prefer concise explanations."
"This individual may respond well to coaching."
"This individual may prefer weekly reports."
Preference Candidates remain provisional.
They shall not influence platform behaviour until sufficiently validated.

Founder Principle
Potential preferences deserve observation before trust.

2.6 Learning Confidence
Every Preference Candidate shall possess its own confidence assessment.
Confidence may increase through:
repeated confirmation, 
behavioural consistency, 
longitudinal stability, 
explicit user confirmation, 
successful application. 
Confidence may decrease through:
contradictory behaviour, 
inactivity, 
inconsistent usage, 
preference changes, 
explicit user override. 
Learning confidence shall remain independent from health confidence.

Engineering Rule
The certainty of a personal preference shall never be confused with the certainty of a health conclusion.

2.7 Preference Promotion
Only Preference Candidates meeting constitutional promotion criteria shall become Personalisation Objects.
Promotion shall consider:
confidence, 
stability, 
consistency, 
duration, 
behavioural evidence, 
explicit user preferences where available. 
Promotion shall remain fully explainable.

Constitutional Principle
Promotion from observation to preference requires sufficient longitudinal evidence.

2.8 Preference Evolution
Personal preferences naturally change over time. The Personalisation Engine shall therefore support gradual evolution.
Examples include:
A user initially prefers concise explanations.
↓
Requests increasingly detailed evidence.
↓
Preference confidence shifts gradually.
↓
Communication Profile adapts progressively.
Learning shall favour continuity over abrupt change.

Founder Principle
People evolve. Personalisation should evolve with them.

2.9 Preference Decay
Preferences that are no longer supported shall gradually lose confidence.
Examples include:
unused communication styles, 
abandoned routines, 
obsolete coaching preferences, 
inactive goals. 
Decay shall occur gradually.
Historical evidence shall remain available for audit.

Constitutional Principle
Learning should be reversible when behaviour genuinely changes.

2.10 Explainability
Every learned preference shall answer:
What has been learned? 
Which behaviours contributed? 
How much evidence exists? 
Why was confidence assigned? 
When was the preference promoted? 
Which engines consume this preference? 
Could this preference change in future? 

2.11 Engineering Constraints
The Adaptive Learning Architecture shall:
distinguish behaviour from preference, 
require longitudinal evidence, 
support reversible learning, 
preserve learning confidence, 
maintain explainability, 
remain fully auditable, 
preserve constitutional boundaries. 

2.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every learned preference follows the constitutional learning pipeline.
✓ Preference Candidates remain distinct from Personalisation Objects.
✓ Learning confidence remains independent from health confidence.
✓ Promotion requires sufficient longitudinal evidence.
✓ Preferences evolve gradually.
✓ Unsupported preferences decay over time.
✓ Every learned preference remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 3
Personalisation Model & Canonical Personalisation Objects

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the canonical Personalisation Model used by BioSense to represent long-term knowledge about an individual's preferences, behaviours and interaction characteristics. The Personalisation Model provides a structured, governed representation of learned user preferences that may be consumed by other constitutional engines.
It deliberately excludes health intelligence.
Its responsibility is to model how BioSense should interact with the individual, not what BioSense believes about the individual's health.

Constitutional Principle
The Personalisation Model describes the individual, not the individual's health.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
Personalisation should be represented using structured, explainable objects rather than hidden application settings or opaque machine learning outputs.
Every learned characteristic shall exist as a governed Personalisation Object possessing:
provenance, 
confidence, 
version, 
history, 
explainability, 
auditability. 
The Personalisation Model therefore becomes the single constitutional source of personalisation intelligence across the BioSense platform.

Founder Principle
If BioSense has learned something about the individual, it should exist as structured intelligence rather than hidden behaviour.

3.3 Canonical Personalisation Domains
The Personalisation Model shall consist of multiple independent domains. Each domain represents a distinct aspect of long-term user understanding.
Domains may include:
Communication
How the individual prefers information to be presented.

Education
How the individual learns most effectively.

Behaviour
How the individual adopts behavioural change.

Motivation
What encourages sustained engagement with health improvement.

Routine
Recurring habits and predictable behavioural patterns.

Coaching
Preferred coaching and support style.

Interaction
How the individual naturally interacts with BioSense.

Accessibility
Preferred communication accessibility requirements.
Each domain evolves independently.

Engineering Rule
No Personalisation Domain shall infer conclusions outside its constitutional responsibility.

3.4 Personalisation Objects
Each domain shall contain one or more Personalisation Objects.
Illustrative examples include:
Communication Preference Object
Preferred explanation depth.
Preferred report format.
Preferred detail level.

Educational Progress Object
Health literacy progression.
Completed learning topics.
Concept mastery.

Behavioural Adherence Object
Intervention completion patterns.
Consistency trends.
Behavioural persistence.

Routine Profile Object
Preferred exercise timing.
Sleep routine stability.
Meal timing consistency.

Motivation Profile Object
Goal-oriented motivation.
Data-driven motivation.
Achievement motivation.
Educational motivation.

Coaching Preference Object
Supportive.
Analytical.
Reflective.
Action-focused.

Interaction Pattern Object
Preferred channels.
Conversation frequency.
Report usage.
Notification responsiveness.

Accessibility Preference Object
Language.
Reading complexity.
Alternative presentation requirements.

Constitutional Principle
Every learned characteristic should belong to one clearly defined Personalisation Object.

3.5 Object Structure
Every Personalisation Object shall contain:
Object Identifier, 
Domain, 
Current State, 
Confidence, 
Evidence Summary, 
Supporting Behaviours, 
Update History, 
Version, 
Explainability Metadata, 
Consumer Engines. 
The internal implementation remains platform-specific.
The constitutional structure shall remain consistent.

Founder Principle
Every Personalisation Object should answer who, what, why, when and how confidently.

3.6 Relationships Between Objects
Personalisation Objects may influence one another.
Examples include:
Educational Progress
↓
Communication Preference
↓
Conversation Depth

Behavioural Adherence
↓
Coaching Preference
↓
Intervention Delivery

Routine Profile
↓
Notification Timing
↓
Intervention Success
Relationships shall be explicitly defined.
Implicit dependencies shall be avoided.

Engineering Rule
Relationships between Personalisation Objects shall remain transparent and auditable.

3.7 Object Lifecycle
Every Personalisation Object shall progress through a governed lifecycle.
Illustrative stages include:
Candidate
↓
Emerging
↓
Validated
↓
Active
↓
Declining
↓
Archived
Lifecycle transitions shall be evidence-based and version-controlled.

Constitutional Principle
Personalisation should mature through evidence rather than assumption.

3.8 Consumer Engines
Personalisation Objects may be consumed by multiple constitutional engines.
Examples include:
ENG-004
Decision Intelligence Engine
Consumes:
Behavioural Adherence.
Routine Profile.
Motivation Profile.

ENG-005
Conversation Engine
Consumes:
Communication Preferences.
Educational Progress.
Coaching Preferences.
Accessibility Preferences.

Future engines may consume Personalisation Objects through governed interfaces.
Consumer engines remain read-only.

Engineering Rule
Only the Personalisation Engine owns Personalisation Objects.
Other engines consume them.

3.9 Explainability
Every Personalisation Object shall answer:
What has been learned? 
Which evidence supports it? 
Why does BioSense believe this preference exists? 
How confident is the platform? 
Which engines currently consume this object? 
Has this preference changed over time? 

3.10 Engineering Constraints
The Personalisation Model shall:
remain domain-based, 
preserve object ownership, 
support independent evolution, 
maintain explainability, 
preserve auditability, 
support future extensibility, 
remain constitutionally consistent. 

3.11 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ The Personalisation Model is established as the canonical representation of user personalisation intelligence.
✓ Every learned characteristic belongs to a governed Personalisation Domain.
✓ Personalisation Objects possess structured metadata and lifecycle states.
✓ Relationships between Personalisation Objects remain explicit.
✓ Consumer engine ownership boundaries remain unambiguous.
✓ Every Personalisation Object remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 4
Behavioural Learning & Intervention Adaptation

4.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how BioSense learns from an individual's behavioural responses to interventions in order to improve future personalisation. The Personalisation Engine shall observe how the individual engages with recommendations over time, identifying stable behavioural characteristics that influence future communication, coaching and intervention delivery. Its objective is not to determine whether an intervention was clinically successful. Its objective is to understand how the individual responds to behavioural change.

Constitutional Principle
Learn how the individual responds to change without redefining the intervention itself.

4.2 Engineering Philosophy
Individuals respond differently to identical health recommendations.
Examples include:
some adopt changes immediately, 
some require repeated education, 
some succeed with gradual progression, 
some prefer structured plans, 
some respond better to autonomy. 
These behavioural differences represent valuable personalisation intelligence. The Personalisation Engine shall therefore learn behavioural characteristics independently from clinical outcomes.

Founder Principle
Successful coaching begins with understanding how the individual changes, not simply what they should change.

4.3 Behavioural Evidence Sources
Behavioural learning may utilise evidence including:
intervention acceptance, 
intervention completion, 
intervention abandonment, 
behavioural persistence, 
routine consistency, 
goal progression, 
voluntary engagement, 
educational completion, 
user-configured preferences, 
longitudinal adherence patterns. 
Behavioural evidence reflects user behaviour.
It does not evaluate clinical effectiveness.

Engineering Rule
Behavioural evidence shall remain distinct from physiological evidence.

4.4 Behavioural Learning Pipeline
Behavioural learning shall follow a governed constitutional process.
Decision Object

↓

Behavioural Response

↓

Behavioural Evidence

↓

Pattern Recognition

↓

Behavioural Preference Candidate

↓

Learning Confidence

↓

Behavioural Personalisation Object
