Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the constitutional architecture responsible for protecting users, maintaining clinical safety and ensuring that every intelligence capability within the BioSense Intelligence Platform operates within clearly governed boundaries. The Constitutional Safety, Clinical Governance & Risk Management Engine provides the platform-wide safety framework responsible for identifying clinical risk, enforcing constitutional guardrails, coordinating escalation and preventing unsafe intelligence from being delivered to users. Its responsibility is not to generate health intelligence. Its responsibility is to ensure that all health intelligence remains constitutionally safe, proportionate and appropriate for preventive healthcare.

Constitutional Principle
Safety shall govern every intelligence outcome before it reaches the user.

1.2 Scope
This specification governs:
Constitutional Safety Objects, 
clinical safety, 
risk identification, 
constitutional guardrails, 
escalation pathways, 
emergency detection, 
contraindication governance, 
uncertainty escalation, 
safe failure, 
publication approval, 
clinical governance, 
user protection, 
risk lifecycle, 
safety auditability. 
It deliberately excludes:
Health Beliefs (ENG-001), 
observation processing (ENG-002), 
confidence evaluation (ENG-003), 
decision generation (ENG-004), 
explanation generation (ENG-005), 
personalisation (ENG-006), 
prediction generation (ENG-007), 
scientific knowledge ownership (ENG-008), 
workflow orchestration (ENG-009). 
This engine owns safety.
Other engines own intelligence.

1.3 Authority
The Constitutional Safety Engine operates across every constitutional engine.
Illustrative participating systems include:
ENG-001 
ENG-002 
ENG-003 
ENG-004 
ENG-005 
ENG-006

review,

escalate, 
delay, 
annotate, 
block publication, 
where constitutional safety requires.
It shall never modify constitutional intelligence directly.

Engineering Rule
The Safety Engine governs publication rather than reasoning.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
Powerful AI systems require equally powerful safety systems.
The BioSense platform shall therefore separate:
intelligence generation, 
intelligence safety. 
Every constitutional engine may generate intelligence. Only the Constitutional Safety Engine determines whether that intelligence is appropriate for publication. This separation provides an independent constitutional safeguard against unsafe platform behaviour.

Founder Principle
No engine should approve its own safety.

1.5 Constitutional Definition
For engineering purposes:
The Constitutional Safety, Clinical Governance & Risk Management Engine is the constitutional system responsible for identifying risk, enforcing safety constraints and governing whether constitutional intelligence may be delivered to users.
It evaluates safety.
It does not generate medical reasoning.

Constitutional Principle
Safety governance should remain independent from intelligence generation.

1.6 Constitutional Safety Objects
The primary constitutional construct governed by this engine shall be the Safety Object. Safety Objects represent structured constitutional assessments of user safety relevant to a specific intelligence outcome.
Illustrative examples include:
Cardiovascular Risk Safety Object, 
Medication Safety Object, 
Red Flag Biomarker Safety Object, 
Emergency Escalation Safety Object, 
Contraindication Safety Object, 
Severe Symptom Safety Object, 
Pregnancy Safety Object,

Each Safety Object shall possess:
Safety Identifier, 
Risk Classification, 
Severity, 
Urgency, 
Escalation Path, 
Publication Rules, 
Constitutional Constraints, 
Supporting Evidence, 
Review History, 
Audit Metadata. 

Founder Principle
Safety should exist as governed constitutional intelligence rather than hidden implementation logic.

1.7 Constitutional Guardrails
The Constitutional Safety Engine establishes platform-wide Constitutional Guardrails. Every intelligence outcome shall satisfy applicable guardrails before publication.
Illustrative guardrail categories include:
clinical safety, 
constitutional authority, 
evidence sufficiency, 
communication safety, 
escalation requirements, 
contraindication protection, 
uncertainty management. 
Guardrails apply consistently across all constitutional engines.

Engineering Rule
Guardrails govern every published intelligence outcome regardless of origin.

1.8 Explainability
Every Safety Object shall remain independently explainable.
BioSense shall always be capable of explaining:
why the Safety Object exists, 
which risks were identified, 
which constitutional rules were applied, 
whether escalation occurred, 
whether publication was restricted, 
which supporting evidence contributed, 
which guardrails were activated. 
Safety decisions that cannot be explained shall not satisfy constitutional requirements.

Constitutional Principle
Safety decisions should be as explainable as health intelligence.

1.9 Constitutional Boundaries
The Constitutional Safety Engine shall never:
diagnose disease, 
replace emergency services, 
prescribe medication, 
override Scientific Knowledge Objects, 
alter Health Beliefs, 
modify Decision Objects, 
generate medical advice beyond constitutional authority. 
Its responsibility is to govern safety, not to practise medicine.

Founder Principle
Protect users without exceeding constitutional authority.

1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Constitutional Safety Objects are established as first-class constitutional constructs.
✓ Safety ownership is clearly separated from intelligence ownership.
✓ Constitutional Guardrails are defined as platform-wide safety controls.
✓ The Safety Engine governs publication without generating medical reasoning.
✓ Safety explainability is constitutionally defined.
✓ Constitutional safety boundaries remain explicit.
✓ Every published intelligence outcome is subject to governed safety review.
Chapter 2
Safety Detection, Risk Identification & Constitutional Safety Pipeline

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Constitutional Safety, Clinical Governance & Risk Management Engine identifies situations requiring constitutional safety evaluation. Safety evaluation shall begin whenever observations, constitutional intelligence or platform behaviour indicate that a meaningful safety assessment may be required. The Safety Engine shall detect these situations through a governed Safety Detection Pipeline before applying constitutional guardrails or determining publication suitability. The objective is to ensure that every meaningful safety concern is identified consistently, explainably and proportionately.

Constitutional Principle
Safety evaluation should begin through governed detection rather than isolated rule checking.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Clinical safety rarely depends upon a single observation.
Meaningful risk often emerges from combinations of:
observations, 
Health Beliefs, 
Decision Objects, 
Prediction Objects, 
Scientific Knowledge, 
user-reported symptoms, 
contextual information. 
The Safety Engine shall therefore evaluate constitutional context rather than isolated values.

Founder Principle
Detect patterns of risk rather than isolated abnormalities.

2.3 Safety Detection Sources
Safety evaluation may be initiated by multiple constitutional sources.
Illustrative examples include:
Observation Sources
blood biomarkers, 
wearable data, 
physiological measurements, 
manual health entries, 
symptom questionnaires. 

Intelligence Sources
Health Beliefs, 
Confidence Objects, 
Decision Objects, 
Prediction Objects. 

Scientific Sources
Scientific Knowledge Objects, 
updated safety evidence, 
revised clinical guidance. 

User Behaviour
repeated ignored recommendations, 
rapidly deteriorating trends, 
conflicting user inputs. 

Platform Behaviour
low confidence, 
conflicting intelligence, 
constitutional inconsistencies, 
workflow anomalies. 
Each source shall remain independently governed.

Engineering Rule
Safety detection shall remain source-independent.

2.4 Constitutional Safety Pipeline
Every safety evaluation shall follow the same constitutional pipeline.
Potential Safety Trigger

↓

Trigger Validation

↓

Risk Identification

↓

Safety Classification

↓

Safety Object Generation

↓

Guardrail Evaluation

↓

Publication Decision

↓

Audit & Monitoring
No Safety Object shall bypass this pipeline.

Constitutional Principle
Every safety decision should follow one governed constitutional pathway.

2.5 Trigger Validation
Before safety assessment begins, every trigger shall undergo constitutional validation.
Validation shall confirm:
trigger authenticity, 
constitutional relevance, 
supporting evidence, 
affected constitutional objects, 
workflow integrity. 
Triggers failing validation shall not initiate Safety Objects.

Founder Principle
Validate the concern before evaluating the risk.

2.6 Risk Identification
Validated triggers shall undergo structured risk identification.
Illustrative categories include:
Clinical Risk
Potential physiological harm.

Behavioural Risk
Unsafe user behaviour.

Medication Risk
Potential contraindications or unsafe medication-related situations.

Prediction Risk
Forecast indicating meaningful deterioration.

Communication Risk
Risk that generated explanations could be misunderstood or exceed constitutional authority.

AI Risk
Low confidence, conflicting evidence or unsupported reasoning.
Multiple categories may exist simultaneously.

Engineering Rule
Risk categories shall remain explicit and independently explainable.

2.7 Safety Classification
Every identified risk shall be classified according to constitutional safety characteristics.
Illustrative dimensions include:
severity, 
urgency, 
reversibility, 
confidence, 
evidence strength, 
constitutional impact, 
required escalation. 
Classification supports downstream safety governance.
It does not itself determine publication.

Constitutional Principle
Classify safety before acting upon safety.

2.8 Safety Object Generation
Following classification, the Safety Engine shall generate one or more Constitutional Safety Objects.
Illustrative examples include:
High Cardiovascular Risk Safety Object.

Critical Biomarker Safety Object.

Medication Interaction Safety Object.

Low Confidence Safety Object.

Emergency Escalation Safety Object.
Each Safety Object shall preserve:
supporting evidence, 
constitutional context, 
applicable guardrails, 
escalation requirements, 
publication constraints. 

Founder Principle
Represent safety as governed constitutional intelligence rather than transient alerts.

2.9 Publication Readiness
Before publication, every Safety Object shall determine whether additional constitutional review is required.
Illustrative outcomes include:
Immediate publication permitted.

Publication with safety annotation.

Escalation required.

Additional evidence required.

Publication prohibited.
The Safety Engine governs publication readiness. It does not modify constitutional reasoning.

Engineering Rule
Safety determines publication eligibility rather than intelligence validity.

2.10 Explainability
Every Safety Object shall answer:
What triggered this safety evaluation? 
Which risks were identified? 
Which constitutional evidence supports this assessment? 
Which guardrails apply? 
Why was this publication outcome selected? 
Was escalation required? 

2.11 Engineering Constraints
The Safety Detection Framework shall:
remain trigger-driven, 
require trigger validation, 
support multiple risk categories, 
generate governed Safety Objects, 
separate classification from publication, 
preserve explainability, 
remain fully auditable. 

2.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every safety evaluation follows the constitutional Safety Pipeline.
✓ Trigger validation precedes risk identification.
✓ Risk categories remain explicit and independently governed.
✓ Safety Objects become the canonical representation of constitutional safety.
✓ Publication readiness remains distinct from intelligence generation.
✓ Every Safety Object is fully explainable.
✓ Every safety evaluation remains fully auditable.
Chapter 3
Constitutional Safety Objects & Risk Representation

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the canonical representation of safety within the BioSense Intelligence Platform. Safety shall exist as structured constitutional intelligence rather than transient warnings, alerts or implementation-specific logic. The Constitutional Safety Engine shall therefore represent every meaningful safety assessment through governed Constitutional Safety Objects. These objects become the authoritative representation of constitutional safety throughout the platform.

Constitutional Principle
Safety should exist as governed constitutional intelligence rather than temporary application behaviour.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
Clinical safety is multidimensional.
A meaningful safety assessment may involve:
physiological risk, 
prediction uncertainty, 
communication safety, 
constitutional authority, 
intervention appropriateness, 
scientific confidence, 
escalation requirements. 
Rather than embedding these concerns across multiple engines, the platform shall centralise them within governed Safety Objects. Safety Objects become the constitutional source of truth for platform safety.

Founder Principle
Represent safety once and govern it everywhere.

3.3 Safety Domains
Every Safety Object shall belong to one or more Constitutional Safety Domains.
Illustrative domains include:
Clinical Safety
Risk arising from physiological observations or Health Beliefs.

Emergency Safety
Situations requiring immediate escalation.

Medication Safety
Potential medication-related risk or contraindications.

Biomarker Safety
Safety associated with laboratory findings.

Prediction Safety
Risks arising from projected future health trajectories.

AI Safety
Low confidence, conflicting reasoning or unsupported conclusions.

Communication Safety
Risk that explanations may exceed constitutional authority or be misunderstood.

Behavioural Safety
Unsafe user behaviours or repeated disregard of significant recommendations. Each Safety Domain shall remain independently governed.

Engineering Rule
Safety Domains shall remain mutually distinguishable with clearly defined ownership.

3.4 Constitutional Safety Objects
Each Safety Domain shall contain one or more Constitutional Safety Objects.
Illustrative examples include:
High Cardiovascular Risk Safety Object.

Critical Blood Pressure Safety Object.

Rapid HRV Decline Safety Object.

Medication Contraindication Safety Object.

Emergency Symptom Safety Object.

Low Confidence Recommendation Safety Object.

Scientific Uncertainty Safety Object.

Pregnancy Contraindication Safety Object.
Every safety concern shall belong to at least one governed Safety Object.

Constitutional Principle
Every constitutional safety concern shall possess a governed Safety Object.

3.5 Object Structure
Every Constitutional Safety Object shall contain:
Safety Identifier, 
Safety Domain, 
Risk Description, 
Severity, 
Urgency, 
Confidence, 
Supporting Constitutional Evidence, 
Supporting Scientific Knowledge, 
Publication Rules, 
Escalation Path, 
Constitutional Constraints, 
Lifecycle Status, 
Version, 
Audit History. 
The constitutional structure shall remain consistent regardless of safety category.

Founder Principle
Every Safety Object should answer what the risk is, why it matters and what constitutional action is required.

3.6 Relationships Between Safety Objects
Safety Objects may contribute to one another.
Illustrative examples include:

Implicit safety dependencies shall be prohibited.

Engineering Rule
Safety relationships shall remain transparent and independently explainable.

3.7 Safety Lifecycle
Every Safety Object shall progress through a governed lifecycle.
Illustrative stages include:
Risk Identified
↓
Risk Validated
↓
Safety Object Generated
↓
Safety Review
↓
Publication Decision
↓

↓

Historical Safety Objects shall remain permanently reproducible. Safety evolution shall occur through lifecycle progression rather than silent modification.

Constitutional Principle
Safety should evolve through governed lifecycle transitions rather than hidden updates.

3.8 Consumer Engines
Safety Objects may influence every constitutional engine.
Illustrative examples include:

Determines whether Decision Objects require escalation.

ENG-005
Determines how explanations should be communicated safely.

ENG-007
Determines whether predictions require additional safety annotations.

ENG-009
Determines whether workflows may proceed to publication.
Ownership remains exclusively with ENG-010.
Consumer engines remain read-only.

Engineering Rule
Safety Objects remain immutable for all consumer engines.

3.9 Safety State
Every Safety Object shall possess a governed Safety State.
Illustrative states include:
Informational.

Monitor.

Action Recommended.

Priority Action Required.

Immediate Escalation Required.

Publication Restricted.
Safety State reflects constitutional response rather than medical diagnosis.

Constitutional Principle
Safety State governs platform behaviour rather than clinical diagnosis.

3.10 Explainability
Every Safety Object shall answer:
What safety concern exists? 
Why was this Safety Object generated? 
Which constitutional evidence supports it? 
Which Scientific Knowledge Objects contributed? 
Which publication rules apply? 
Which escalation pathway has been selected? 
What constitutional constraints are active? 

3.11 Engineering Constraints
The Constitutional Safety Model shall:
remain domain-based, 
preserve constitutional ownership, 
support explicit safety relationships, 
preserve lifecycle history, 
support governed Safety States, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable. 

3.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Safety Objects become the canonical representation of constitutional safety.
✓ Every safety concern belongs to one or more governed Safety Domains.
✓ Safety relationships remain explicit.
✓ Safety lifecycle stages are constitutionally defined.
✓ Safety States are explicitly represented.
✓ Consumer engine ownership remains unambiguous.
✓ Every Safety Object remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 4
Safety Response, Escalation & Constitutional Intervention Governance

4.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Constitutional Safety, Clinical Governance & Risk Management Engine determines and governs the platform's response following the identification of a Constitutional Safety Object.
Identifying risk alone is insufficient.
The Safety Engine shall determine the appropriate constitutional response based upon the nature of the identified risk, the supporting evidence, the confidence of the underlying intelligence and the constitutional authority of the BioSense platform. The objective is to ensure that every identified safety concern receives a proportionate, explainable and constitutionally appropriate response.

Constitutional Principle
Every identified safety concern should result in a governed constitutional response.

4.2 Engineering Philosophy
Not all risks require the same response.
Some risks require:
monitoring, 
others require:
timely action, 
others require:
immediate escalation, 
while others require:
publication restriction. 
The Constitutional Safety Engine shall therefore determine responses through governed constitutional rules rather than fixed severity thresholds alone.

Founder Principle
Respond proportionately rather than uniformly.

4.3 Constitutional Response Categories
Every Safety Object shall produce one governed Constitutional Response.
Illustrative response categories include:
Inform
No immediate action required.
Provide contextual education.

Monitor
Continue observing.
Increase monitoring frequency if appropriate.

Recommend Action
Advise the user to undertake an appropriate preventive action within constitutional authority.

Recommend Professional Review
Advise discussion with an appropriate healthcare professional.

Urgent Clinical Review
Advise prompt medical assessment due to elevated constitutional concern.

Emergency Escalation
Advise immediate contact with emergency medical services or attendance at an emergency department where constitutionally appropriate.

Publication Restriction
Prevent publication of intelligence until constitutional safety requirements are satisfied. Each response category shall remain independently governed.

Engineering Rule
Every Safety Object shall produce one explicit Constitutional Response.

4.4 Escalation Pathways
Where escalation is required, the Safety Engine shall determine the appropriate escalation pathway.
Illustrative pathways include:
Routine follow-up.
↓
Primary healthcare professional.
↓
Relevant medical specialist.
↓
Urgent assessment.
↓

8.6a Clinical Adverse Event Governance
This section intentionally defines only the constitutional engineering workflow. Clinical definitions, adverse-event classification, causality assessment and regulatory reporting obligations remain governed by the Medical Advisory Function under Constitution 1B/1C and are deliberately outside the scope of the Engineering Library.
A Clinical Adverse Event is distinct from a Safety Incident (§8.6): §8.6 governs platform and AI malfunctions (inappropriate publication, missed escalation, guardrail failure), whereas Clinical Adverse Event Governance handles clinical or physiological adverse outcomes associated with a BioSense intervention, as raised by ENG-004 §8.4. The two are different event classes with different owners and shall not be merged.
ENG-010 owns the workflow — what triggers it, how it is governed, who owns review, how findings feed back, and its auditability, versioning and prospective-only learning. The Medical Advisory Function owns the clinical content and every clinical decision within it.
Trigger. An Adverse Outcome (ENG-004 §8.4) deterministically triggers this workflow. The trigger is deterministic and non-discretionary: it is not subject to model judgement (consistent with the Deterministic Safety Rule Engine, §7.x).

Workflow:
Adverse Outcome (ENG-004 §8.4) → Deterministic Trigger → Clinical Adverse Event Record (governed, immutable, content per Medical Advisory Function) → Clinical Governance Review → Medical Advisory Assessment → Safety Rule Review (ENG-010 Deterministic Safety Rule Engine) → Scientific Library Review (ENG-008) → Future Release Candidate → Governed Deployment

No direct path to production. Nothing in this workflow feeds directly into live platform behaviour. Every outcome passes through governance. This mirrors the platform-wide rule that safety, science and models evolve only through governed change (§7.6, §8.5, ENG-009 §6).

Prospective-only; historical intelligence is never altered. This process never modifies existing historical intelligence. It affects only future Safety Rule versions, future Scientific Library versions, and future model releases. Historical Health Beliefs, Decision Objects, published communications and Memory Objects remain exactly as originally produced, preserving historical reproducibility (ENG-009 §5.3a; ENG-011 §3).

Interaction with CAPA (§8.7). Where a Medical Advisory Assessment identifies a needed platform change, it is enacted through the existing Corrective & Preventive Actions machinery (§8.7): documented, version-controlled, independently validated, auditable, and applied prospectively. Clinical Adverse Event Governance supplies the clinical finding; §8.7 supplies the governed mechanism for acting on it. The two do not duplicate each other.

Auditability & versioning. Every Clinical Adverse Event Record, review stage, assessment and resulting release candidate is a governed, permanently-retained constitutional record. Records retain no content beyond what the Medical Advisory Function authorises, and are subject to the same erasure and audit boundaries as other health data (1C; ENG-002 §11.11a) where they reference an identifiable user.


Engineering Rule

An Adverse Outcome deterministically enters this workflow, which feeds only future Safety Rule, Scientific Library and model versions through governance, and never alters historical intelligence or reaches production directly.
