Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define how the BioSense Intelligence Platform forecasts future health trajectories using trusted constitutional intelligence. Unlike previous engines, which establish the current understanding of an individual's health, the Predictive Intelligence & Longitudinal Forecasting Engine projects how that understanding may evolve over time. The engine shall identify plausible future health trajectories, emerging opportunities and potential risks while preserving scientific integrity, explainability and constitutional transparency. Its responsibility is to support proactive decision-making rather than retrospective analysis.

Constitutional Principle
Forecast possible futures without presenting them as certainty.

1.2 Scope
This specification governs:
longitudinal forecasting, 
Health Trajectories, 
Prediction Objects, 
forecast confidence, 
Opportunity Windows, 
future health scenarios, 
intervention impact forecasting, 
Biological Age forecasting, 
risk trajectory forecasting, 
goal achievement forecasting, 
counterfactual modelling, 
predictive explainability. 
It deliberately excludes:
Health Belief formation (ENG-001), 
observation processing (ENG-002), 
confidence calculation (ENG-003), 
Decision Object generation (ENG-004), 
communication delivery (ENG-005), 
personalisation learning (ENG-006). 
The Predictive Intelligence Engine consumes constitutional intelligence. It does not replace or reinterpret it.

1.3 Authority
The Predictive Intelligence Engine consumes outputs from upstream constitutional engines.
It may consume:
Health Beliefs, 
Canonical Observations, 
Confidence Objects, 
Decision Objects, 
Behavioural Identity, 
Biological Identity, 
Personalisation Objects, 
intervention history, 
longitudinal trends, 
historical outcomes. 
It shall not modify upstream intelligence. Instead, it produces governed Prediction Objects that may be consumed by other constitutional engines.

Engineering Rule
The Predictive Intelligence Engine is a read-only consumer of constitutional intelligence and a producer of predictive intelligence.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
Prediction within BioSense is not an attempt to determine the future. It is an evidence-based estimation of plausible future trajectories under explicitly stated assumptions.
Forecasts shall always remain:
evidence-driven, 
confidence-aware, 
explainable, 
transparent, 
reproducible, 
constitutionally governed. 
The engine shall communicate uncertainty as prominently as prediction.

Founder Principle
A trustworthy forecast explains both what may happen and why it may not.

1.5 Constitutional Definition
For engineering purposes:
The Predictive Intelligence & Longitudinal Forecasting Engine is the constitutional system responsible for generating governed, explainable and confidence-aware forecasts of an individual's future health trajectories using trusted BioSense intelligence.
Prediction Objects represent plausible future states. They do not represent guaranteed outcomes.

Constitutional Principle
Every forecast is conditional upon the evidence and assumptions from which it was generated.

1.6 Prediction Is Not Decision-Making
A strict architectural boundary exists between forecasting and decision-making.
Decision-making occurs within:
ENG-004 
Prediction occurs within:
ENG-007 
The Predictive Intelligence Engine shall never:
generate Decision Objects, 
reprioritise interventions, 
reinterpret Health Beliefs, 
alter Confidence Objects, 
create recommendations independently. 
Its responsibility is to forecast likely futures. Decision Intelligence determines how BioSense should respond.

Engineering Rule
Forecasts inform decisions.
They do not become decisions.

1.7 Prediction Objects
The primary output of this engine shall be governed Prediction Objects.
Illustrative examples include:
Health Trajectory Object 
Risk Trajectory Object 
Biological Age Projection Object 
Goal Achievement Forecast Object 
Opportunity Window Object 
Intervention Impact Forecast Object 
Scenario Comparison Object 
Forecast Confidence Object 
Each Prediction Object shall possess:
provenance, 
assumptions, 
confidence, 
supporting evidence, 
version, 
validity period, 
explainability, 
audit history. 

Founder Principle
Predictions should exist as structured constitutional intelligence rather than generated narrative.

1.8 Explainability
Every Prediction Object shall remain independently explainable.
BioSense shall always be capable of explaining:
what is being forecast, 
why the forecast exists, 
which evidence supports it, 
which assumptions were applied, 
how confidence was determined, 
which engines consume the forecast, 
how the forecast may change if new evidence becomes available. 
Forecasts that cannot be explained are constitutionally incomplete.

Constitutional Principle
Every prediction should be as explainable as every observation.

1.9 Forecast Boundaries
The Predictive Intelligence Engine exists to support proactive health management.
It shall never:
guarantee outcomes, 
diagnose future disease, 
predict exact dates of future events, 
present speculative conclusions as established fact, 
conceal uncertainty, 
overstate precision beyond available evidence. 
Forecasting shall remain proportionate to the quality of the supporting intelligence.

Founder Principle
Precision should never exceed evidence.

1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ The Predictive Intelligence Engine is clearly separated from all reasoning and decision-making engines.
✓ Prediction Objects are established as first-class constitutional objects.
✓ Forecasts are explicitly defined as conditional rather than deterministic.
✓ Assumptions are recognised as constitutional components of every forecast.
✓ Forecast confidence remains distinct from health confidence.
✓ Forecast boundaries prohibit unsupported certainty.
✓ Every Prediction Object remains explainable, versioned and auditable.
Chapter 2
Predictive Intelligence Architecture

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the constitutional architecture responsible for generating future health forecasts within the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
Forecasting is a governed process.
The Predictive Intelligence Engine shall not extrapolate future outcomes directly from raw observations. Instead, it shall consume trusted constitutional intelligence from upstream engines, combine this with explicit forecasting assumptions and produce structured Prediction Objects that describe plausible future health trajectories. The objective is to provide preventive intelligence while preserving scientific integrity, explainability and constitutional transparency.

Constitutional Principle
Forecasts should emerge from trusted intelligence rather than direct extrapolation of raw data.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Forecasting is the final stage of constitutional reasoning. The engine shall not independently analyse physiological observations or generate new Health Beliefs.
Instead, it builds upon:
trusted understanding, 
validated evidence, 
quantified confidence, 
governed decisions, 
longitudinal behavioural context. 
Every forecast therefore inherits the constitutional integrity of the intelligence upon which it is based.

Founder Principle
Forecast from understanding, not from data alone.

2.3 Forecast Inputs
The Predictive Intelligence Engine may consume:
From ENG-001
Health Beliefs 
Biological Identity 
Behavioural Identity 
Dynamic Health Model 

From ENG-002
Canonical Observations 
Longitudinal trends 
Observation quality 
Observation completeness 

From ENG-003

Remaining uncertainty 

From ENG-004
Decision Objects 
Expected Health Impact 
Intervention history 

From ENG-006
Behavioural Adherence Objects 
Routine Profile Objects 
Personalisation Objects 
The engine consumes intelligence.
It does not reinterpret ownership.

Engineering Rule
Every consumed object remains read-only and version-pinned throughout forecast generation.

2.4 Forecasting Pipeline
Every Prediction Object shall follow the same constitutional forecasting pipeline.
Constitutional Intelligence

↓

Forecast Context Assembly

↓

↓

Trajectory Modelling

↓

Scenario Evaluation

↓

Forecast Confidence

↓

Prediction Object

↓

Consumed by Other Engines
No Prediction Object shall bypass this pipeline.

Constitutional Principle
Every forecast should follow one explainable constitutional pathway.

2.5 Forecast Context Assembly
Before forecasting begins, the engine shall assemble a Forecast Context. The Forecast Context shall contain only the intelligence required to evaluate the requested forecast.
Illustrative components include:
relevant Health Beliefs, 
supporting observations, 
confidence assessments, 
historical trends, 
intervention history, 
behavioural adherence, 
active goals. 
Irrelevant intelligence shall not be included.

Founder Principle
Forecast with sufficient context, not maximum context.

2.6 Forecast Assumptions
Every forecast shall explicitly define the assumptions upon which it depends.
Examples include:
current behaviour remains unchanged, 
interventions continue as currently observed, 
observation quality remains stable, 
no major external health events occur, 
physiological relationships remain consistent with current evidence. 
Assumptions shall be version-controlled and attached to every Prediction Object. Forecasts without explicit assumptions shall be constitutionally incomplete.

Constitutional Principle
Every forecast should declare the assumptions that make it possible.

2.7 Trajectory Modelling
Trajectory modelling estimates how relevant Health Beliefs may evolve under the declared assumptions.
Examples include:
Recovery trajectory.
Biological Age trajectory.
Cardiometabolic trajectory.
Fitness trajectory.
Sleep trajectory.
Nutritional trajectory.
Trajectory modelling shall preserve uncertainty and confidence throughout the forecasting process. The engine shall estimate ranges of plausible evolution rather than deterministic future states.

Engineering Rule
Trajectory models estimate plausible futures rather than exact outcomes.

2.8 Scenario Evaluation
Multiple scenarios may exist for the same individual.
Examples include:
Current behaviour continues.
↓
Sleep improves.
↓
Exercise increases.
↓
Alcohol intake decreases.
↓
Multiple interventions combined.
Each scenario shall be independently modelled. Scenario comparisons shall remain transparent and reproducible.

Founder Principle
Compare plausible futures rather than declaring a single inevitable future.

2.9 Forecast Production
Trajectory modelling and scenario evaluation produce one or more Prediction Objects.
Each Prediction Object shall contain:
forecast description, 
predicted trajectory, 
forecast assumptions, 
supporting Health Beliefs, 
supporting observations, 
confidence, 
uncertainty, 
validity period, 
version, 
explainability metadata. 
Prediction Objects shall remain immutable once published.

Constitutional Principle
Forecasts become governed intelligence once generated.

2.10 Explainability
Every forecast shall answer:
What future state is being forecast? 
Which intelligence contributed? 
Which assumptions were applied? 
Which scenarios were evaluated? 
Why was this trajectory selected? 
How certain is BioSense? 
What new evidence could materially change this forecast? 

2.11 Engineering Constraints
The Predictive Intelligence Architecture shall:
consume constitutional intelligence only, 
preserve ownership boundaries, 
require explicit assumptions, 
support multiple scenarios, 
preserve uncertainty, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable. 

2.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every Prediction Object follows the constitutional forecasting pipeline.
✓ Forecast Context contains only relevant intelligence.
✓ Every forecast declares explicit assumptions.
✓ Scenario evaluation is independently governed.
✓ Prediction Objects remain immutable after publication.
✓ Ownership boundaries remain unambiguous.
✓ Every forecast remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 3
Prediction Model & Canonical Prediction Objects

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the canonical Prediction Model used by BioSense to represent future-oriented health intelligence. Prediction Objects provide a governed, explainable and reproducible representation of plausible future health trajectories. They serve as the constitutional source of predictive intelligence for the BioSense platform.
Prediction Objects describe what may occur under explicitly stated assumptions. They do not represent guaranteed outcomes.

Constitutional Principle
Prediction Objects represent possible futures rather than promised futures.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
Forecasts should exist as structured constitutional intelligence rather than generated narrative.
Every prediction shall therefore exist as a governed Prediction Object possessing:
provenance, 
assumptions, 
confidence, 
supporting evidence, 
validity period, 
version, 
explainability, 
auditability. 
The Prediction Model becomes the canonical representation of all future-oriented intelligence within BioSense.

Founder Principle
Future intelligence deserves the same engineering discipline as present intelligence.

3.3 Canonical Prediction Domains
Prediction Objects shall belong to one or more Prediction Domains.
Illustrative domains include:
Health Trajectory
Expected evolution of current health state.

Risk Trajectory
Projected change in future health risk.

Opportunity Window
Periods where intervention is expected to provide disproportionate benefit.

Biological Age Projection
Expected Biological Age evolution under defined scenarios.

Goal Achievement
Likelihood of achieving active health goals.

Intervention Impact
Expected change following implementation of a Decision Object.

Behavioural Forecast
Expected behavioural adherence under current personalisation.

Comparative Scenario
Comparison of multiple plausible futures.
Each Prediction Domain shall remain independently governed.

Engineering Rule
Prediction Domains shall remain mutually distinguishable with clearly defined ownership.

3.4 Prediction Objects
Each Prediction Domain shall contain one or more Prediction Objects.
Illustrative examples include:
Health Trajectory Object
Expected direction of overall health.

Recovery Forecast Object
Projected recovery evolution.

Biological Age Projection Object
Expected Biological Age range.

Cardiometabolic Forecast Object
Projected cardiometabolic trajectory.

Intervention Impact Forecast Object
Estimated physiological effect of implementing a recommendation.

Opportunity Window Object
Periods where intervention timing is expected to maximise benefit.

Goal Achievement Forecast Object
Estimated likelihood of achieving a user-defined goal.

Comparative Scenario Object
Structured comparison of multiple future trajectories.

Constitutional Principle
Every forecast shall belong to one clearly defined Prediction Object.

3.5 Object Structure
Every Prediction Object shall contain:
Object Identifier, 
Prediction Domain, 
Forecast Description, 
Forecast Horizon, 
Forecast Assumptions, 
Supporting Intelligence, 
Confidence, 
Uncertainty Summary, 
Validity Period, 
Version, 


Derived-Metric Method Version (where applicable),
Explainability Metadata, 
Consumer Engines. 
The constitutional structure shall remain consistent regardless of prediction type.

Founder Principle
Every Prediction Object should answer what, why, how likely and under which assumptions. Where a Prediction Object forecasts a derived metric (Biological Age Projection, or a Health Score projection under Goal Achievement), its Derived-Metric Method Version shall pin the BIOLOGICAL_AGE_METHOD_VERSION or HEALTH_SCORE_METHOD_VERSION (ENG-001 §7.8) and the Scientific Knowledge versions (ENG-008) it forecasts against. A derived-metric projection is reproducible only against the method version of the present-value computation it extends; forecasting a derived metric without pinning its method version is prohibited.

Engineering Rule
Any projection of a derived metric (Biological Age, Health Score) pins the derived-metric method version it extends. This is guarantee (a)/(b) reproducibility per ENG-009 §5.3a applied to derived-metric forecasts.

3.6 Relationships Between Prediction Objects
Prediction Objects may contribute to one another.
Illustrative examples include:

↓
Risk Trajectory
↓
Opportunity Window

↓

Behavioural Forecast
↓
Scenario Comparison
Relationships shall remain explicit.
Implicit predictive dependencies shall be prohibited.

Engineering Rule
Relationships between Prediction Objects shall remain transparent and independently explainable.

3.7 Prediction Lifecycle
Every Prediction Object shall progress through a governed lifecycle.
Illustrative stages include:
Forecast Requested
↓
Forecast Generated
↓
Validated
↓
Published
↓
Consumed
↓

↓

Forecasts shall never silently change after publication. Generation of a new forecast creates a new Prediction Object version.
Historical forecasts remain permanently reproducible.

Constitutional Principle
Predictions evolve through replacement rather than silent modification.

3.8 Consumer Engines
Prediction Objects may be consumed by multiple constitutional engines.
Illustrative examples include:
ENG-004
Decision Intelligence Engine
Consumes:
Opportunity Window Objects.
Intervention Impact Forecasts.
Behavioural Forecasts.
Purpose:
Support intervention prioritisation.

ENG-005
Conversation Engine
Consumes:
Prediction Objects.
Scenario Comparisons.
Biological Age Projections.
Purpose:
Explain future trajectories.

Future constitutional engines may consume Prediction Objects through governed interfaces.
Ownership remains exclusively with ENG-007.

Engineering Rule
Prediction Objects remain read-only for all consumer engines.

3.9 Forecast Horizons
Every Prediction Object shall declare its intended forecast horizon.
Illustrative examples include:
Immediate
Hours to days.

Short-term
Days to weeks.

Medium-term
Weeks to months.

Long-term
Months to years.
Forecast confidence shall be expected to vary across different horizons. Longer forecast horizons shall generally communicate greater uncertainty unless supported by exceptional longitudinal evidence.

Constitutional Principle
Forecast horizon and forecast confidence should evolve together.

3.10 Explainability
Every Prediction Object shall answer:
What future state is being forecast? 
Which assumptions support the forecast? 
Which constitutional intelligence contributed? 
Which Prediction Domain does it belong to? 
What is the forecast horizon? 
Which engines consume this Prediction Object? 
What future evidence could materially change this prediction? 

3.11 Engineering Constraints
The Prediction Model shall:
remain domain-based, 
preserve ownership boundaries, 
support multiple forecast horizons, 
maintain explicit relationships, 
preserve explainability, 
preserve historical reproducibility, 
remain fully auditable. 

3.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ The Prediction Model is established as the canonical representation of predictive intelligence.
✓ Every forecast belongs to a governed Prediction Domain.
✓ Prediction Objects possess structured constitutional metadata.
✓ Forecast horizons are explicitly represented.
✓ Relationships between Prediction Objects remain transparent.
✓ Consumer engine ownership remains unambiguous.
✓ Every Prediction Object remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 4
Health Trajectories & Longitudinal Trend Forecasting

4.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Predictive Intelligence & Longitudinal Forecasting Engine models the expected evolution of an individual's health over time. Rather than viewing observations as isolated measurements, the engine shall identify longitudinal trajectories that describe the likely direction, pace and stability of health change. Health Trajectories provide the constitutional foundation for preventive intelligence by enabling BioSense to recognise where an individual is heading before meaningful deterioration or improvement becomes clinically obvious.

Constitutional Principle
Health should be understood as a trajectory rather than a collection of isolated measurements.

4.2 Engineering Philosophy
Human health is dynamic.
Individual observations rarely provide sufficient information to understand long-term direction.
Meaningful forecasting therefore requires the integration of:
historical observations, 
current Health Beliefs, 
evidence quality, 
confidence, 
intervention history, 
behavioural context, 
longitudinal consistency. 
Health Trajectories represent the evolution of constitutional intelligence rather than simple statistical trend lines.

Founder Principle
The direction of health is often more valuable than its current position.

4.3 Trajectory Categories
The Predictive Intelligence Engine may model multiple trajectory categories.
Illustrative examples include:
Recovery Trajectory
Expected evolution of physiological recovery.

Cardiometabolic Trajectory
Expected progression of cardiometabolic health.

Fitness Trajectory
Expected evolution of physical capacity.

Sleep Trajectory
Expected changes in sleep quality and recovery.

Nutritional Trajectory
Expected development of nutritional health.

Biological Age Trajectory
Projected evolution of Biological Age.

Composite Health Trajectory
Overall direction of long-term health optimisation. Each trajectory remains independently explainable and version-controlled.

Engineering Rule
Trajectory categories shall remain constitutionally independent while supporting higher-level synthesis.

4.4 Trajectory Construction
Every Health Trajectory shall be constructed from constitutional intelligence rather than raw observations alone.
Illustrative inputs include:
validated Health Beliefs, 
Canonical Observations, 
Confidence Objects, 
longitudinal trends, 
intervention history, 
behavioural adherence, 
Personalisation Objects, 
previously generated Prediction Objects where constitutionally appropriate. 
Trajectory construction shall preserve uncertainty throughout the forecasting process.

Constitutional Principle
Forecast from trusted understanding rather than isolated measurements.

4.5 Trajectory Characteristics
Every Health Trajectory shall describe characteristics including:
Direction.
Rate of change.
Stability.
