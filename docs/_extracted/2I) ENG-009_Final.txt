Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the constitutional architecture responsible for coordinating, orchestrating and governing the execution of every intelligence workflow within the BioSense Intelligence Platform. The BioSense platform comprises multiple specialised constitutional engines, each responsible for a distinct area of intelligence. The Constitutional Orchestration & Intelligence Workflow Engine provides the coordination layer that ensures these engines operate together as one coherent, explainable and auditable system.
It determines:
when workflows begin, 
which engines participate, 
execution order, 
dependency resolution, 
workflow completion, 
recovery from failure, 
historical replay. 
The objective is to ensure that every intelligence process follows a governed constitutional workflow from initiation to completion.

Constitutional Principle
Intelligence should emerge through governed orchestration rather than independent engine execution.

1.2 Scope
This specification governs:
Constitutional Workflows, 
workflow orchestration, 
engine coordination, 
execution sequencing, 
dependency resolution, 
workflow state, 
event handling, 
execution lifecycle, 
workflow replay, 
failure recovery, 
orchestration versioning, 
workflow governance, 
execution auditability. 
It deliberately excludes:
Health Beliefs (ENG-001), 
observations (ENG-002), 
confidence evaluation (ENG-003), 
decision generation (ENG-004), 
communication (ENG-005), 
personalisation (ENG-006), 
prediction (ENG-007), 
scientific knowledge (ENG-008). 
This engine owns orchestration.
Other engines own intelligence.

1.3 Authority
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine governs execution across every constitutional engine.
Illustrative participants include:
ENG-001 
ENG-002 
ENG-003 
ENG-004 
ENG-005 
ENG-006 
ENG-007 
ENG-008 
The Orchestration Engine never assumes ownership of intelligence produced by these engines. It coordinates their execution while preserving clear constitutional ownership boundaries.

Engineering Rule
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine coordinates intelligence without producing intelligence.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
Each constitutional engine has one clearly defined responsibility. However, meaningful preventive intelligence requires those engines to work together through repeatable, governed workflows.
The orchestration layer therefore separates:
execution from reasoning, 
coordination from ownership, 
workflow from intelligence. 
This separation enables BioSense to evolve individual engines independently while maintaining consistent platform behaviour.

Founder Principle
Coordinate specialised intelligence rather than centralising it.

1.5 Constitutional Definition
For engineering purposes:
The Constitutional Orchestration & Intelligence Workflow Engine is the constitutional system responsible for coordinating the execution, sequencing, dependency management and lifecycle of every intelligence workflow within the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
It governs how intelligence is produced. It does not produce the intelligence itself.

Constitutional Principle
Workflow ownership should remain independent from intelligence ownership.

1.6 Constitutional Workflows
The primary construct governed by this engine shall be the Constitutional Workflow. A Constitutional Workflow represents a complete, governed sequence of coordinated engine activities initiated by a specific event.
Illustrative examples include:
User Erasure Workflow
Blood Test Upload Workflow,

Prediction Refresh Workflow,

Each Constitutional Workflow shall possess:
Workflow Identifier, 
Trigger, 
Participating Engines, 
Dependency Graph, 
Execution State, 
Workflow Version, 
Audit History, 
Completion Status, 
Failure Recovery Metadata, 
Replay Metadata. 

Founder Principle
Workflows should exist as governed constitutional objects rather than hidden implementation details.

1.7 Orchestration Responsibilities
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine is responsible for:
initiating workflows, 
coordinating engine execution, 
resolving dependencies, 
maintaining execution order, 
preventing duplicate execution, 
preserving workflow state, 
coordinating retries, 
ensuring workflow completion, 
recording execution history. 
It shall never:
modify Health Beliefs, 
alter Scientific Knowledge Objects, 
calculate Confidence Objects, 
generate Decision Objects, 
produce Prediction Objects, 
communicate directly with users. 

Engineering Rule
Orchestration coordinates execution without changing constitutional intelligence.

1.8 Explainability
Every Constitutional Workflow shall remain independently explainable.
BioSense shall always be capable of explaining:
why the workflow started, 
which event triggered execution, 
which engines participated, 
execution order, 
dependencies, 
completion status, 
failures encountered, 
recovery actions performed, 
final workflow outcome. 
A workflow that cannot explain its execution shall not satisfy constitutional standards.

Constitutional Principle
Execution should be as explainable as intelligence.

1.9 Constitutional Boundaries
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall never:
bypass constitutional engines, 
reorder workflows without governance, 
execute unsupported dependencies, 
modify intelligence ownership, 
conceal execution failures, 
silently retry indefinitely, 
bypass audit requirements. 
Every workflow shall remain fully governed from initiation through completion.

Founder Principle
Reliable orchestration depends upon disciplined execution rather than hidden automation.

1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Constitutional Workflows are established as first-class constitutional constructs.
✓ Workflow ownership is clearly separated from intelligence ownership.
✓ The Orchestration Engine coordinates all constitutional engines without assuming their responsibilities.
✓ Workflow execution remains governed, versioned and auditable.
✓ Workflow explainability is constitutionally defined.
✓ Execution boundaries remain explicit.
✓ Every Constitutional Workflow satisfies the principles of transparency, reproducibility and auditability.
Chapter 2
Event Architecture & Workflow Initiation

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how Constitutional Workflows are initiated within the BioSense Intelligence Platform through governed constitutional events. Every intelligence workflow shall begin in response to a recognised event. Events represent changes within the platform that require constitutional processing.
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall evaluate these events, determine whether workflow execution is required and initiate the appropriate Constitutional Workflow. The objective is to ensure that every intelligence process begins through explicit, governed and reproducible execution triggers.

Constitutional Principle
Every workflow should begin because something meaningful has changed.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
The BioSense platform is fundamentally event-driven. Rather than continuously reprocessing intelligence, the platform responds only when constitutional events justify further reasoning.
This architecture provides:
efficient execution, 
deterministic behaviour, 
improved scalability, 
reduced computational overhead, 
complete auditability. 
Every workflow therefore begins with an explicitly identifiable constitutional event.

Founder Principle
Execute intelligence because change occurred, not because time passed unnecessarily.

2.3 Constitutional Events
A Constitutional Event represents a governed occurrence capable of initiating one or more Constitutional Workflows.
Illustrative event categories include:
Observation Events
wearable synchronisation, 
blood test upload, 
questionnaire completion, 
manual health entry. 

User Events
profile update, 
goal modification, 
preference change, 
account creation. 

Scientific Events
Scientific Knowledge Object revision, 
guideline update, 
governance approval. 

System Events
scheduled health refresh, 
workflow replay, 
integrity validation, 
platform maintenance. 

External Events
connected device update, 
third-party laboratory result, 
healthcare integration, 
approved API notification. 
Each event shall possess a governed constitutional definition.

Engineering Rule
Events shall remain categorised, versioned and independently explainable.

2.4 Event Detection
The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall detect constitutional events through governed interfaces.
Illustrative event sources include:
Observation Engine, 
Personalisation Engine, 
Scientific Knowledge Engine, 
user interfaces, 
external integrations, 
scheduled platform services, 
governance systems. 
Detection confirms that an event has occurred. It does not automatically begin workflow execution.

Constitutional Principle
Event detection precedes workflow initiation.

2.5 Event Evaluation
Every detected event shall undergo constitutional evaluation.
Evaluation determines:
event validity, 
event authenticity, 
workflow eligibility, 
affected constitutional objects, 
required participating engines, 
execution priority. 
Events failing constitutional validation shall not initiate workflows. Evaluation outcomes shall be permanently recorded.

Founder Principle
Understand the event before responding to it.

2.6 Workflow Selection
Following evaluation, the Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall identify the appropriate Constitutional Workflow.
Illustrative mappings include:

↓
Blood Test Processing Workflow.

↓

↓

Each event shall map to one or more governed workflows.
Mappings shall remain version-controlled.

Engineering Rule
Workflow selection shall be deterministic and reproducible.

2.7 Workflow Prioritisation
Where multiple workflows become eligible simultaneously, the Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall determine execution priority.
Illustrative priority considerations include:
dependency requirements, 
clinical significance, 
user-facing impact, 
workflow urgency, 
execution efficiency, 
governance policy. 
Priority determines execution order.
It shall not alter workflow behaviour.

Constitutional Principle
Execution priority should optimise orchestration without changing intelligence.

2.8 Event Deduplication
Multiple identical events may occur within short periods. The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall detect duplicate events and determine whether additional workflow execution is necessary.
Illustrative examples include:
Repeated wearable synchronisations.
↓
Single observation refresh.

Repeated profile updates before execution begins.
↓
Single personalisation workflow.
Deduplication shall preserve correctness while avoiding unnecessary execution.

Founder Principle
Repeated events should not produce repeated intelligence unnecessarily.

2.9 Event Metadata
Every Constitutional Event shall preserve:
Event Identifier, 
Event Type, 
Source, 
Detection Time, 
Validation Status, 
Associated Workflow, 
Execution Priority, 
Dependency Information, 
Audit Metadata. 
Event metadata shall remain immutable following publication. The Event Identifier shall serve as the Constitutional Correlation Identifier. It shall be propagated onto every constitutional object produced anywhere downstream of the event — Health Beliefs, Confidence Objects, Decision Objects, Prediction Objects, Safety Objects, Structured Explanations, Memory Objects, and any workflow record — so that all intelligence derived from one triggering event is retrievable by that single identifier without walking the dependency graph.

Engineering Rule
Events become governed constitutional records once validated. Every constitutional object carries the Correlation Identifier of the event that caused its creation or revision. One event's complete downstream footprint is retrievable by correlation ID across every engine.

2.10 Explainability
Every Constitutional Event shall answer:
What occurred? 
When did it occur? 
Which system generated the event? 
Which workflows were initiated? 
Why were those workflows selected? 
Was the event successfully processed? 
Were duplicate events detected? 

2.11 Engineering Constraints
The Event Architecture shall:
remain event-driven, 
require event validation, 
separate detection from execution, 
support deterministic workflow selection, 
preserve event metadata, 
support deduplication, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable. 

2.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Constitutional Events are established as governed constitutional constructs.
✓ Event detection remains separate from workflow initiation.
✓ Workflow selection is deterministic and version-controlled.
✓ Workflow prioritisation preserves execution integrity.
✓ Event deduplication prevents unnecessary processing.
✓ Event metadata remains immutable after validation.
✓ Every Constitutional Event remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 3
Workflow Execution, Dependency Resolution & Engine Coordination

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Constitutional Orchestration & Intelligence Workflow Engine executes Constitutional Workflows, coordinates participating constitutional engines and resolves execution dependencies. Constitutional Workflows consist of multiple specialised intelligence engines that collectively produce preventive health intelligence. The Orchestration Engine shall coordinate these engines through governed execution sequences while preserving constitutional ownership, reproducibility and explainability. The objective is to ensure that every workflow executes efficiently, deterministically and consistently regardless of workflow complexity.

Constitutional Principle
Coordinate constitutional engines according to their dependencies rather than their implementation order.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
Constitutional engines operate independently.
However, they are not independent of one another. Many engines require intelligence generated by upstream engines before they can execute correctly. The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall therefore coordinate execution through explicit dependency relationships rather than arbitrary sequencing. Execution order emerges from constitutional dependencies.

Founder Principle
Dependencies should determine execution rather than assumptions.

engine availability, 
workflow priority, 
execution efficiency,

Scheduling determines when engines execute.

Founder Principle
Schedule intelligently without changing intelligence.

The Constitutional Orchestration Engine shall coordinate:
workflow state, 
engine readiness, 
dependency completion, 
execution timing, 
completion confirmation, 
downstream notifications. 
Participating engines remain unaware of the broader workflow beyond their constitutional interfaces.

Constitutional Principle
Engines should specialise in intelligence while orchestration specialises in coordination.

recovery checkpoints. 
Workflow State shall never modify constitutional intelligence. It exists solely to coordinate execution.

Founder Principle
Execution state should coordinate work rather than contain intelligence.

3.9 The User Erasure Workflow
Erasure is a first-class Constitutional Workflow owned and orchestrated by ENG-009. Policy is owned by Document 1C (ER-28/ER-29); the per-store shred and audit mechanics are owned by the individual engines. ENG-009 owns the sequence, completion and verification of erasure across all stores; no engine erases in isolation.
The workflow is triggered by a validated erasure event (a user erasure request, or a source-consent revocation that erases that source's contribution per 1C §3.3 / ER-29). On trigger, ENG-009 coordinates per-user crypto-shredding across every store that holds the user's keyed data, in an order that preserves the mandatory belief-review bridge:ing execution, the Orchestration Engine shall maintain a governed Workflow State.
Observation Store (ENG-002 §11.11a) — crypto-shred observations; emit the withdrawal notification through the transactional-outbox path so dependent beliefs enter review; retain a content-free tombstone and audit record.
Dynamic Health Model (ENG-001) — crypto-shred derived belief state under the same per-user key; the belief-review signal from step 1 is consumed here.
Conversation State (ENG-005 §7.3a) — erase conversation history under the same mechanism.
Personalisation Objects (ENG-006 §9.8) — reset/erase learned personalisation.
Constitutional Health Record / Memory Objects (ENG-011 §5.9) — perform Constitutional Deletion, retaining deletion provenance and a content-free redaction event only.
Decision, Prediction and Safety objects derived from the erased user — shred under the same per-user key.
Every step appends a content-free redaction/tombstone event for audit; no step retains erased PII in any audit record (ENG-002 §11.11a; ENG-011 §5.9). Because a committed DHM version and its downstream objects live in independently-owned stores (§6.7a), erasure is checkpointed (§4.7): a failure mid-sequence leaves the workflow in partial completion (§4.8) and resumes, and the workflow is idempotent (§5.4) so a re-issued erasure request does not double-shred or fail.
Erasure is complete only when every keyed store has confirmed shred and emitted its redaction event. ENG-009 records a single Erasure Completion record — a content-free attestation, spanning all participating stores, that erasure occurred and when. Erasure-induced loss of replay for the erased user is the correct and ratified outcome (1C §3.2 / ER-28), not an execution-integrity failure (see §5.7 / §5.9).

Engineering Rule
Erasure is one ENG-009-owned Constitutional Workflow that shreds every keyed store in a belief-review-preserving order, is checkpointed and idempotent, retains only content-free redaction events, and completes only when all stores confirm. No engine erases outside this workflow.

3.10 Completion Validation
Before a workflow is declared complete, the Orchestration Engine shall verify:
all required engines executed successfully, 
dependency requirements satisfied, 
workflow outputs published, 
audit records completed, 
execution integrity maintained. 
Incomplete workflows shall not be published as complete.

Engineering Rule

5.3a Three Reproducibility Guarantees — Normative Definitions
The platform uses three distinct reproducibility guarantees. They are not interchangeable, and "reproducible" or "reconstructed" without qualification shall be read as one of the following:
Deterministic re-execution. Given identical workflow definition, engine versions, constitutional inputs, Scientific Knowledge Object versions, governance configuration and dependency graph, re-running the work yields materially equivalent constitutional outputs (§5.3). Applies only to non-generative work: significance gating, validation, grounding resolution, confidence computation, decision selection, cascade traversal. Does not require identical wording.
Stored-artifact retrieval. Model-generated natural language cannot be regenerated bit-for-bit. Its reproducibility is achieved by storing the delivered artifact verbatim and pinning it to a cross-engine manifest; reconstruction means retrieving the stored artifact and re-inspecting it against its pinned inputs (ENG-005 §10.4a). Applies to generative output — delivered communications, reports, notifications.
Historical snapshot retrieval. A past constitutional state is preserved as an immutable Historical State and retrieved as-stored; never regenerated using current platform knowledge (ENG-011 §3.3, §3.5). Applies to historical reconstruction of a user's health understanding at a point in time.
A single 'as-of' state spans all engines through the aligned version manifests (ENG-005 §10.4a; ENG-001/002/003/004 manifests). Deterministic re-execution (a) reproduces non-generative reasoning; retrieval (b, c) reproduces generative and historical artifacts. Replay (§5.5) combines them: non-generative stages may be re-executed under (a), while generated and historical artifacts are retrieved under (b) and (c) and are never regenerated.

Engineering Rule
"Reproducible" is not one guarantee. Non-generative work is reproducible by deterministic re-execution; generated and historical artifacts are reproducible by retrieval. No implementation shall attempt bit-for-bit regeneration of model-generated output.

6.7a Cross-Store Consistency & the DHM Session Boundary
The DHM single-writer session (ENG-001 §3.12a) is atomic for the Dynamic Health Model only: one session commits exactly one DHM version, including its cascade, or none of it. Downstream engines that own separate stores — the Decision store (ENG-004 §2.6a), the Prediction store (ENG-007), Safety Objects (ENG-010) — are written after and outside the DHM session, in dependency order (§6.4). A workflow is therefore a checkpointed sequence (§4.7), not one platform-wide transaction.
The intermediate state in which a DHM version has committed but its downstream Decision, Prediction or published response do not yet exist is a valid, governed state, represented through Object Validity Status (§6.7): the downstream objects are Awaiting Dependency or Pending Refresh, never treated as missing or corrupt. If a downstream write fails after the DHM has committed, the workflow enters partial completion (§4.8) and resumes from the last checkpoint; the committed DHM version is never rolled back to compensate for a downstream failure, because it is independently valid.

Engineering Rule
DHM-session atomicity does not extend across independently-owned downstream stores. Cross-store consistency is eventual and checkpoint-governed; a committed DHM version with pending downstream objects is a valid state, not an integrity failure.
