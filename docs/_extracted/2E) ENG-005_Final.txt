Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define how BioSense transforms trusted intelligence into clear, accurate, personalised and constitutionally compliant communication.
The Conversation, Explanation & Intelligence Delivery Engine is responsible for presenting BioSense's understanding to users in a way that is:
understandable, 
scientifically accurate, 
appropriately cautious, 
context-aware, 
explainable, 
actionable, 
consistent with the BioSense Constitution. 
Unlike a general-purpose conversational AI, this engine does not generate health knowledge.
It communicates health knowledge that has already been established by the upstream intelligence engines.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense communicates what it knows. It does not invent what it does not know.

1.2 Scope
This specification governs:
conversational responses, 
Health AI interactions, 
biomarker explanations, 
Discovery explanations, 
recommendation explanations, 
"Latest Intelligence" summaries, 
home screen insights, 
weekly reports, 
monthly reports, 
notification content, 
educational content, 
blood test interpretations, 
longitudinal narratives, 
coaching responses, 
follow-up conversations. 
It deliberately excludes:
observation processing (ENG-002), 
confidence formation (ENG-003), 
decision generation (ENG-004), 
user interface implementation, 
notification delivery infrastructure. 
Its responsibility begins only after trusted intelligence has already been established.

1.3 Authority
The Conversation Engine consumes outputs from every previous engine.
It may consume:
Dynamic Health Model objects, 
Health Beliefs, 
Discoveries, 
Biological Identity, 
Behavioural Identity, 
Confidence Objects, 
Explanation Objects, 
Decision Objects, 
Canonical Observations, 
User Goals. 
It shall not modify any upstream engine.

Engineering Rule
The Conversation Engine is read-only with respect to all upstream intelligence.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
The purpose of conversation is not to answer questions.
The purpose of conversation is to help the user understand their health.
Every response should therefore optimise:
understanding, 
trust, 
clarity, 
context, 
scientific integrity, 
user autonomy. 
The engine shall never optimise for:
conversation length, 
entertainment, 
engagement, 
persuasive behaviour, 
unnecessary interaction. 

Founder Principle
Conversation exists to improve understanding—not to prolong conversation.

1.5 Constitutional Definition
For engineering purposes:
The Conversation, Explanation & Intelligence Delivery Engine is the constitutional system responsible for translating trusted BioSense intelligence into human-understandable communication without altering its meaning, confidence or constitutional authority.
The engine is therefore an interpreter of intelligence rather than a creator of intelligence.

Constitutional Principle
Communication should preserve meaning, not reinterpret it.

1.5a Normative Enumerations & Glossary
Where this specification lists 'examples' that gate behaviour — intents (2.5), conversation states (4.3), delivery channels (1.7, 6.3) and validation outcomes (9.11) — those sets shall be closed, versioned enumerations, marked normative rather than illustrative, since the intent set in particular determines which intelligence is eligible for a response. A normative glossary shall also fix terms that overlap with upstream specifications: 'Structured Explanation' relative to ENG-003's Explanation Object, and 'Narrative' relative to ENG-001's Health Narrative.
Engineering Rule
Behaviour-gating vocabularies are closed, versioned and normative; shared terms are aligned to their owning specification.
1.6 Communication Is Not Reasoning
A strict architectural boundary exists between reasoning and communication.
Reasoning occurs within:
ENG-001 
ENG-002 
ENG-003 
ENG-004 
Communication begins only after those processes have completed.
The Conversation Engine shall never:
generate new Health Beliefs, 
create new confidence, 
alter Decision Objects, 
reinterpret observations, 
change constitutional safety outcomes. 
It communicates.
It does not reason.

Engineering Rule
The Conversation Engine shall never become a second reasoning engine.

1.7 Intelligence Delivery
Conversation represents only one method through which BioSense communicates.
The same underlying intelligence may be delivered through:
interactive conversation, 
home screen summaries, 
Latest Intelligence, 
weekly reports, 
monthly reports, 
biomarker pages, 
blood test reports, 
goal updates, 
educational cards, 
push notifications, 
clinician-ready exports. 
Every delivery channel consumes the same underlying intelligence objects.
Only presentation changes.

Founder Principle
One source of truth. Many methods of communication.

1.8 Explainability
Every response produced by the engine shall remain traceable to its originating intelligence.
BioSense shall always be capable of explaining:
Why this response was generated. 
Which Health Beliefs contributed. 
Which observations supported it. 
Which confidence assessments influenced it. 
Which Decision Objects informed it. 
Which uncertainties remain. 
If this cannot be demonstrated, the response is constitutionally incomplete.

Constitutional Principle
Every explanation should itself be explainable.

1.9 Human-Centred Communication
BioSense shall optimise for human understanding.
This includes:
plain language, 
progressive disclosure, 
contextual education, 
appropriate terminology, 
emotional neutrality, 
encouragement without coercion, 
transparency regarding uncertainty. 
Scientific accuracy shall never be sacrificed.
Communication shall adapt to the user's level of understanding while preserving the underlying truth.

Founder Principle
Explain complexity without hiding it.

On Acceptance Criteria
Acceptance criteria throughout this specification shall be objective, automatable assertions rather than aspirations. Each should be restated verifiably — for example: 'every delivered response resolves each significant factual claim to a grounding token'; 'a response with an unresolved claim is never delivered'; 'the communicated confidence band equals the source Confidence Object band'; 'user input never enters the trusted-context channel'.
Engineering Rule
A criterion that cannot be checked by a test or a defined inspection is not an acceptance criterion. This applies to every chapter's acceptance section.
1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ The Conversation Engine is clearly separated from reasoning engines.
✓ Communication is established as a read-only consumer of trusted intelligence.
✓ Multiple delivery channels are recognised as consumers of the same intelligence.
✓ Explainability requirements are explicitly defined.
✓ Human understanding is established as the primary communication objective.
✓ The engine is constitutionally prohibited from creating new health intelligence.
✓ Architectural ownership boundaries are unambiguous.
Chapter 2
Context Assembly & Grounding Architecture

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Conversation, Explanation & Intelligence Delivery Engine assembles, validates and grounds the information used to generate every BioSense communication.
Large Language Models are highly effective at generating natural language.
They are not authoritative sources of health knowledge.
BioSense shall therefore construct every response from trusted intelligence that has already been established elsewhere within the platform.
The Conversation Engine is responsible for assembling that intelligence into a structured Conversation Context before language generation begins.

Constitutional Principle
Language shall be generated from trusted intelligence, never used to discover it.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
The quality of a response depends primarily upon the quality of its context.
Rather than exposing the language model to the entirety of the user's health record, the Conversation Engine shall provide only the information necessary to answer the user's request accurately.
Context assembly is therefore a process of intelligent selection rather than indiscriminate retrieval.
The objective is:
accuracy, 
relevance, 
explainability, 
efficiency, 
constitutional safety. 

Founder Principle
The best conversations begin with the right context, not the most context.

2.3 Context Sources
The Conversation Engine may assemble context from the following authoritative sources.
Dynamic Health Model (ENG-001)
Health Beliefs 
Discoveries 
Biological Identity 
Behavioural Identity 
User Goals 
Health Narratives 

Observation Engine (ENG-002)
Canonical Observation references 
Observation summaries 
Evidence provenance 
Quality Profiles 

Confidence Engine (ENG-003)
Confidence Objects 
Explanation Objects 
Remaining uncertainty 
Competing hypotheses 

Decision Intelligence Engine (ENG-004)
Decision Objects 
Active interventions 
Expected Health Impact 
Timing 
Intensity 
Outcome evaluations 

Additional sources may include:
user preferences, 
conversation history, 
educational library, 
canonical scientific library. 
The Conversation Engine shall not access information outside approved constitutional sources.

Engineering Rule
Every context element shall possess an identifiable authoritative source.

2.3a Engine Interface Contracts
The sources above are enumerated but not contracted. For each upstream engine the interface shall specify what is read, that it is read strictly read-only, and the versions it is pinned to. The Conversation Engine consumes beliefs, confidence, decisions and observations without modifying any of them (consistent with 1.6 and with ENG-003's rule that the communication layer consumes intelligence and never alters it).
Every concept consumed here shall be defined in its owning specification. 'Explanation Objects' (ENG-003), 'Health Narratives', 'Behavioural Identity' and 'User Goals' (ENG-001) and 'Expected Health Impact' (ENG-004) shall be defined in those specifications or added to them; they are not assumed to exist.
Engineering Rule
Every upstream input is read-only, version-pinned, and defined in its owning specification. Communication consumes intelligence; it never writes upstream engines.
2.4 Context Assembly Pipeline
Every response shall follow the same constitutional assembly process.
User Intent

↓

Intent Classification

↓

Relevant Health Beliefs

↓

Relevant Discoveries

↓

Relevant Observations

↓

Confidence Objects

↓

Decision Objects

↓

Explanation Objects

↓

Educational References

↓

Conversation Context

↓

Language Model

↓

BioSense Response
The Language Model consumes the assembled Conversation Context.
It does not independently retrieve platform intelligence.

Constitutional Principle
Grounding precedes generation.

2.5 Intent Classification
Before assembling context, the Conversation Engine shall determine the user's primary intent.
Examples include:
explanation, 
education, 
recommendation clarification, 
biomarker interpretation, 
progress review, 
goal discussion, 
comparison, 
troubleshooting, 
general health education. 
Intent Classification determines which intelligence objects are eligible for inclusion.

Founder Principle
Understand the question before assembling the answer.

2.5a Intent Confidence & Ambiguity Fallback
Intent Classification gates which intelligence is eligible for a response, so a silent misclassification produces a confident but wrong or incomplete answer. Intent classification shall therefore carry a confidence, and where intent is ambiguous or low-confidence the engine shall fail safe — assembling broader context and, where appropriate, asking a clarifying question (4.5) — rather than committing to a single intent. Misclassification errs toward more context or clarification, never toward a narrow, confident answer.
Engineering Rule
Intent classification carries a confidence; ambiguity triggers broader context or clarification, not a committed wrong intent.
2.6 Context Selection
Only context relevant to the identified intent shall be assembled.
Example:
User asks:
"Why is my HRV low?"
Relevant context may include:
Recovery Health Belief 
Sleep Health Belief 
Recent HRV observations 
Recent illness 
Active interventions 
Confidence explanation 
Irrelevant information such as historical Vitamin D status or hydration education shall not be included.
Context selection shall minimise noise while preserving completeness.

2.7 Context Prioritisation
Where multiple relevant intelligence objects exist, the Conversation Engine shall prioritise context according to:
constitutional relevance, 
confidence, 
Expected Health Impact, 
recency, 
explanatory value, 
user intent. 
Context shall remain bounded.
It shall not exceed practical limits required for accurate reasoning.

Constitutional Principle
Prioritise information that improves understanding, not information that merely exists.

2.8 Context Integrity
Every Conversation Context shall preserve:
source attribution, 
version references, 
confidence, 
uncertainty, 
evidence provenance. 
Context assembly shall never alter the underlying intelligence.
It shall only organise it for communication.

Engineering Rule
The Conversation Context is a view of intelligence, not a modification of intelligence.

2.9 Missing Information
Where required context is unavailable, the Conversation Engine shall recognise the limitation explicitly.
Examples include:
disconnected wearable, 
missing laboratory data, 
insufficient longitudinal evidence, 
incomplete behavioural history. 
The engine may:
communicate uncertainty, 
request additional information, 
recommend evidence collection, 
defer conclusions. 
It shall never fabricate missing context.

Founder Principle
Missing context should lead to honest uncertainty, not confident invention.

2.10 Context Freshness
Before response generation, the Conversation Engine shall verify that context remains current.
Checks may include:
recent observations, 
updated Decision Objects, 
revised confidence, 
active intervention status, 
Dynamic Health Model version. 
If newer intelligence exists, the Conversation Context shall be refreshed before language generation.

Constitutional Principle
Responses should reflect the current state of understanding, not stale intelligence.

2.10a Read-Consistency — Pin the Response to a Version Snapshot
Because the platform updates continuously, the Dynamic Health Model can change between context assembly, generation and validation. Freshness (2.10) is therefore a read-consistency requirement, not a race: the entire response — assembly, generation and validation — shall run against a single pinned engine-version snapshot. 'Freshness' selects the pin; once selected, newer intelligence produces a new response rather than being swapped in mid-flight, so a delivered response is always internally consistent with one state of understanding.
Engineering Rule
A response is pinned to one read-consistent version snapshot end to end. Newer intelligence triggers a new response, never a mid-flight swap.
2.11 Explainability
Every assembled Conversation Context shall answer:
Why was this information selected? 
Which sources contributed? 
Which intelligence was intentionally excluded? 
Which confidence assessments influenced inclusion? 
What uncertainty remains? 
Which version of each engine supplied the information? 

2.12 Engineering Constraints
The Context Assembly Framework shall:
consume only authoritative intelligence, 
preserve complete source attribution, 
support intent-driven context selection, 
minimise irrelevant information, 
remain explainable, 
remain deterministic in selection, grounding and validation (generative wording excepted, per 9.10),
remain fully auditable. 

2.13 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every response begins with Intent Classification.
✓ Context is assembled only from authoritative BioSense engines.
✓ The Language Model consumes an assembled Conversation Context rather than raw platform data.
✓ Context selection remains relevant, bounded and explainable.
✓ Missing information is communicated honestly.
✓ Context freshness is verified before response generation.
✓ Every Conversation Context preserves complete source attribution and engine version references.
Chapter 3
Explanation Engine & Structured Intelligence Communication

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how BioSense constructs clear, accurate and constitutionally compliant explanations from trusted platform intelligence.
An explanation is more than a collection of facts.
It is a structured interpretation of trusted understanding designed to help the individual comprehend:
what BioSense knows, 
why it knows it, 
how certain it is, 
what it recommends, 
what uncertainty remains. 
Every explanation generated by BioSense shall follow a consistent constitutional structure regardless of the communication channel.

Constitutional Principle

No response shall be delivered solely because it is linguistically plausible.

A fluent response is not necessarily a truthful response.

The Validation Framework determines whether that response faithfully communicates the underlying intelligence.
Validation exists to protect:
scientific accuracy,

regulatory readiness.

9.4a Grounding & Hallucination Validation Mechanism
Grounding Validation (9.4) and Hallucination Detection (9.8) are the platform's final safety barrier and shall be specified as a mechanism, not only an intent. The generated response is decomposed into its significant factual claims — any statement asserting a fact about the individual's health, a physiological relationship, a measurement, a confidence, or a recommendation. Each such claim shall carry, or must resolve to, a grounding token identifying the source intelligence object (Health Belief, Confidence Object, Decision Object, Observation, Structured Explanation, or Scientific-Library entry). A claim that does not resolve is not presented as fact: it is stripped, revised, or the response regenerated.
This resolution shall be performed by a non-generative resolver, not by a second language model alone. The validation framework is partly non-generative: a deterministic grounding resolver, a confidence-versus-language consistency check (the communicated band must equal the Confidence Object band), a safety-template check (8.6a) and a scope/PII check. A generative reviewer may assess tone and consistency but is never the sole guarantee — consistent with the Constitutional Review layer of ENG-001. 'Significant factual statement' is defined above and shall be maintained as governed configuration.
Engineering Rule
Every significant factual claim must resolve to a grounding token via a non-generative resolver; unresolved claims are never delivered. Validation is partly non-generative, never sole-LLM self-critique.

9.11a Bounded Validation, Latency & Reject Behaviour
The regenerate-on-failure loop (9.8, 9.11) shall be bounded: a maximum number of regeneration attempts, after which the response falls back to a safe, fully-grounded message rather than looping. Each response has a defined validation latency and cost budget; the deterministic checks (9.4a) run first and cheaply, and generative review runs only where needed, so validation scales.
The user-facing behaviour of each outcome shall be defined. In particular, a Rejected response shall never surface a fabricated answer or a raw error; it surfaces a safe, grounded fallback (for example, an honest statement of uncertainty or a suggestion to consult a professional where constitutionally justified).
Engineering Rule
Regeneration is bounded and falls back to a safe grounded message; validation has a latency budget with deterministic checks first; Reject never yields a fabricated answer.
