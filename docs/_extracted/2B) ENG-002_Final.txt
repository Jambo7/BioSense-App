Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
This specification defines the engineering architecture of the BioSense Observation Engine and the Canonical Observation Store.
The Observation Engine is responsible for receiving, validating, normalising, versioning and preserving every observation used by BioSense.
The Observation Store represents the constitutional source of objective truth within the BioSense platform.
Unlike the Dynamic Health Model, which stores interpreted understanding, the Observation Store stores only validated observations.
Every Health Belief, Discovery, Narrative, Biological Identity and Recommendation defined within ENG-001 ultimately depends upon the quality and integrity of the observations defined within this specification.
The purpose of this document is therefore to ensure that BioSense reasons from trusted evidence rather than inconsistent or ambiguous data.

1.2 Scope
This specification defines:
Observation Engine architecture 
Canonical Observation Store 
Observation lifecycle 
Validation pipeline 
Canonical translation 
Version control 
Provenance 
Data quality 
Observation relationships 
Observation integrity 
Source management 
Engineering constraints 
Acceptance criteria 
This specification deliberately excludes:
Health Beliefs 
Confidence calculation 
Recommendations 
Conversation logic 
Health Score 
Biological Age 
Dynamic Health Model reasoning 
Those subjects are defined in separate engineering specifications.

1.3 Authority
The Observation Engine shall operate in accordance with:
BioSense Intelligence Constitution (1A–1C) 
ENG-001 Dynamic Health Model Engineering Specification 
Where any implementation conflicts with those governing documents:
The Constitution shall take precedence.
The Dynamic Health Model specification shall take precedence over implementation decisions affecting personalised understanding.

2. Design Philosophy

2.1 Core Principle
The Observation Store is not an intelligence system.
It does not interpret.
It does not diagnose.
It does not recommend.
It does not reason.
Its responsibility is far simpler—and equally important.
It preserves objective evidence.

Constitutional Principle
The Observation Store preserves facts. The Dynamic Health Model creates understanding.

2.2 Engineering Definition
For engineering purposes:
An Observation is a timestamped, version-controlled, evidence-backed record describing something that was measured, reported or observed about an individual without adding interpretation.
Every word is significant.
Timestamped
Every observation exists at a specific point in time.

Version-controlled
Observations are never overwritten.
Corrections create new versions.

Evidence-backed
Every observation has identifiable provenance.

Without Interpretation
The Observation Store never concludes what an observation means.
Interpretation belongs exclusively within the Dynamic Health Model.

2.3 Why the Observation Store Exists
Modern health data originates from many independent sources:
Wearables 
Blood laboratories 
Medical records 
User questionnaires 
Nutrition apps 
Manual entry 
Future devices 
Each source uses:
different terminology, 
different units, 
different timestamps, 
different confidence, 
different quality standards. 
Without a canonical Observation Store:
duplicate observations would exist, 
conflicting units would appear, 
provenance would be lost, 
reasoning would become inconsistent, 
explainability would fail. 
The Observation Store therefore creates a single constitutional representation of objective evidence before any reasoning begins.

2.4 Engineering Principles
Every implementation of the Observation Engine shall satisfy the following principles.
Principle 1 — Observations Are Facts
Observations represent measured or reported information.
They shall never contain interpretation.
Example:
Observation:
Ferritin = 24 µg/L
Not:
"Iron stores are declining."
That conclusion belongs to the Dynamic Health Model.

Principle 2 — Every Observation Has Provenance
Every observation shall identify:
where it originated, 
how it was collected, 
when it was collected, 
how it entered BioSense. 
Anonymous observations are constitutionally invalid.

Principle 3 — Observations Are Immutable
Observations are append-only objects.
Errors are corrected through versioning.
The original observation remains permanently recoverable.

Principle 4 — Canonical Before Storage
Every observation shall be transformed into BioSense's canonical format before entering the Observation Store.
Internal reasoning engines should never require source-specific translation.

Principle 5 — One Observation Exists Once
Duplicate observations shall never create duplicate evidence.
Repeated synchronisation should identify existing observations rather than inflate evidence.

Principle 6 — Validation Before Persistence
Every observation shall pass constitutional validation before it becomes eligible for storage.
Invalid observations shall never influence the Dynamic Health Model.

Principle 7 — Separation of Fact and Meaning
The Observation Store never performs reasoning.
The Dynamic Health Model never stores raw observations.
These responsibilities shall remain permanently separated.

4. Acceptance Criteria (Chapter 1)

Chapter 2
Canonical Observation Model

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the canonical engineering model used to represent every observation stored within BioSense.
Regardless of origin, every observation shall ultimately be represented using a common structure.
This enables every downstream subsystem to consume observations consistently without requiring source-specific logic.
The Canonical Observation Model therefore becomes the universal language of evidence within the BioSense platform.

Constitutional Principle
Every source may speak differently. Every observation shall be stored in one canonical language.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Observation sources are heterogeneous.
Examples include:
WHOOP 
Apple Health 
Garmin 
Oura 
Fitbit 
Polar 
Laboratory Information Systems 
PDF Blood Reports 
Manual User Entry 
Daily Check-ins 
Nutrition Platforms 
Medical Records 
Future APIs 
Each source possesses:
different naming conventions, 
different timestamps, 
different measurement units, 
different precision, 
different metadata. 
The Observation Engine shall normalise these differences before persistence.
The Canonical Observation Store shall therefore remain completely source-independent.

2.3 Definition
A Canonical Observation is defined as:
A validated, immutable, timestamped and version-controlled representation of a single objective fact concerning an individual's health, expressed using BioSense's standard internal schema.
A Canonical Observation contains no interpretation.
It contains only evidence.

2.4 Observation Granularity
Each Canonical Observation shall represent one measurable fact.
Examples:
✔ Ferritin = 24 µg/L
✔ Resting Heart Rate = 49 bpm
✔ Sleep Duration = 7h 18m
✔ Body Weight = 81.4 kg
✔ Energy Level = 6/10
Not:
✘ Blood Test
✘ Daily Summary
✘ Health Report
Large documents shall be decomposed into multiple canonical observations.

Engineering Rule
One observation shall represent one fact.

2.5 Canonical Observation Object
Every observation shall contain the following mandatory attributes.

Identity
Observation ID 
Individual ID 
Observation Version 
Observation Type 

Source
Source System 
Source Object ID 
Source Record Version 
Source Collection Method 
Examples:
WHOOP API
Apple Health
PDF Extraction
Manual Entry
Clinician Portal
Future Integration

Timing
Measurement Timestamp 
Observation Timestamp 
Ingestion Timestamp 
Time Zone 
Time Precision 
The distinction between these timestamps is constitutionally important.

Measurement Timestamp
When the measurement actually occurred.

Observation Timestamp
When the source recorded it.

Ingestion Timestamp
When BioSense received it.

2.5a Object Specification Convention & ENG-001 Reference Contract
The Canonical Observation is defined here (2.5, 2.6) and again in 5.5 using different attribute lists. These shall be reconciled into one normative Data Dictionary that is the single definition of the object. Every attribute — here and in 5.5 — shall specify data type, cardinality (1:1 or 1:N), whether it is required, and its permitted value set. Attribute names alone are not an implementable schema.
The Data Dictionary shall include, as required fields, the identifiers ENG-001 pins to: observation_id and observation_version. ENG-001 references an observation as ObservationRef{id, version}; this reference contract is binding and shall not change without a coordinated ENG-001 amendment.
Engineering Rule
One object, one schema. 2.5, 2.6 and 5.5 shall describe the same fields, and no engine may rely on an attribute that has no Data Dictionary entry.
2.6 Measurement
Every observation shall include:
Metric Name 
Canonical Metric ID 
Raw Value 
Canonical Value 
Unit 
Canonical Unit 
Precision 
Examples:
Raw:
98.6 °F
Canonical:
37.0 °C

Raw:
5.2 mmol/L
Canonical:
94 mg/dL
(where clinically appropriate and according to BioSense's canonical standards)

Engineering Rule
Raw values shall always be preserved.
Canonical values shall always be stored separately.

2.7 Validation
Every observation shall record its validation status.
Examples:
Validated
Pending Review
Rejected
Estimated
Incomplete
Corrected
Validation Status determines whether an observation may participate in reasoning.

Constitutional Principle
Unvalidated observations shall never influence the Dynamic Health Model.

2.8 Provenance
Every observation shall preserve complete provenance.
Required attributes include:
Original Source 
Collection Method 
Device 
Laboratory 
Parser Version 
OCR Version (where applicable) 
API Version 
Import Method 
Provenance shall never be removed.

2.9 Quality Assessment
Every observation shall possess a Quality Profile.
Example attributes include:
Source Reliability
Measurement Precision
Completeness
Consistency
Verification Status
Data Integrity
Quality Score
The Observation Engine shall not interpret quality.
It shall preserve it.

2.10 Relationships
Observations may possess relationships.
Examples:
Blood Sample
↓
Ferritin
↓
Iron
↓
Transferrin Saturation
↓
Haemoglobin
These observations originate from the same laboratory event.
Relationships shall preserve context without combining observations.

Engineering Rule

3.4a External Data Trust → Ingestion Composition
ENG-012 owns the Constitutional Boundary and produces an External Data Trust Decision for every inbound dataset (ENG-012 §4.9); ENG-002 owns ingestion and observation quality. The two gates compose deterministically, so a limited-trust dataset is neither silently dropped nor silently promoted:
Trusted — proceed through the full ingestion pipeline normally
Trusted with Limitations — proceed through ingestion, and carry the limitation forward into the Observation Quality Profile (§4.3): the ENG-012 limitation degrades Source Reliability and/or Provenance Completeness rather than blocking ingestion. Trust limitations lower observation quality; they never silently raise it, and they are never discarded.
Pending Review — defer ingestion; the dataset is held, not persisted, until ENG-012 resolves the trust decision. No belief-affecting observation is created from a pending dataset. 
Retry Required — do not ingest; the dataset re-enters at the ENG-012 boundary under ENG-012's retry handling. 
Rejected — do not ingest; no observation is created. The rejection is audited at the boundary.
External Data Trust remains independent from observation quality and from Health Belief confidence (ENG-012 §4.3): it governs whether and how a dataset enters ingestion, mapped here to Quality Profile inputs, and never sets clinical meaning.

Engineering Rule
Every externally-sourced dataset carries an ENG-012 Trust Decision into ingestion. Trusted proceeds; Trusted-with-Limitations proceeds and degrades Quality Profile source/provenance fields; Pending defers; Retry re-enters the boundary; Rejected creates no observation. The mapping is deterministic and the two gates never silently override one another.

3.12a Canonical Ingestion Decision Procedure
Duplicate detection (3.10), version assessment (3.12), duplicate synchronisation (6.8), corrections (6.9) and late-arriving evidence (6.10) all decide the same question and shall be governed by one deterministic procedure defined here; those sections reference it rather than restating heuristics.
Every incoming record is reduced to an Observation Identity Key: source_system + source_object_id + source_record_version + canonical_metric_id + measurement_timestamp. The Correction Correlation Key is the same tuple without source_record_version (see 6.9a).
The decision is made in fixed order: (1) if an observation with the same Identity Key already exists, the record is identical and is ignored as an idempotent no-op; (2) else if the Correlation Key matches an existing observation and source_record_version is higher, it is a corrected, superseding version; (3) else if the Correlation Key matches and the measurement_timestamp is older than the individual's latest for that metric, it is late-arriving; (4) otherwise it is an independent new observation.
The outcome is deterministic: the same record always yields the same outcome regardless of arrival path or retry.
Engineering Rule
There shall be exactly one ingestion decision procedure and one Identity Key. No stage may implement its own duplicate or version heuristic.

3.13a Ingestion Concurrency & Ordering
The pipeline is described per observation, but records for the same individual arrive concurrently. Duplicate and version assessment (3.10, 3.12, 3.12a) and persistence for a given individual and canonical metric shall be serialised, so two concurrent syncs cannot interleave a version decision or corrupt lineage. Serialisation is per (individual, canonical_metric); different individuals and metrics proceed in parallel, preserving scale.
This is consistent with, and upstream of, ENG-001's per-individual single-writer reasoning. Observation notifications for one individual shall be delivered in a defined order (by observation_version, then ingestion timestamp) so the Dynamic Health Model can order its triggers.
Engineering Rule
Version and duplicate assessment for one (individual, canonical_metric) shall never run concurrently with itself. Notification ordering per individual shall be defined and preserved.
3.14 Notification

Notification does not trigger reasoning directly.

The DHM independently determines whether reasoning should occur.

Founder Principle
New evidence requests attention. It does not demand new understanding.

3.14a Notification Delivery Guarantee — Transactional Outbox
Persistence (3.13) is atomic, but the notification to the Dynamic Health Model (3.14) is a separate step. If the process fails between commit and delivery, the observation is stored yet the DHM is never informed, so it is never reasoned upon. The canonical sequence is therefore: within the same atomic commit as persistence, write the outbound notification to a transactional outbox; a separate dispatcher then delivers it at-least-once.
The DHM notification shall be idempotent, keyed on observation_id + observation_version, so redelivery is safe. This handoff is the ENG-001 interface: the notification is the trigger ENG-001's trigger registry expects, and it must be delivered exactly the once it is acted upon, or safely more than once.
This canonical sequence — persist, commit outbox in the same transaction, dispatch — supersedes the minor ordering differences between the pipeline diagram (3.4), 3.13/3.14, and the lifecycle in 6.3.
Engineering Rule
No observation is persisted without its notification being committed in the same transaction. The DHM is notified at-least-once and processes notifications idempotently.

3.16a Idempotency Key & Enforcement
Idempotency (3.16, 6.8) requires a key. The ingestion idempotency key is the Observation Identity Key (3.12a). Persistence shall be conditional on it — an insert-if-absent operation — so re-processing the same source record, whether from a retry, a provider resend, or a bulk re-import, is a no-op that returns the already-stored version rather than creating a new one.
Idempotency covers the whole effect, not just the stored row: a re-processed record shall not emit a second audit event or a second DHM notification.
Engineering Rule
Re-ingesting a record with an existing Identity Key shall create no new observation, no new audit event, and no new notification.

6.3a Canonical Operational State Model
Two lifecycle state sequences are given for the same object — 2.13 and 6.3 — with different states. They shall be reconciled into one canonical operational state machine, defined here as authoritative; 2.13's list becomes an informal summary that references it.
Operational state (an observation's position in the pipeline: Detected, Received, Authenticating, Validating, Canonicalising, Quality Assessment, Duplicate Assessment, Persisted, Available for Reasoning, Archived, with exception states Rejected, Quarantined, Awaiting Manual Review) is distinct from version relationship. 'Superseded' is not an operational state; it is a version-lineage property (7.10) of an observation that a newer version has replaced. An observation may be Persisted or Available and also superseded.
Engineering Rule
One object, one operational state machine. Superseded is a version relationship, not an operational state, resolving the 'exactly one state' rule (6.3).

6.9a Correction Correlation
Corrections create a new version (2.14, 4.12, 6.9) and are expressed as 'Correction Of' and 'Supersedes' relationships (7.10), but the rule for matching an incoming correction to the original it supersedes is unspecified. Matching uses the Correction Correlation Key (3.12a): the Identity Key without source_record_version. When a record arrives whose Correlation Key matches an existing observation and whose source_record_version is higher, it supersedes that observation, and the lineage link is recorded explicitly.
Engineering Rule
Corrections are correlated by a defined key and recorded as an explicit version-lineage link. A correction that cannot be correlated is quarantined for review, never stored as an orphan.

6.13a Quarantine & Manual Review Operations
Quarantine (3.6, 3.15, 6.12) and Manual Review (6.13) are entered but never defined as operational states with exits. Each shall have defined entry reasons; allowed transitions (retry, approve, correct, reject, escalate); an owner; and a retention or SLA policy so items do not accumulate unbounded — all fully auditable. Items leave quarantine only by an explicit transition, never silently.
Payloads suspected to be malicious (6.12) are handled differently: isolated, never auto-retried, and escalated to security rather than to data review.
Engineering Rule
Quarantine and Manual Review are defined states with explicit, audited exits and owners. Suspected-malicious input is isolated and escalated, never auto-retried.

support manual review, 
remain fully auditable.

9.4a Dictionary Boundaries & Version Pinning

9.5a Unit Conversion Governance
Canonical values are 'calculated independently' (2.6, 9.5) but with no conversion-factor source, rounding rule, or precision policy — yet the worked example (5.2 mmol/L to 94 mg/dL) already hides a rounding decision. Conversion factors are versioned data; rounding and precision are a defined per-metric policy; the conversion-rule version is recorded on every canonical value.

9.6a Canonical Temporal Model
'Preserve temporal truth' (9.6) requires a concrete model. Each observation shall store a canonical UTC instant, the original local time, the original IANA timezone, and an explicit Time Precision (for example exact, minute, day, or unknown). The three timestamps of 2.5 — measurement, observation, ingestion — each follow this model.
Rules currently unstated and to be defined: a timezone-naive source timestamp is resolved using the source or device timezone, and if none is known the observation is marked reduced temporal quality rather than assumed UTC; DST fall-back ambiguity resolves to a defined choice (for example the earlier instant) and is flagged; device clock skew beyond a defined tolerance routes the observation to review. Terminology shall be consistent: 'Observation Timestamp' (when the source recorded it) is distinct from the sort field 'Observation Time' (10.7) and from an 'Observation Event' (Chapter 7).
Engineering Rule
Time is stored as canonical UTC plus original local time plus IANA timezone plus explicit precision. Naive timestamps, DST ambiguity and clock skew have defined, audited handling; temporal uncertainty is never silently assumed away.

11.11a Lawful Erasure and the ENG-001 Bridge
This specification states in several places that observations are never deleted (2.13, 5.14, 6.11) while 11.11 permits legally-required deletion, and ENG-001 requires right-to-erasure. These are reconciled as follows. Scientific immutability means no silent in-place mutation of evidence; it does not forbid lawful erasure. Lawful erasure is a distinct, audited operation implemented as per-user crypto-shredding (consistent with ENG-001 and Document 1C): the observation's content becomes unrecoverable while a content-free tombstone and an audit record that erasure occurred are retained.
Erasure shall emit a withdrawal notification through the same transactional-outbox path as 3.14a, so that dependent Dynamic Health Model beliefs enter review rather than silently resting on evidence that no longer exists. This is the ENG-001 bridge and is mandatory.
Because erased evidence must not be re-exposed through audit, audit records retained after erasure shall hold references and tombstones only, never the erased PII payload — reconciling 11.7 (audit immutability) with 11.11 (erasure).
This erasure operation is one step of the User Erasure Workflow orchestrated by ENG-009; the withdrawal-notification bridge defined here is invoked as part of that workflow's sequence.
Engineering Rule
Immutability forbids silent mutation, not lawful erasure. Erasure is audited crypto-shred that withdraws evidence, triggers ENG-001 belief review, and leaves no PII in audit. Align 2.13, 5.14 and 6.11 with this.
