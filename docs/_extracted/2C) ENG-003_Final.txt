Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the architecture governing how BioSense evaluates evidence, forms confidence, manages uncertainty and determines the reliability of its understanding.
The Confidence & Evidence Evaluation Engine is responsible for transforming objective observations into quantified confidence that can safely support Health Beliefs, Discoveries, Narratives, Recommendations and future intelligence capabilities.
Unlike the Observation Engine, which determines whether evidence is valid, the Confidence Engine determines how strongly BioSense should trust the conclusions derived from that evidence.
It is therefore responsible for ensuring that BioSense expresses certainty only where certainty has been earned.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence is not generated by intelligence. Confidence is earned through evidence.

1.2 Scope
This specification defines:
Evidence evaluation 
Confidence formation 
Confidence propagation 
Evidence weighting 
Confidence decay 
Confidence recovery 
Conflict resolution 
Belief promotion 
Belief retirement 
Scientific calibration 
Explainability 
Confidence governance 
Confidence auditing 
Operational safeguards 
This specification deliberately excludes:
Observation ingestion 
Canonical observation modelling 
Dynamic Health Model architecture 
Recommendation generation 
Conversation generation 
User interface behaviour 
Those responsibilities are defined within other engineering specifications.

1.3 Authority
This specification operates under:
BioSense Constitution (1A–1C)
↓
ENG-001 Dynamic Health Model
↓
ENG-002 Observation Engine
↓
ENG-003 Confidence Engine
Where conflicts occur:
The Constitution shall prevail.
The Dynamic Health Model defines what BioSense may understand.
The Observation Engine defines what BioSense may observe.
The Confidence Engine defines how strongly BioSense may believe those understandings.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
Health intelligence should never confuse information with certainty.
Large quantities of data do not automatically produce reliable conclusions.
Similarly, a single high-quality observation may not justify a strong Health Belief.
Confidence should emerge gradually through the accumulation, consistency and quality of evidence over time.
The Confidence Engine therefore exists to quantify the strength of BioSense's understanding while preserving appropriate scientific uncertainty.

Founder Principle
Confidence should grow more slowly than evidence.

1.5 Constitutional Definition of Confidence
For engineering purposes:
Confidence represents BioSense's quantified assessment of the reliability of its current understanding, based upon the quality, quantity, consistency and longitudinal behaviour of all relevant evidence currently available.
Confidence is therefore:
dynamic, 
evidence-dependent, 
explainable, 
reversible, 
continuously recalculated. 
Confidence is not:
a probability of disease, 
a diagnosis, 
a prediction, 
a recommendation, 
a measure of user risk. 

Engineering Rule
Confidence shall always describe BioSense's confidence in its own understanding.
Never confidence in future outcomes.

1.6 Confidence Is Not Observation Quality
Observation Quality was defined in ENG-002.
Observation Quality answers:
"How trustworthy is this individual observation?"
Confidence answers:
"How trustworthy is the understanding produced after evaluating all available evidence?"
These concepts shall remain permanently separated.
Example:
A laboratory ferritin result may possess extremely high Observation Quality.
However:
If only one ferritin result exists across six years of history, BioSense's confidence in any long-term iron trend may remain limited.
Conversely:
Thousands of consistent wearable observations may individually possess moderate Observation Quality while collectively producing high confidence in a behavioural pattern.

Constitutional Principle
Evidence quality contributes to confidence. It does not define confidence.

1.7 The Nature of Scientific Confidence
Confidence shall behave more like scientific consensus than machine certainty.
As additional evidence accumulates:
Confidence may:
Increase.
Remain stable.
Decrease.
Reverse completely.
BioSense therefore treats every understanding as provisional rather than permanent.
Understanding evolves continuously as new evidence enters the platform.

Founder Principle
Every belief remains open to revision.

1.8 Confidence Is Continuous
Confidence shall exist as a continuously evolving property.
It is not recalculated only when users open the application.
Confidence may change whenever:
new observations arrive, 
observations are corrected, 
evidence expires operationally, 
conflicting evidence emerges, 
discoveries evolve, 
scientific knowledge changes. 
The Confidence Engine shall ensure confidence is never acted upon while stale. ‘Always-current’ means recomputed in response to a recognised trigger (see 1.8a) and, where not yet recomputed, evaluated lazily on read. It does not mean every belief is recomputed continuously for every user.

1.8a Continuous Means Triggered, Not Constant
This reconciles 'continuous confidence' with ENG-001's rule that no Dynamic Health Model update occurs without a recognised trigger. Confidence is recomputed only in response to a trigger: new or corrected observations, evidence expiry, contradiction, discovery or narrative change, scientific-library change, calibration change, or the ENG-001 Scheduled-Review trigger for time-based decay. Between triggers, a value may be evaluated lazily on read.
'Always-current' therefore describes the effect — confidence is never acted upon while stale — not a mechanism of constant recomputation. Recomputing every belief for every user continuously is infeasible at platform scale; event-triggered plus scheduled plus lazy evaluation delivers the same guarantee affordably.
Engineering Rule
Confidence changes only in response to a recognised trigger, aligned to ENG-001's trigger registry. Continuous does not mean constant.
1.9 Explainability
Every confidence value produced by BioSense shall be explainable.
The platform shall always be capable of answering:
Why is confidence this high? 
Why is confidence this low? 
Which observations contributed? 
Which observations reduced confidence? 
When did confidence last change? 
What additional evidence would increase confidence? 
If these questions cannot be answered, the confidence calculation is constitutionally incomplete.

Engineering Rule
Every confidence value shall possess a complete evidential explanation.

1.10 Epistemic Humility
The Confidence Engine shall embody the principle of epistemic humility.
BioSense shall explicitly recognise uncertainty rather than concealing it.
Examples include:
"Current evidence moderately supports this conclusion."
"Additional blood testing would significantly improve confidence."
"Existing observations are insufficient to establish a stable long-term pattern."
The platform shall never exaggerate certainty for the purpose of improving user confidence.

Constitutional Principle
Admitting uncertainty increases trust.

1.11 Engineering Constraints
The Confidence Engine shall:
remain evidence-driven, 
remain explainable, 
remain reversible, 
support longitudinal reasoning, 
separate observation quality from confidence, 
preserve scientific humility, 
avoid deterministic certainty, 
remain fully auditable. 

On Acceptance Criteria
Acceptance criteria throughout this specification shall be objective, automatable assertions rather than aspirations. 'The Confidence Engine is positioned as the constitutional authority' is a design statement, not a test. Each should be restated verifiably — for example: 'recomputing a belief's confidence at a pinned engine and configuration version reproduces the recorded value'; 'an edge that would create a graph cycle is rejected'; 'a recommendation outcome changes no confidence value (11.10)'; 'confidence is written only through the DHM single-writer path'.
Engineering Rule
A criterion that cannot be checked by a test or a defined inspection is not an acceptance criterion. This applies to every chapter's acceptance section.
1.12 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Confidence is formally distinguished from observation quality.
✓ Confidence represents understanding rather than raw evidence.
✓ Confidence remains dynamic and continuously revisable.
✓ Every confidence value is fully explainable.
✓ Scientific uncertainty is explicitly represented.
✓ Confidence never represents diagnosis, prediction or clinical certainty.
✓ The architecture preserves epistemic humility.
✓ The Confidence Engine is positioned as the constitutional authority governing how strongly BioSense trusts its own understanding.
Chapter 2
Evidence Evaluation Framework

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the Evidence Evaluation Framework used by the Confidence Engine to assess the evidential strength of information available within the Dynamic Health Model.
The framework establishes the constitutional rules governing how BioSense evaluates evidence before confidence is calculated.
Rather than treating every observation equally, the Evidence Evaluation Framework recognises that confidence emerges through the interaction of multiple evidential characteristics over time.
These characteristics collectively determine the reliability of BioSense's understanding.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence is earned through the evaluation of evidence, not merely the accumulation of data.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Evidence possesses many characteristics.
Examples include:
quality, 
quantity, 
consistency, 
recency, 
diversity, 
longitudinal stability, 
independence, 
reproducibility. 
No single characteristic shall determine confidence independently.
Instead, confidence shall emerge through balanced evaluation across multiple dimensions.

Engineering Rule
No individual observation shall independently establish high confidence unless explicitly permitted elsewhere within the constitutional architecture.

2.3 Evidence Evaluation Domains
The Confidence Engine shall evaluate evidence across eight constitutional domains.
These domains are intentionally separated to maximise explainability and future extensibility.
The domains are:
Evidence Quality 
Evidence Quantity 
Evidence Consistency 
Longitudinal Stability 
Evidence Diversity 
Temporal Relevance 
Evidence Independence 
Scientific Alignment 
Each domain contributes independently to confidence formation.

2.3a Reconciliation with ENG-001 Confidence Inputs
ENG-001 enumerates the inputs to confidence; this chapter enumerates eight evaluation domains. These shall be one canonical set, not two. The eight domains of 2.3 are the authoritative realisation of ENG-001's confidence inputs; where ENG-001 lists inputs and ENG-003 lists domains, they refer to the same evidential characteristics under one governed vocabulary.
Engineering Rule
There is one canonical set of confidence inputs and domains, referenced by both ENG-001 and ENG-003. Neither introduces an independent list.
2.4 Domain One — Evidence Quality
Evidence Quality represents the overall trustworthiness of the observations supporting a particular understanding.
This domain consumes the Observation Quality Profiles defined in ENG-002.
Factors may include:
Source reliability 
Measurement methodology 
Provenance completeness 
Validation status 
Structural integrity 
Authenticity 
The Confidence Engine consumes these characteristics.
It does not redefine them.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence inherits evidence quality. It does not replace it.

2.5 Domain Two — Evidence Quantity
Evidence Quantity describes the amount of relevant evidence supporting a Health Belief.
Quantity includes:
number of observations, 
number of independent events, 
number of observation collections, 
observation frequency, 
observation density. 
Large quantities of evidence may increase confidence.
However:
Quantity alone shall never justify high confidence.

Founder Principle
Many observations are valuable. Repeated observations are more valuable.

2.6 Domain Three — Evidence Consistency
Evidence Consistency evaluates whether independent observations support a coherent understanding.
Examples include:
Repeated elevated resting heart rate.
Consistent improvements in sleep duration.
Repeated ferritin decline across multiple laboratory visits.
Consistency strengthens confidence.
Contradictory evidence reduces confidence.
Consistency is assessed across observations rather than within individual observations.

Engineering Rule
Confidence shall increase more readily from consistent evidence than from isolated observations.

2.7 Domain Four — Longitudinal Stability
Health patterns become more reliable when they persist over time.
Examples include:
Three years of stable HRV.
Multiple annual blood tests showing gradual improvement.
Long-term weight stability.
Longitudinal Stability evaluates persistence rather than magnitude.
Short-lived trends shall not be treated as stable patterns.

Constitutional Principle
Time transforms repeated observations into reliable understanding.

2.8 Domain Five — Evidence Diversity
Independent sources supporting the same conclusion strengthen confidence.
Examples:
WHOOP.
Apple Health.
Blood biomarkers.
Nutrition logs.
Daily Check-ins.
Clinical records.
If multiple independent sources converge upon the same understanding, confidence shall increase.

Founder Principle
Agreement between independent sources is stronger than repetition from one source.

2.9 Domain Six — Temporal Relevance
Not all evidence remains equally relevant indefinitely.
Recent evidence may be more representative of current physiology.
Historical evidence remains valuable for longitudinal understanding.
Temporal Relevance evaluates the relationship between observation age and the question being considered.
The weighting mechanism shall be defined in later chapters.

Engineering Rule
Recency influences confidence differently depending on the type of understanding being evaluated.

2.10 Domain Seven — Evidence Independence
The Confidence Engine shall recognise that observations are not always statistically independent.
Example:
HRV.
Recovery Score.
Readiness Score.
These may all originate from the same wearable algorithm.
Evidence generated from the same underlying calculation shall not be treated as multiple independent confirmations.

Constitutional Principle
Correlated evidence should not masquerade as independent evidence.

2.11 Domain Eight — Scientific Alignment
BioSense's understanding shall remain aligned with the Canonical Scientific Library.
Evidence supporting well-established scientific principles may strengthen confidence.
Evidence suggesting novel or unexpected relationships shall require proportionately stronger supporting evidence before confidence increases.
Scientific Alignment influences the burden of evidence.
It does not prohibit novel discoveries.

Founder Principle
Extraordinary conclusions require proportionately stronger evidence.

2.12 Domain Interaction
The evaluation domains shall remain logically independent.
For example:
High-quality evidence with low quantity.
↓
Moderate confidence.

Large quantity with poor quality.
↓
Limited confidence.

Long-term stability with poor consistency.
↓
Reduced confidence.
The Confidence Engine shall evaluate all domains collectively rather than sequentially.

2.13 Domain Evolution
Additional evaluation domains may be introduced in future versions of BioSense.
Existing domains shall remain backwards compatible.
Historical confidence calculations shall remain reproducible using the evaluation framework that existed at the time.

Engineering Rule
Confidence history shall remain reproducible across engine versions.

2.14 Explainability
Every confidence assessment shall be capable of identifying:
Which evaluation domains contributed positively. 
Which domains reduced confidence. 
Which domains lacked sufficient evidence. 
Which domains were not applicable. 
This enables BioSense to explain confidence transparently.

2.15 Engineering Constraints
The Evidence Evaluation Framework shall:
evaluate evidence across multiple domains, 
remain explainable, 
remain extensible, 
preserve independence between evaluation domains, 
support future scientific evolution, 
avoid deterministic scoring from any single characteristic. 

2.16 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Confidence is evaluated across multiple independent evidence domains.
✓ Observation Quality remains distinct from confidence.
✓ Quantity alone cannot create high confidence.
✓ Longitudinal stability is explicitly recognised.
✓ Independent evidence sources strengthen confidence appropriately.
✓ Correlated evidence is not incorrectly treated as independent.
✓ Scientific alignment influences confidence without preventing novel discoveries.
✓ Every confidence assessment remains fully explainable through its contributing domains.
Chapter 3
Confidence Formation & Confidence Lifecycle

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Confidence Engine transforms evaluated evidence into measurable confidence within the Dynamic Health Model.
Confidence is not assigned.
Confidence is not estimated.
Confidence is not guessed.
Confidence emerges through the structured evaluation of evidence according to the constitutional principles defined within this specification.
This chapter defines the lifecycle through which confidence is established, strengthened, weakened, revised and, where necessary, retired.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence is an emergent property of accumulated evidence rather than a manually assigned attribute.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
Confidence should behave like scientific understanding.
When evidence is scarce:
Confidence should remain cautious.
When evidence accumulates:
Confidence should strengthen gradually.
When contradictory evidence appears:
Confidence should reduce appropriately.
When evidence becomes overwhelming:
Confidence may become very strong.
At no point should confidence become absolute.

Founder Principle
The absence of doubt is not the presence of certainty.

3.3 Confidence Formation Process
Every confidence assessment shall progress through a common constitutional process.
Relevant Evidence Identified

↓

Evidence Evaluation

↓

Evidence Domain Assessment

↓

Cross-Domain Balancing

↓

Confidence Formation

↓

Confidence Validation

↓

Confidence Assignment

↓

Dynamic Health Model Updated
Each stage shall remain independently explainable.
No stage may be bypassed.

3.4 Confidence Candidates
Whenever the Dynamic Health Model evaluates a Health Belief, Discovery or Narrative, the Confidence Engine shall generate a Confidence Candidate.
A Confidence Candidate represents a provisional confidence assessment.
It is not yet accepted.
It exists solely for evaluation.
Each Confidence Candidate shall contain:
Target object (Health Belief, Discovery, Narrative, etc.) 
Supporting observations 
Supporting evidence domains 
Conflicting evidence 
Evaluation timestamp 
Engine version 
Only after validation may a Confidence Candidate become an active confidence assessment.

Engineering Rule
Every confidence value shall begin as a Confidence Candidate.

3.5 Domain Evaluation
Each evidence domain defined in Chapter 2 shall be evaluated independently.
The Confidence Engine shall determine:
How strongly each domain supports the understanding. 
Whether any domain weakens the understanding. 
Whether any domain lacks sufficient evidence. 
Whether any domain should be excluded. 
Domains shall never overwrite one another.
Each contributes independently.

3.6 Cross-Domain Balancing
Following independent evaluation, the Confidence Engine shall balance all evidence domains collectively.
Examples:
High Observation Quality.
Low Quantity.
↓
Moderate confidence.

High Quantity.
Poor Consistency.
↓
Limited confidence.

Strong Longitudinal Stability.
Weak Evidence Diversity.
↓
Moderately High confidence.
No individual domain may dominate unless constitutionally authorised.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence should emerge from balance rather than domination.

3.6a Domain Combination Contract
Cross-domain balancing (3.6) is described qualitatively. Because confidence is the platform's central quantity, the combination shall be a single versioned function owned by the data and clinical function: each domain is normalised to a defined scale; the domains are combined by a defined method (not simple averaging) that is monotonic and bounded; correlated evidence is handled per Domain Seven (2.10) so convergence raises confidence only where independence is established; and the result maps to the continuous confidence score and the C0–C5 bands defined in ENG-001. Weights and thresholds are to be confirmed; the contract is binding now.
This realises the confidence-computation contract ENG-001 delegated to the Confidence Engine. 'No domain dominates' (3.6) is expressed as bounds within this function, not left to implementation.
Engineering Rule
Confidence is produced by one versioned combination function that maps the eight domains to ENG-001's score and C0–C5. No implementation may invent its own weighting.
3.7 Confidence Validation
Before confidence is assigned to the Dynamic Health Model, the Confidence Engine shall perform validation.
Validation confirms:
sufficient evidence exists, 
evaluation completed successfully, 
contradictions have been considered, 
engine rules were followed, 
explainability requirements are satisfied, 
audit records are complete. 
If validation fails:
The Confidence Candidate shall be rejected.
No confidence update occurs.

3.8 Confidence Assignment
Once validated, confidence becomes associated with the relevant Dynamic Health Model object.
Examples include:
Health Belief
Discovery
Narrative
Biological Identity
Prediction
Future intelligence objects.
Confidence shall remain attached to the understanding rather than the underlying observations.

Engineering Rule
Confidence belongs to understanding, not evidence.

3.8a Engine Interface Contracts
The Confidence Engine sits between ENG-002 and ENG-001, and its seams shall be explicit. Three contracts are defined. First, ENG-002 to Confidence Engine: the Observation Quality Profile maps to named confidence inputs through a defined mapping (the counterpart of ENG-002's quality-scoring contract), not re-derived per engine. Second, Dynamic Health Model to Confidence Engine: the DHM invokes the engine on a revision or evaluation trigger, supplying the target object and its evidence; the engine returns a confidence assessment and explanation.
Third, and critical for concurrency: confidence is written to the Dynamic Health Model through the DHM's per-individual single-writer path (ENG-001), never by the Confidence Engine writing the DHM directly. The DHM invokes the engine and commits the result within its own reasoning session, preserving ENG-001's serialisation guarantee.
Engineering Rule
The Confidence Engine computes and returns confidence; it does not write the DHM. All confidence writes go through the DHM's single-writer path.
3.9 Confidence States
Every active confidence assessment shall exist in one recognised state.
Proposed states include:
Emerging
Evidence is beginning to accumulate.

Developing
Evidence supports a plausible understanding.

Established
Evidence consistently supports the understanding.

Strong
Longitudinal evidence demonstrates reliable support.

Under Review
Conflicting or newly arrived evidence requires reassessment.

Declining
Confidence is decreasing due to changing evidence.

Retired
The understanding is no longer supported.

Founder Principle
Confidence should evolve through recognised stages rather than abrupt transitions.

3.9a Canonical Confidence Representation & Crosswalk
Confidence is represented in one canonical form: the continuous score in the range 0 to 1 and the C0–C5 bands defined in ENG-001. The confidence states of 3.9 (Emerging, Developing, Established, Strong, Under Review, Declining, Retired) and the threshold categories of 7.4 (Insufficient, Emerging, Informational, Actionable, Established, Discovery) are derived labels over that one representation, not independent scales. 
The threshold category 'Discovery' (7.4) is a confidence gate only; it is distinct from Discovery-the-object, whose definition, lifecycle and provenance are owned by ENG-001 (Chapter 9). Crossing the 'Discovery' confidence threshold is at most a precondition consumed by ENG-001 Discovery validation (ENG-001 §9.7); it does not itself create, promote or define a Discovery.
A single crosswalk shall map score and band ranges to states and to threshold categories, and no term shall carry two meanings: where 'Emerging' and 'Established' appear in both 3.9 and 7.4, the crosswalk fixes one meaning each, or the colliding label is renamed. A confidence state is a property of the assessment; it is not a second belief lifecycle — the belief's lifecycle is owned by ENG-001, and the crosswalk shows how confidence state relates to it.
Engineering Rule
One canonical confidence representation (ENG-001 score plus C0–C5). States and thresholds are derived views defined by one crosswalk; no label means two things.

↓

3.11 Confidence Stability
Confidence should demonstrate inertia.
Minor evidence changes should not produce dramatic confidence fluctuations.
The Confidence Engine shall therefore favour stability over volatility.
Confidence should move gradually unless:
major contradictory evidence appears, 
critical observations are corrected, 
scientific knowledge changes significantly. 

Constitutional Principle
Confidence should be difficult to earn and equally difficult to lose without meaningful evidence.

3.12 Confidence Revision
Confidence shall be recalculated whenever:

observations are corrected, 
observations are retired, 
evidence quality changes,

Confidence history shall never be overwritten.

Founder Principle
Understanding should remember how certain it once was.

3.13a Ownership of the Stored Confidence Object
ENG-001 already defines the stored Confidence Object attached to Dynamic Health Model objects, independently versioned and carrying its history. To avoid two schemas, ENG-001 owns the stored Confidence Object's structure; ENG-003 owns how confidence is computed and what each history entry must contain (previous state, trigger, evidence summary, engine version, reason for change). Engineering Rule

3.14 Explainability

3.15 Engineering Constraints
The Confidence Formation framework shall:

support future evidence domains.

Chapter 4

4.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how confidence changes propagate throughout the Dynamic Health Model when new evidence alters BioSense's understanding.
Health understanding is inherently interconnected.
A change in one Health Belief may influence numerous related beliefs, discoveries, narratives and recommendations.
The Confidence Engine shall therefore manage confidence as a connected network rather than a collection of isolated values.
This chapter defines the constitutional rules governing that propagation.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence may spread through understanding, but it shall never spread without evidence.

4.2 Engineering Philosophy
Health systems rarely operate independently.
Examples include:
Improved sleep
↓
Improved recovery
↓
Improved training capacity
↓
Improved energy
↓
Improved mood
↓
Improved productivity
Similarly:
Declining ferritin
↓
Reduced oxygen transport
↓
Reduced endurance
↓
Reduced exercise capacity
↓
Reduced recovery
↓
Reduced perceived wellbeing
BioSense should therefore recognise interconnected understanding while avoiding uncontrolled propagation.

Engineering Rule
Confidence shall propagate only through defined relationships within the Dynamic Health Model.

4.3 Confidence Dependency Graph

This graph represents how confidence relationships exist between Dynamic Health Model objects.

↓
Discoveries
↓
Narratives
↓
Biological Identity
↓
Recommendations
↓
Predictions
Confidence relationships shall never be inferred dynamically without constitutional support.

4.3a Single Dependency Graph & Ownership
There is one dependency graph in the platform: the belief dependency graph owned by the Dynamic Health Model (ENG-001 truth-maintenance). The 'Confidence Dependency Graph' named here is a view of that same graph, not a second structure. Confidence propagation is confidence recomputation performed as the DHM traverses its graph; ENG-003 defines how much confidence changes along an edge (attenuation, limits), while the DHM owns the graph, its edges, and the traversal order.
The graph is a strict directed acyclic graph maintained under ENG-001's cascade rules (topological traversal, bounded depth, cycle rejection). ENG-003 does not define a separate graph, a separate traversal, or a separate cycle policy.
Engineering Rule
One graph, owned by the DHM. ENG-003 supplies confidence deltas along its edges; it does not maintain a second graph or a different cycle policy.

Steps that involve model-assisted reasoning — for example assessing why a contradiction exists, or proposing competing hypotheses — are not bit-for-bit reproducible; for those, a structured reasoning trace is recorded so the decision is auditable even though the prose is not regenerable.

Deterministic applies to the confidence calculation and graph traversal; model-assisted reasoning is captured by an auditable trace, not regenerated. Determinism claims elsewhere are read with this scope.

Maximum dependency chain.
Circular propagation prevention.

↓
Recovery
↓

↓

This makes Chapter 4 consistent with the anti-circularity rule of 11.6. Confidence propagates strictly downstream — from evidence to belief to narrative to identity to recommendation to prediction — and never upstream into the evidence of an ancestor. A derived object's confidence may inform objects further downstream, but a derived object never becomes evidence for an object upstream of it. Only observations entering through the Observation Engine are evidence.
Engineering Rule
Propagation is downstream-only. A derived object never becomes evidence for an ancestor; only Observation-Engine observations are evidence (11.6).

prevent circular reasoning, 
remain deterministic, 
remain explainable, 
preserve auditability, 
support future dependency types. 

4.15 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:

✓ Propagation weakens appropriately with increasing distance from direct evidence.
✓ Multiple evidence pathways are evaluated independently.
✓ Circular confidence propagation is prevented.
✓ Conflicting evidence limits inappropriate propagation.
✓ Every propagation event remains fully explainable and auditable.
✓ Propagation produces stable, deterministic outcomes.
Chapter 5
Conflicting Evidence Resolution & Contradiction Management

5.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Confidence Engine identifies, evaluates and resolves conflicting evidence without compromising scientific integrity.
Contradictory evidence is an expected characteristic of real-world health data.
The objective of the Confidence Engine is not to eliminate contradiction.
Its objective is to understand it.
Rather than treating disagreement as an engineering failure, BioSense shall treat it as an opportunity to refine its understanding.

Constitutional Principle
Contradictory evidence reduces certainty. It does not invalidate evidence.

5.2 Engineering Philosophy
Human physiology is complex.
Different evidence sources frequently disagree.
Examples include:
Wearables indicating excellent recovery despite poor subjective wellbeing. 
Blood biomarkers improving while symptoms worsen. 
Increased exercise performance despite declining sleep quality. 
Manual reports conflicting with automated measurements. 
Two laboratories producing slightly different values. 
Device recalculations changing historical observations. 
These disagreements should not be ignored or arbitrarily resolved.
Instead, they should become explicit inputs into BioSense's reasoning process.

Founder Principle
The goal is not to remove contradiction. The goal is to understand why contradiction exists.

5.3 Types of Contradiction
The Confidence Engine shall distinguish between different forms of contradiction.
Each type has different implications.
Observation Contradiction
Two observations measuring the same phenomenon disagree.
Example:
Two laboratories report materially different ferritin values collected within a short interval.

Source Contradiction
Independent providers report conflicting measurements.
Example:
Apple Health indicates six hours of sleep.
WHOOP reports seven and a half hours.

Temporal Contradiction
Historical evidence conflicts with recent evidence.
Example:
Five years of improving HRV followed by a sustained decline.

Behavioural Contradiction
Observed behaviour conflicts with reported behaviour.
Example:
The user reports excellent sleep quality.
Wearable data consistently indicates fragmented sleep.

Physiological Contradiction
Different physiological systems appear inconsistent.
Example:
Excellent cardiovascular fitness.
Rapid deterioration in inflammatory biomarkers.

Scientific Contradiction
A conclusion conflicts with the Canonical Scientific Library.
Example:
Evidence appears to support a relationship that is currently weakly supported or not yet established in scientific literature.

Engineering Rule
Contradictions shall be classified before they are evaluated.

5.4 Contradiction Detection
The Confidence Engine shall continuously monitor for conflicting evidence.
Detection may be triggered by:
New observations. 
Corrected observations. 
Confidence recalculation. 
Discovery revision. 
Scientific Library updates. 
Manual clinical review. 
Contradiction detection shall operate continuously rather than only during user interactions.

5.5 Contradiction Assessment
When a contradiction is detected, the engine shall evaluate:
Which evidence disagrees? 
How significant is the disagreement? 
Which evidence possesses higher quality? 
Are the observations independent? 
Is the disagreement temporary or persistent? 
Could the disagreement represent normal biological variation? 
Does the disagreement suggest a previously unknown relationship? 
The objective is understanding rather than immediate resolution.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense should investigate contradiction before attempting to resolve it.

5.6 Resolution Strategies
Not every contradiction should be handled in the same way.
Possible outcomes include:
Preserve Both Interpretations
Both explanations remain plausible.
Confidence decreases.
Further evidence requested.

Prefer Stronger Evidence
Higher-quality or more reliable evidence is provisionally favoured.
Lower-quality evidence remains preserved.

Await Additional Evidence
No conclusion is reached.
The understanding remains under review.

Create Competing Hypotheses
Multiple Health Beliefs may temporarily coexist.
Each maintains its own confidence assessment.

Retire Existing Understanding
New evidence overwhelmingly invalidates the previous understanding.
Historical confidence remains preserved.

Founder Principle
Sometimes the correct answer is: we don't know yet.

5.6a Ownership — the Dynamic Health Model Creates Beliefs
Creating, merging, retiring or maintaining competing Health Beliefs (5.6, 5.7, 8.6) is a Dynamic Health Model operation (ENG-001). The Confidence Engine detects and assesses contradiction and supplies an independent confidence assessment for each hypothesis; the DHM decides whether to instantiate competing beliefs, which to make dominant, and when to retire them. ENG-003 informs; the DHM acts.
Likewise, an evidence request (5.9) is signalled by the Confidence Engine as 'specific additional evidence would raise confidence', but the decision to request it and the act of requesting belong to the recommendation and conversation layers, not the Confidence Engine.
Engineering Rule
The Confidence Engine assesses contradiction and confidence per hypothesis; the DHM creates and retires beliefs, and downstream engines act on evidence requests.
5.7 Competing Hypotheses
The Confidence Engine shall support multiple competing hypotheses when evidence is genuinely inconclusive.
Example:
Hypothesis A
Reduced recovery caused by insufficient sleep.
Confidence:
Developing.

Hypothesis B
Reduced recovery caused by early illness.
Confidence:
Emerging.

Hypothesis C
Reduced recovery caused by excessive training load.
Confidence:
Developing.
The Dynamic Health Model may maintain multiple concurrent explanations until sufficient evidence allows one to become dominant.

Constitutional Principle
Multiple plausible explanations are preferable to one unsupported certainty.

5.8 Contradiction Impact on Confidence
Contradictions shall not automatically reduce confidence to zero.
Instead, they may:
reduce confidence, 
pause confidence growth, 
trigger re-evaluation, 
initiate manual review, 
create competing hypotheses, 
request additional evidence. 
The magnitude of confidence change shall remain proportionate to the significance of the contradiction.

5.9 Evidence Requests
Certain contradictions may justify requesting additional evidence.
Examples include:
New blood testing. 
Additional wearable data. 
Daily check-ins. 
Symptom questionnaire. 
Medication update. 
The Confidence Engine may recommend evidence collection.
It shall never require it.

Founder Principle
The best response to uncertainty is often better evidence.

5.10 Scientific Curiosity
Persistent contradictions may represent:
data quality issues, 
measurement limitations, 
previously unknown relationships, 
genuine biological individuality. 
The Confidence Engine shall preserve contradictory evidence for future longitudinal analysis rather than discarding it.
Contradictions may eventually become Discoveries.

Constitutional Principle
Today's contradiction may become tomorrow's discovery.

5.11 Explainability
Every contradiction assessment shall answer:
What evidence conflicts? 
Why is it considered contradictory? 
Which evidence is stronger? 
Which hypotheses currently exist? 
Why has confidence changed? 
What evidence would resolve the contradiction? 
Has this contradiction occurred previously? 

5.12 Engineering Constraints
The Contradiction Framework shall:
preserve all evidence, 
avoid premature conclusions, 
support competing hypotheses, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable, 
distinguish temporary from persistent contradiction, 
preserve scientific curiosity. 

5.13 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Contradictions are detected continuously.
✓ Different contradiction types are explicitly recognised.
✓ Contradictions reduce certainty without discarding evidence.
✓ Multiple competing hypotheses can coexist.
✓ Confidence adjusts proportionately to the significance of conflicting evidence.
✓ Additional evidence may be requested but never required.
✓ Contradictions remain explainable and auditable.
✓ Persistent contradictions can contribute to future discoveries.

8.2 Engineering Philosophy
Human understanding evolves.
Scientific understanding evolves.
BioSense should evolve in exactly the same way.
Every Health Belief should therefore be regarded as provisional.
No Health Belief shall be considered permanently correct.
Instead, each belief continuously competes against newly arriving evidence.
Understanding should improve over time through revision rather than replacement.

Founder Principle
BioSense should never defend an old belief against better evidence.

8.1a Ownership Boundary — Belief Revision
Belief revision is owned by the Dynamic Health Model (ENG-001, Belief Revision). This chapter defines the Confidence Engine's part in it, not a second revision engine. When the DHM revises a belief — refine, expand, narrow, merge, split, suspend, retire — it invokes the Confidence Engine to recompute confidence and to record the confidence history entry. The belief mutations themselves, Discovery evolution (8.8) and Biological-Identity evolution (8.9) are DHM operations that the Confidence Engine informs through confidence, and does not perform.
Engineering Rule
The DHM orchestrates belief revision and owns belief mutations, Discovery and Identity evolution. ENG-003 recomputes confidence during revision; it does not mutate beliefs.

Historical records remain permanently preserved.

Chapter 11

The purpose of this chapter is to define the operational safeguards that preserve the integrity of the Confidence Engine throughout the lifetime of the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
The Confidence Engine continuously influences the Dynamic Health Model.
Without appropriate safeguards, confidence may become progressively biased, self-reinforcing or detached from objective evidence.
This chapter establishes the constitutional mechanisms that prevent such behaviour.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence shall remain accountable to evidence throughout its entire lifecycle.

11.2 Engineering Philosophy
Every intelligent system develops assumptions.
Those assumptions become dangerous when they begin influencing future reasoning without continued evidential support.
BioSense shall therefore continuously distinguish between:
evidence, 
understanding, 
confidence, 
assumptions. 
Each layer shall remain independently verifiable.
Confidence shall never become its own source of evidence.

Founder Principle
Confidence should influence decisions. It should never become evidence itself.

11.3 Evidence Primacy
Objective observations shall always remain the highest authority within the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
If confidence conflicts with new objective evidence:
Objective evidence shall prevail.
The Confidence Engine shall immediately initiate structured belief review.
No confidence assessment may override validated observations.

Constitutional Principle
Evidence always outranks confidence.

11.4 Confirmation Bias Prevention
The Confidence Engine shall actively prevent confirmation bias.
New observations shall always be evaluated independently.
The engine shall not preferentially interpret evidence that supports existing Health Beliefs while discounting contradictory observations.
Evidence shall enter the evaluation process without prior confidence weighting.
Only after objective evaluation may confidence influence understanding.

Engineering Rule
Incoming evidence shall be evaluated before existing beliefs are considered.

11.5 Self-Reinforcement Prevention
Confidence shall never increase solely because it was previously high.
Example of prohibited behaviour:
High confidence
↓
Recommendation generated
↓
User follows recommendation
↓
Recommendation existence increases confidence
↓
Confidence grows further
This creates an artificial feedback loop.
Confidence may increase only through newly acquired objective evidence.

Founder Principle
Confidence should grow because reality supports it, not because BioSense repeats it.

11.6 Circular Reasoning Detection
The Confidence Engine shall continuously detect circular reasoning.
Examples include:
Health Belief
↓
Narrative
↓
Recommendation
↓
Recommendation cited as supporting evidence
↓
Health Belief strengthened
This is constitutionally prohibited.
Only observations entering through the Observation Engine may create new evidence.
Derived intelligence objects shall never become evidence.

Constitutional Principle
Derived understanding must never validate itself.

11.6a Relationship Between Integrity Mechanisms
Several safeguards address related risks and shall be understood as one layered scheme rather than duplicate mechanisms. Circular-dependency prevention (4.11 and 4.3a) prevents cycles in the graph; circular-reasoning detection (11.6) prevents a derived object from becoming evidence for an ancestor (a provenance rule, per 4.12a). Self-reinforcement (11.5) and confidence-inflation detection (11.7) are calibration monitors and are implemented within the Chapter 9 calibration loop, which is the single home for detecting systematic bias.
Engineering Rule
Graph-cycle prevention, evidence-provenance circularity, and inflation/self-reinforcement are distinct, named layers with defined relationships; inflation and self-reinforcement live in the Chapter 9 calibration monitors.
11.7 Confidence Inflation Detection
The Confidence Engine shall monitor for systematic confidence inflation.
Indicators may include:
unusually rapid confidence growth, 
excessive threshold crossings, 
persistent high-confidence assignments, 
reduced contradiction rates without evidential explanation, 
unusually low belief retirement. 
Detected inflation shall trigger calibration review.

Founder Principle
Confidence should become harder—not easier—to earn as understanding matures.

11.8 Hidden Assumption Monitoring
Every Health Belief contains implicit assumptions.
Examples include:
Sufficient wearable coverage.
Representative laboratory history.
Stable lifestyle.
Accurate manual reporting.
These assumptions shall remain explicit within the Confidence Engine.
Where assumptions become invalid:
Confidence shall be re-evaluated.

11.9 Novelty Protection
Unexpected evidence shall not be rejected merely because it conflicts with established understanding.
Instead:
Novel evidence shall:
undergo additional evaluation, 
remain preserved, 
contribute to competing hypotheses where appropriate, 
support future Discovery formation. 
Novelty shall increase scrutiny.
Not automatic rejection.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense should remain sceptical of novelty without becoming resistant to it.

11.10 Recommendation Independence
The Recommendation Engine shall consume confidence.
It shall never modify confidence.
Recommendation performance shall not directly alter confidence.
Example:
A user follows a recommendation.
↓
Outcome improves.
↓
New observations demonstrate improvement.
↓
Confidence increases.
Not:
Recommendation issued
↓
Confidence increases.
This preserves causal integrity.

11.11 Human Oversight
Certain confidence behaviours shall support optional expert review.
Examples include:

Repeated belief reversals.
Unexpected calibration drift.
Novel Discovery formation.
Large-scale confidence recalibration.
Human oversight shall:
Review.
Validate.
Refine.
It shall not replace evidence-based confidence formation.

Founder Principle
Human expertise strengthens governance, not day-to-day reasoning.

11.12 Engine Isolation
The Confidence Engine shall remain operationally independent from:
Recommendation Engine.
Conversation Engine.
User Interface.
Notification System.
Marketing systems.
Commercial incentives.
Confidence formation shall never be influenced by commercial objectives or engagement optimisation.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence exists to reflect evidence, not to maximise engagement.

11.13 Integrity Monitoring
Operational integrity monitoring shall continuously evaluate:
Confidence distribution.
Propagation behaviour.
Revision frequency.
Calibration stability.
Bias indicators.
Engine performance.
Unexpected behavioural drift.
Monitoring supports early identification of systemic issues.

11.14 Engineering Constraints
Operational safeguards shall:
preserve evidence primacy, 
prevent self-reinforcing confidence, 
prevent circular reasoning, 
detect confidence inflation, 
preserve novelty, 
maintain engine independence, 
remain fully auditable, 
support future safeguard mechanisms. 

11.15 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:

Chapter 13
End-to-End Confidence Lifecycle

13.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to demonstrate the complete operational lifecycle of confidence within the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
Previous chapters define the architecture governing evidence evaluation, confidence formation, propagation, calibration and governance.
This chapter illustrates how those components interact during a realistic longitudinal health journey.
The objective is to demonstrate that the Confidence Engine behaves as a coherent, explainable and scientifically accountable system.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence is earned continuously throughout the lifetime of evidence, not at the moment evidence arrives.

13.2 Introducing the User
For the purposes of this example we introduce a fictional BioSense user.
Name: Alex Carter
Age: 36
Alex has used BioSense consistently for eighteen months.
The Dynamic Health Model currently contains:
68 active Health Beliefs 
11 validated Discoveries 
4 Behavioural Identity traits 
3 Biological Identity characteristics 
The Observation Store contains:
approximately 1.3 million observations, 
complete wearable history, 
three annual blood panels, 
continuous daily check-ins. 
All confidence assessments are currently stable.

13.3 New Evidence Arrives
Alex uploads a new annual blood panel.
The Observation Engine (ENG-002):
authenticates the source, 
validates the observations, 
canonicalises each biomarker, 
generates Quality Profiles, 
stores forty-three new Canonical Observations. 
The Observation Store publishes an internal notification.
The Dynamic Health Model becomes aware that new evidence exists.
At this stage:
No confidence has changed.
Only evidence has changed.

Constitutional Principle
New evidence creates the opportunity for confidence. It does not create confidence itself.

13.4 Confidence Candidate Formation
The Confidence Engine evaluates every Health Belief potentially affected by the new observations.
Examples include:
Iron Status
Recovery Capacity
Inflammatory Activity
Exercise Adaptation
Metabolic Health
Each affected understanding generates a new Confidence Candidate.
The candidates contain:
relevant observations, 
evidence domains, 
supporting history, 
contradictory evidence, 
current confidence, 
proposed confidence. 
Nothing has yet been accepted.

13.5 Evidence Evaluation
Each Confidence Candidate undergoes structured evaluation.
The engine assesses:
Evidence Quality.
Evidence Quantity.
Evidence Consistency.
Longitudinal Stability.
Evidence Diversity.
Temporal Relevance.
Evidence Independence.
Scientific Alignment.
Each domain contributes independently.
The engine records both supporting and limiting factors.

13.6 Contradiction Assessment
During evaluation, the engine detects an unexpected finding.
Ferritin has improved considerably.
However:
Alex's reported fatigue has increased.
WHOOP Recovery has declined.
This contradiction is classified as:
Physiological Contradiction.
Rather than rejecting either explanation, the engine creates competing hypotheses.
Hypothesis A:
Recovery decline primarily reflects increased training load.
Hypothesis B:
Recovery decline reflects early illness.
Hypothesis C:
Recovery decline reflects factors unrelated to iron status.
Confidence is temporarily moderated.

Founder Principle
Conflicting evidence should create curiosity before certainty.

13.7 Confidence Formation
Following domain evaluation and contradiction analysis, the Confidence Engine balances all available evidence.
The revised Iron Status Health Belief demonstrates:
High-quality laboratory evidence.
Three consecutive annual improvements.
Strong longitudinal consistency.
Independent confirmation through symptom improvement over the preceding year.
The belief progresses from:
Developing
↓
Established
A complete Confidence Explanation Object is generated.

13.8 Confidence Propagation
The updated Iron Status Health Belief influences related understandings.
Examples include:
Exercise Capacity.
↓
Recovery Narrative.
↓
Biological Identity.
↓
Future Recommendations.
Propagation attenuates naturally.
No downstream confidence exceeds the confidence of its originating evidence.
All propagated changes remain explainable.

13.9 Decision Gates
The revised confidence reaches several operational thresholds.
The Confidence Engine authorises:
Updated Health Belief.
Updated Narrative.
Updated Recommendation.
However:
The threshold for a validated Discovery is not reached.
BioSense deliberately withholds promotion.
The platform continues monitoring.

Constitutional Principle
Not every improvement in confidence justifies a new conclusion.

13.10 User Communication
The Conversation Engine requests an explanation.
The Confidence Engine provides the structured Explanation Object.
The Conversation Engine translates this into user-friendly language.
Example:
"Over the past three annual blood tests, your iron status has shown consistent improvement. This conclusion is supported by high-quality laboratory evidence collected over a prolonged period. Confidence has increased because the improvement has remained stable over time, although we continue to monitor your recovery as recent wearable data suggests additional factors may also be influencing your performance."
The explanation reflects the underlying reasoning exactly.
It introduces no new conclusions.

Founder Principle
The Conversation Engine communicates confidence. It does not create confidence.

13.14 Confidence Integrity
Throughout Alex's journey, the Confidence Engine continuously protects itself.

Closing Statement
The Confidence Engine is not a scoring system.
It is not a probability calculator.
It is not a statistical confidence estimator.
It is the constitutional authority governing how BioSense earns, maintains, questions and revises its own understanding.
Every confidence assessment begins with evidence.
Every confidence assessment is evaluated transparently.
Every confidence assessment remains open to revision.
Every confidence assessment preserves its own history.
Every confidence assessment can explain itself.
Every confidence assessment acknowledges uncertainty where uncertainty remains.
Through this architecture, BioSense does not seek to appear certain.
It seeks to become appropriately certain as evidence accumulates.
The Confidence Engine therefore transforms observations into trusted understanding while preserving the intellectual humility required of any system operating within the complex domain of human health.
