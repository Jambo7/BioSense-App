Chapter 1
Purpose & Design Philosophy

1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the architecture governing how BioSense converts trusted understanding into safe, prioritised and personalised health decisions.
The Decision Intelligence & Intervention Engine is responsible for determining whether BioSense should act, what action should be taken, when that action should occur and how it should be communicated.
Unlike the Dynamic Health Model, which develops understanding, or the Confidence Engine, which determines the reliability of that understanding, the Decision Intelligence Engine determines the most appropriate course of action for the individual.
Its responsibility is not to maximise the number of recommendations.
Its responsibility is to maximise meaningful health improvement while minimising unnecessary intervention.

Constitutional Principle
The purpose of intelligence is not merely to understand. It is to improve outcomes through appropriate action.

1.2 Scope
This specification defines:
Decision Intelligence 
Intervention selection 
Recommendation generation 
Recommendation prioritisation 
Intervention timing 
Intervention intensity 
Competing intervention resolution 
Expected Health Impact evaluation 
Outcome monitoring 
Recommendation retirement 
Safety controls 
Intervention governance 
This specification deliberately excludes:
Observation ingestion (ENG-002) 
Dynamic Health Model architecture (ENG-001) 
Confidence calculation (ENG-003) 
User conversation generation 
Notification delivery 
User interface design 
Those responsibilities are defined elsewhere within the BioSense engineering library.

1.3 Authority
This specification operates under the constitutional hierarchy:
BioSense Constitution
↓
ENG-001 — Dynamic Health Model
↓
ENG-002 — Observation Engine
↓
ENG-003 — Confidence Engine
↓
ENG-004 — Decision Intelligence Engine
The Decision Intelligence Engine consumes understanding and confidence.
It does not create either.

Engineering Rule
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall never modify observations, Health Beliefs or confidence directly.
It consumes the outputs of previous engines and produces decision objects for downstream systems.

1.4 Engineering Philosophy
The presence of a Health Belief does not automatically justify intervention.
Likewise, high confidence does not necessarily require a recommendation.
BioSense shall first determine whether intervention is beneficial, proportionate and likely to improve the individual's long-term health.
In some circumstances, the most appropriate decision is to continue observing.
In others, it may be to educate rather than advise.
In others, it may be to recommend seeking professional medical advice.
The Decision Intelligence Engine therefore exists to optimise action, not activity.

Founder Principle
The best recommendation is sometimes no recommendation at all.

1.5 Constitutional Definition of Decision Intelligence
For engineering purposes:
Decision Intelligence is the structured process by which BioSense transforms trusted understanding into prioritised, evidence-based interventions designed to improve long-term health outcomes.
Decision Intelligence therefore considers:
evidence, 
confidence, 
expected health impact, 
user context, 
intervention burden, 
competing priorities, 
uncertainty, 
constitutional safety principles. 
It does not optimise for engagement, notification frequency or application usage.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense should optimise for better health, not more interactions.

1.6 Action Is Not Recommendation
A key architectural distinction exists between a decision and a recommendation.
A decision is the internal conclusion reached by the Decision Intelligence Engine.
A recommendation is only one possible expression of that decision.
Possible decision outcomes include:
No action 
Continue monitoring 
Collect additional evidence 
Provide education 
Generate a lifestyle recommendation 
Suggest behavioural change 
Recommend discussion with a healthcare professional 
Escalate through constitutional safety pathways 
The Conversation Engine and User Interface determine how these decisions are presented.
They do not determine which decisions are made.

Engineering Rule
Every recommendation originates from a decision, but not every decision results in a recommendation.

1.7 Outcome Optimisation
The objective of the Decision Intelligence Engine is not to maximise the number of interventions.
Its objective is to maximise meaningful improvements in long-term health.
Every decision shall therefore be evaluated according to its expected contribution to:
healthspan, 
longevity, 
disease prevention, 
behavioural sustainability, 
user wellbeing, 
constitutional safety. 

Founder Principle
Measure success by better health, not by completed recommendations.

1.8 Explainability
Every decision generated by the engine shall remain explainable.
BioSense shall always be capable of answering:
Why was this action selected? 
Why was another action not selected? 
Why is this the highest priority? 
Why now? 
Why not later? 
Why is no action recommended? 
If these questions cannot be answered, the decision is constitutionally incomplete.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention should be as explainable as the understanding that created it.

1.9 Expected Health Impact
Decision prioritisation shall be driven primarily by Expected Health Impact, not by the number of abnormalities detected.
Expected Health Impact represents BioSense's estimate of the potential long-term improvement associated with a successful intervention.
Factors contributing to Expected Health Impact may include:
strength of supporting evidence, 
confidence, 
magnitude of potential benefit, 
sustainability, 
interaction with other Health Beliefs, 
relevance to the individual's goals, 
constitutional safety. 
Expected Health Impact shall be formally defined in later chapters.

Founder Principle
Address what matters most, not what is easiest to detect.

On Acceptance Criteria
Acceptance criteria throughout this specification shall be objective, automatable assertions rather than aspirations. Each should be restated verifiably — for example: 're-running prioritisation at pinned engine and configuration versions reproduces the same ranking'; 'an intervention exceeding constitutional authority is blocked deterministically'; 'an intervention outcome never becomes supporting evidence (8.8)'; 'every Decision Object resolves to a reproducibility manifest (11.4a)'.
Engineering Rule
A criterion that cannot be checked by a test or a defined inspection is not an acceptance criterion. This applies to every chapter's acceptance section.
1.10 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Decision Intelligence is clearly separated from understanding and confidence.
✓ Recommendations are recognised as only one possible decision outcome.
✓ The engine consumes previous intelligence without modifying it.
✓ Decisions optimise long-term health rather than engagement.
✓ Expected Health Impact is established as the primary prioritisation philosophy.
✓ Every decision remains explainable.
✓ The architecture clearly positions the Decision Intelligence Engine within the wider BioSense Intelligence Platform.
Chapter 2
Decision Formation Architecture

2.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the constitutional workflow through which the Decision Intelligence Engine transforms trusted understanding into structured intervention decisions.
Every decision generated by BioSense shall follow a common architectural process.
The objective is to ensure that interventions remain:
evidence-based, 
confidence-aware, 
proportionate, 
explainable, 
reproducible, 
constitutionally compliant. 
No intervention shall bypass this workflow.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention shall be earned through structured decision-making rather than generated by intuition or heuristics.

2.2 Engineering Philosophy
Decision-making represents the transition between intelligence and action.
By the time the Decision Intelligence Engine begins processing:
observations already exist, 
Health Beliefs already exist, 
confidence has already been evaluated. 
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore avoid re-evaluating evidence.
Instead, it consumes trusted understanding and determines the most appropriate response.
This separation preserves clear architectural boundaries between engines.

Engineering Rule
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall consume intelligence.
It shall not recreate intelligence.

2.3 Decision Inputs
Every decision shall be formed from structured inputs supplied by upstream engines.
These include:
From ENG-001
Health Beliefs 
Discoveries 
Biological Identity 
Behavioural Identity 
User Goals 
Narratives 

From ENG-002
Supporting Observation References 
Observation Quality metadata 
Evidence provenance 

From ENG-003
Confidence assessment 
Confidence threshold 
Explanation Object 
Remaining uncertainty 
Competing hypotheses 

Additional contextual inputs may include:
User preferences 
User availability 
Intervention history 
Active recommendations 
Safety constraints 
The Decision Engine shall not independently generate these inputs.

Constitutional Principle
Decisions should be built upon trusted intelligence, not reconstructed intelligence.

2.3a Engine Interface Contracts
The inputs listed above are enumerated but not contracted. For each upstream engine the interface shall specify what is read, that it is read strictly read-only, and the versions it is pinned to. In particular, the Decision Intelligence Engine consumes confidence from ENG-003 and beliefs from ENG-001 without modifying them (ENG-003 states that the recommendation layer consumes confidence and never modifies it), and it reads observations and quality from ENG-002 by reference.
Every concept consumed here shall be defined in its owning specification. 'Behavioural Identity' and 'User Goals' are consumed from ENG-001 and shall either be defined there or added to ENG-001; they shall not be assumed to exist. The provenance of contextual inputs (user availability, active interventions, safety constraints) shall likewise be named.
Engineering Rule
Every upstream input is read-only, version-pinned, and defined in its owning specification. The Decision Engine consumes intelligence; it never writes upstream engines.
2.4 Decision Candidate
Every potential intervention shall begin life as a Decision Candidate.
A Decision Candidate represents a proposed course of action.
It has not yet been approved.
It shall contain:
Candidate ID 
Target Health Belief(s) 
Supporting confidence 
Supporting evidence references 
Proposed intervention 
Expected Health Impact 
Safety assessment 
Decision timestamp 
Engine version 
Decision Candidates remain internal objects until validated.

Engineering Rule
Every intervention shall begin as a Decision Candidate.

2.5 Decision Formation Workflow
Every Decision Candidate shall progress through the same constitutional workflow.
Trusted Understanding

↓

Relevant Health Beliefs Identified

↓

Decision Candidates Generated

↓

Eligibility Assessment

↓

Expected Health Impact Assessment

↓

Conflict Resolution

↓

Safety Evaluation

↓

Priority Assessment

↓

Decision Validation

↓

Approved Decision Object
Each stage shall remain independently explainable.

2.6 Decision Objects
Once validated, a Decision Candidate becomes a Decision Object.
Decision Objects represent the constitutional source of truth for all downstream actions.
A Decision Object may ultimately result in:
no action, 
continued monitoring, 
evidence collection, 
educational guidance, 
lifestyle intervention, 
behavioural coaching, 
referral advice, 
future observation scheduling, 
recommendation generation. 
Every downstream engine consumes Decision Objects.
No downstream engine creates them.

Founder Principle
Recommendations are outputs of Decision Objects, not substitutes for them.

2.6a Decision Object Storage & Ownership
Decision Objects are the source of truth for downstream actions, so their store shall be owned unambiguously. The Decision Intelligence Engine owns the Decision store; ENG-001's Recommendation History and a belief's linked recommendations reference Decision Objects by identifier and version rather than duplicating them. Any write into the Dynamic Health Model that results from a decision (for example scheduling a future observation) goes through the DHM's per-individual single-writer path (ENG-001), never by the Decision Engine writing the DHM directly.
Engineering Rule
ENG-004 owns the Decision store; the DHM references decisions by id and version. Decision-driven DHM writes use the DHM single-writer path.
2.7 Separation of Decision and Communication
Decision formation shall remain entirely independent from communication.
Example:
Decision:
Collect additional blood biomarkers.
Communication:
"To improve our understanding of your iron status, BioSense recommends repeating your blood test in approximately three months."
The Conversation Engine determines wording.
The Decision Intelligence Engine determines intent.

Constitutional Principle
The meaning of a decision should never depend upon how it is communicated.

2.8 Decision States
Every Decision Object shall progress through recognised lifecycle states.
Suggested states include:
Candidate
Awaiting evaluation.

Eligible
Suitable for further assessment.

Validated
Passes constitutional checks.

Approved
Ready for downstream consumption.

Deferred
Temporarily postponed.

Active
Currently influencing the user experience.

Completed
Intervention finished.

Retired
No longer applicable.

Founder Principle
Decisions should mature through recognised stages rather than appear instantaneously.

2.8a Enumerations Are Closed and Normative
Where this specification lists 'suggested' states, levels, groups or categories that gate behaviour — Decision States (2.8), Priority Groups (4.9), Timing States (5.4), Intensity levels (6.3), intervention categories (3.3), Outcome Categories (8.4) and lifecycle states (9.3) — those sets shall be closed, versioned enumerations, marked normative rather than illustrative, and extended only through governed addition. A normative glossary shall also fix the distinct meanings of 'Decision', 'Recommendation', 'Intervention' and 'Decision Object', which are currently used with overlapping sense.
Engineering Rule
Behaviour-gating vocabularies are closed, versioned and normative. Illustrative examples are marked as such.
2.9 Decision Independence
Every Decision Object shall remain independent.
Multiple decisions may originate from:
one Health Belief, 
multiple Health Beliefs, 
one Discovery, 
several Discoveries. 
Similarly:
One Health Belief may generate no decisions whatsoever.
Decision generation shall remain evidence-driven rather than rule-driven.

2.10 Decision Lifecycle
Decision Objects shall evolve in response to the triggers listed below — not by constant recomputation. 'Continuous' means triggered plus scheduled review plus lazy evaluation on read, consistent with ENG-001's trigger model and ENG-003.
Triggers include:
confidence revision, 
new observations, 
completed interventions, 
changing user goals, 
emerging discoveries, 
safety reassessment, 
competing recommendations. 
Historical Decision Objects shall remain preserved.
New decisions shall supersede rather than overwrite previous ones.

Constitutional Principle
Every decision should have a history.

2.11 Decision Validation
Before approval, every Decision Object shall satisfy constitutional validation.
Validation confirms:
sufficient confidence, 
sufficient evidence,

Chapter 3
Intervention Classification & Action Taxonomy

3.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the constitutional classification framework used by the Decision Intelligence Engine to categorise every intervention generated by BioSense.
Not all health decisions are equivalent.
Different interventions possess different objectives, evidence requirements, safety implications and communication styles.
By classifying interventions into clearly defined categories, BioSense ensures that every action is governed consistently throughout the platform.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention should belong to a clearly defined constitutional category before it is delivered.

3.2 Engineering Philosophy
The objective of BioSense is not simply to generate recommendations.
Its objective is to determine the most appropriate intervention for the individual's current situation.
That intervention may involve:
providing knowledge, 
collecting additional evidence, 
encouraging behavioural change, 
suggesting professional medical advice, 
or intentionally taking no action. 
The classification of an intervention influences:
eligibility, 
prioritisation, 
confidence requirements, 
communication, 
outcome monitoring, 
retirement. 

Founder Principle
Choosing the right type of intervention is as important as choosing the right intervention.

3.3 Constitutional Intervention Categories
Every Decision Object shall belong to one primary intervention category.
The following categories are constitutionally recognised.

Category One — No Action
The Decision Intelligence Engine determines that no intervention is currently appropriate.
Reasons may include:
insufficient confidence, 
low expected health impact, 
temporary physiological variation, 
adequate current management, 
conflicting evidence, 
ongoing observation. 
No Action remains an active decision.
It is not the absence of decision-making.

Constitutional Principle
Choosing not to intervene is itself an intelligent intervention.

Category Two — Continued Observation
BioSense continues monitoring without recommending behavioural change.
Examples include:
emerging trends, 
incomplete longitudinal evidence, 
unresolved competing hypotheses, 
ongoing confidence maturation. 
Observation allows understanding to develop before action is taken.

Category Three — Evidence Collection
Additional evidence is likely to improve BioSense's understanding.
Examples include:
repeat blood testing, 
wearable reconnection, 
symptom questionnaire, 
nutrition logging, 
medication update, 
body composition assessment. 
The purpose is to reduce uncertainty.
Not to increase engagement.

Founder Principle
The most valuable intervention is sometimes better evidence.

Category Four — Education
The user would benefit from additional knowledge without requiring immediate behavioural change.
Examples include:
biomarker education, 
sleep physiology, 
recovery science, 
nutrition fundamentals, 
exercise principles, 
longevity education. 
Educational interventions improve understanding rather than directly altering behaviour.

Category Five — Behavioural Intervention
The engine recommends modification of an established behaviour.
Examples include:
bedtime consistency, 
hydration, 
alcohol reduction, 
stress management, 
meal timing, 
screen exposure. 
Behavioural interventions focus upon habits rather than isolated actions.

Constitutional Principle
Sustainable behaviour change should be preferred over isolated actions.

Category Six — Lifestyle Optimisation
Recommendations intended to improve long-term healthspan.
Examples include:
physical activity, 
nutrition quality, 
strength training, 
recovery routines, 
cardiovascular fitness, 
social wellbeing. 
Lifestyle optimisation generally influences multiple Health Beliefs simultaneously.

Category Seven — Nutritional Intervention
Recommendations related specifically to dietary intake.
Examples include:
increasing dietary fibre, 
improving protein intake, 
reducing ultra-processed foods, 
increasing omega-3 consumption, 
improving hydration. 
Supplement recommendations remain governed separately.

Category Eight — Supplement Guidance
BioSense may suggest consideration of supplements where constitutionally appropriate.
Examples include:
Vitamin D, 
Iron, 
Magnesium, 
Omega-3. 
Supplement guidance shall remain:
evidence-based, 
proportionate, 
confidence-aware, 
constitutionally cautious. 
It shall always prioritise nutritional improvement where appropriate.

Founder Principle
Food should generally be considered before supplementation.

Category Nine — Medical Discussion
BioSense recommends discussing findings with a suitably qualified healthcare professional.
Examples include:
persistent abnormal biomarkers, 
unexpected physiological changes, 
concerning longitudinal deterioration, 
red-flag findings defined within the Constitution. 
BioSense does not diagnose.
It facilitates appropriate escalation.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense supports clinical care. It does not replace it.

Category Ten — Safety Escalation
Certain situations require accelerated constitutional handling.
Examples include:
red-flag biomarker combinations, 
significant physiological deterioration, 
repeated constitutional safety triggers. 
Safety Escalation is a request signal only: it raises a safety-relevant escalation request to ENG-010, which computes the final Escalation Level and publication response. ENG-004 activates the pathway; it does not determine the escalation level.

Category Eleven — Goal Optimisation
Interventions designed specifically to improve progress towards user-defined goals.
Examples include:
marathon preparation, 
weight reduction, 
muscle gain, 
healthy ageing, 
improved sleep. 
Goal Optimisation shall remain aligned with overall health priorities.
Goal achievement shall never compromise constitutional safety.

Founder Principle
Goals should shape recommendations without compromising health.

3.4 Multi-Category Interventions
Certain Decision Objects may legitimately belong to multiple categories.
Example:
Poor ferritin.
↓
Education.
↓
Nutrition.
↓
Repeat blood test.
↓
Medical discussion.
Each component shall remain independently identifiable.
The intervention remains one coordinated strategy.

3.5 Intervention Hierarchy
Categories differ along two independent axes that shall not be conflated: intervention magnitude (how much is being asked of the individual, from No Action upward) and safety urgency (how time-critical the situation is). See 3.5a.
Conceptually:
No Action

↓

Observation

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Education

↓

Behavioural Intervention

↓

Lifestyle Optimisation

↓

Nutritional Intervention

↓

Supplement Guidance

↓

Medical Discussion

↓

Safety Escalation
Movement towards higher-impact categories generally requires increasing confidence and stronger evidence.

Engineering Rule
Higher-consequence interventions shall require proportionately stronger constitutional justification.

3.5a Two Axes — Intervention Magnitude and Safety Urgency
This chapter is the engineering realisation of the recommendation hierarchy defined in the Constitution (Volume 1A) and the least-necessary-intervention rule in ENG-001; it does not introduce a second, divergent hierarchy, and the eleven categories map to the constitutional set.
Two axes shall be kept separate. The intervention-magnitude axis expresses least-necessary-intervention: prefer the lowest-magnitude sufficient response (No Action, Observation, Evidence Collection, Education, then behavioural and lifestyle categories). The safety-urgency axis is orthogonal: Medical Discussion and Safety Escalation are not 'more intervention' than a supplement — they are time-critical responses that override the magnitude ordering via the safety floor (10.4a), regardless of tier.
Engineering Rule
Prefer the least-magnitude sufficient intervention; safety urgency is a separate axis that overrides magnitude through the safety floor. The two are never collapsed into one ranking.
3.6 Category Evolution
Decision Objects may transition between categories.
Example:

↓
Evidence Collection
↓
Education
↓
Behavioural Intervention
↓
Lifestyle Optimisation
As BioSense's understanding matures, intervention type may evolve naturally.
Historical classifications shall remain preserved.

3.7 Explainability
Every intervention shall explain:
Why was this category selected? 
Why were alternative categories rejected? 
Which confidence threshold authorised this category? 
Which Health Beliefs contributed? 
Which safety principles apply? 
Could the category change with additional evidence? 

3.8 Engineering Constraints
The Intervention Classification Framework shall:
classify every Decision Object, 
support multiple intervention categories, 
preserve constitutional safety, 
remain explainable, 
remain extensible, 
remain fully auditable, 
support future intervention types. 

3.9 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every Decision Object belongs to at least one constitutional intervention category.
✓ "No Action" is recognised as an explicit intervention outcome.
✓ Educational, behavioural, nutritional, lifestyle and medical interventions remain distinct.
✓ Multi-category intervention strategies are supported.
✓ Higher-consequence interventions require stronger justification.
✓ Intervention categories evolve without losing historical lineage.
✓ Every intervention classification remains fully explainable and auditable.
Chapter 4
Decision Eligibility & Intervention Prioritisation

4.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Decision Intelligence Engine determines which interventions are eligible for consideration and how eligible interventions are prioritised before being presented to the user.
The existence of a valid Decision Object does not guarantee that it should become an active intervention.
BioSense may possess numerous appropriate interventions simultaneously.
Its responsibility is to identify those expected to produce the greatest meaningful improvement while avoiding unnecessary cognitive burden.

Constitutional Principle
The value of an intervention depends not only on its correctness, but on whether it deserves the user's attention today.

4.2 Engineering Philosophy
Health optimisation is constrained by human attention.
Even highly motivated individuals can realistically sustain only a limited number of meaningful behavioural changes at any given time.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore prioritise interventions according to their expected contribution to long-term health rather than attempting to optimise every measurable variable simultaneously.
The objective is not completeness.
The objective is meaningful progress.

Founder Principle
Improve the person, not every metric.

4.3 Eligibility Assessment
Before an intervention enters the prioritisation process it shall undergo Eligibility Assessment.
Eligibility confirms that the intervention satisfies all constitutional requirements.
Assessment shall consider:
supporting confidence, 
constitutional safety, 
intervention category, 
expected health impact, 
conflicting interventions, 
user context, 
active intervention history. 
Only eligible Decision Objects may proceed to prioritisation.

Engineering Rule
Eligibility determines whether an intervention may compete for attention.
It does not determine whether it will be selected.

4.4 Prioritisation Philosophy
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall rank interventions according to their Expected Health Impact.
Expected Health Impact represents the estimated long-term benefit likely to result if the intervention is successfully adopted.
The engine shall not prioritise interventions based solely upon:
abnormal laboratory values, 
confidence, 
novelty, 
recent observations, 
ease of implementation. 
Expected Health Impact shall consider the individual's health as an integrated system.

Constitutional Principle
BioSense should prioritise interventions by expected improvement in health, not by the severity of isolated measurements.

4.5 Expected Health Impact
Expected Health Impact is a constitutional property of every Decision Object.
It represents BioSense's best estimate of the overall value of the proposed intervention.
Factors influencing Expected Health Impact may include:
strength of supporting evidence, 
confidence, 
number of Health Beliefs affected, 
anticipated longevity benefit, 
disease prevention potential, 
behavioural sustainability, 
interaction with existing interventions, 
alignment with user goals, 
constitutional safety. 
Expected Health Impact shall remain explainable.
Expected Health Impact shall not be a single opaque number, but it shall be a structured, decomposable and comparable score: explainable in its parts (see 4.5a) yet ordered enough to rank interventions. 'Explainable, not a black box' and 'rankable' are reconciled by such a structured score, not by refusing to define one.

Founder Principle
Prioritise what changes lives, not what changes dashboards.

4.5a Expected Health Impact Contract & Calibration
Expected Health Impact is the quantity by which every intervention is ranked, so it is defined here rather than deferred. It shall be produced by a single versioned methodology owned by the data and clinical function that combines its stated factors (evidence strength, confidence, magnitude and durability of benefit, disease-prevention potential, breadth across Health Beliefs, behavioural sustainability, goal alignment and constitutional safety) into a structured, decomposable and comparable score. The score is explainable in its components and ordered enough to rank; it is not a single opaque number, and it is not left undefined.
Because Expected Health Impact is a prediction of future benefit, it shall be calibrated: the Outcome Evaluation loop (Chapter 8) compares predicted impact against observed outcomes and the methodology is adjusted under governance (8.9a), exactly as ENG-003 calibrates confidence. Weights and thresholds are to be confirmed; the contract is binding now.
Engineering Rule
Expected Health Impact is one versioned, explainable, comparable score, and it is calibrated against actual outcomes. Prioritisation ranks on it; it is never left undefined.
4.6 Intervention Burden
Not all beneficial interventions carry the same cost to the individual.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall consider Intervention Burden.
Examples include:
Low burden:
increasing hydration, 
adjusting bedtime, 
short educational content. 
Moderate burden:
structured exercise programme, 
nutrition planning, 
alcohol reduction. 
High burden:
significant lifestyle restructuring, 
repeated laboratory testing, 
major behavioural change. 
High Expected Health Impact may justify higher Intervention Burden.
Minor Expected Health Impact generally shall not.

Constitutional Principle
The burden of an intervention should be proportionate to its expected benefit.

4.7 Behavioural Capacity
BioSense shall recognise that users possess finite behavioural capacity.
The Decision Intelligence Engine should avoid presenting more concurrent interventions than the individual can reasonably sustain.
Factors may include:
existing active interventions, 
historical adherence, 
life circumstances, 
current stress, 
illness, 
travel, 
major life events. 
Behavioural capacity influences prioritisation.
It does not determine medical importance.

Founder Principle
Sustainable progress beats ambitious overload.

4.7a Burden, Behavioural Capacity and Intensity as Defined Models
Intervention Burden (4.6), Behavioural Capacity (4.7) and Intervention Intensity (6.4) each shape what the individual is asked to do and shall be defined, versioned models — inputs, combination and output scale — owned by the data and clinical function, not qualitative judgement. 'Low/Moderate/High' shall be the output of a defined model, not an example.
The inputs to Behavioural Capacity (current stress, illness, travel, major life events) are sensitive and shall have a named provenance — declared by the user or inferred with an attached confidence — and shall be handled within the privacy boundary of Document 1C. Inferred capacity errs toward fewer concurrent interventions, never more.
Engineering Rule
Burden, capacity and intensity are defined versioned models with named, privacy-governed inputs. Inference is conservative.
4.8 Interaction Between Interventions
Interventions rarely exist independently.
Some interventions reinforce one another.
Examples:
Improved sleep
↓
Better recovery
↓
Improved exercise consistency
↓
Improved metabolic health.
Others compete.
Examples:
Increase calorie intake.
↓
Weight-loss goal.
Or:
Increase training intensity.
↓
Recovery deficit.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall recognise both synergistic and competing interventions before determining priority.

Constitutional Principle
The best intervention strategy is often a coordinated sequence rather than a collection of isolated recommendations.

4.9 Priority Groups
Eligible interventions shall be organised into priority groups.
Suggested groups include:
Immediate Priority
Expected Health Impact is substantial.
Timely intervention is important.

Near-Term Priority
Intervention is valuable but not immediately time-sensitive.

Development Priority
Intervention should become active after higher-priority work is established.

Monitoring Priority
Continue observing.
No immediate behavioural change.

Deferred
Constitutionally appropriate but intentionally postponed.

Priority Groups represent planning rather than urgency alone.

4.10 Limiting Active Interventions
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall intentionally limit the number of concurrently active interventions.
The maximum number shall remain configurable.
Selection shall favour:
highest Expected Health Impact, 
behavioural sustainability, 
complementary interventions, 
constitutional safety. 
Remaining interventions remain preserved as Decision Objects for future reassessment.

Founder Principle
Helping someone do three important things is better than asking them to do twenty.

4.11 Re-Prioritisation
Intervention priority shall remain dynamic.
Re-prioritisation may occur following:
new observations, 
confidence changes, 
successful intervention completion, 
changing user goals, 
changing behavioural capacity, 
illness, 
travel, 
significant life events. 
Priority shall therefore evolve alongside the individual.

4.12 Explainability
Every prioritisation decision shall answer:
Why was this intervention selected? 
Why is it ranked above other interventions? 
Which interventions were deferred? 
What expected health impact justified this ranking? 
Which competing interventions were considered? 
What future events could alter this priority? 

4.13 Engineering Constraints
The Prioritisation Framework shall:
evaluate eligibility before prioritisation, 
optimise Expected Health Impact, 
consider behavioural capacity, 
minimise intervention burden, 
support coordinated intervention strategies, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable. 

4.14 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every intervention undergoes Eligibility Assessment before prioritisation.
✓ Expected Health Impact becomes the primary prioritisation principle.
✓ Intervention Burden and Behavioural Capacity are explicitly considered.
✓ Synergistic and competing interventions influence ranking.
✓ Active interventions remain intentionally limited.
✓ Deferred interventions remain preserved for future review.
✓ Every prioritisation decision remains explainable and auditable.
Chapter 5
Intervention Timing & Decision Scheduling

5.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Decision Intelligence Engine determines the optimal timing for interventions.
The effectiveness of an intervention depends not only upon its appropriateness, but also upon when it is introduced.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore determine:
whether intervention should occur, 
when intervention should occur, 
whether intervention should be delayed, 
whether intervention should be repeated, 
whether intervention should expire. 
Timing is treated as an integral component of decision intelligence rather than a communication concern.

Constitutional Principle
The right intervention delivered at the wrong time may become the wrong intervention.

5.2 Engineering Philosophy
Health exists within time.
Recovery occurs over days.
Behaviour changes over weeks.
Physiology changes over months.
Biological Identity evolves over years.
Interventions shall therefore align with the natural timescale of the physiological process they are intended to influence.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall optimise intervention timing to maximise the probability of meaningful behavioural adoption and positive health outcomes.

Founder Principle
Decision quality includes deciding when not to act.

5.3 Timing Inputs
Intervention timing shall consider:
current Health Beliefs, 
confidence, 
Expected Health Impact, 
intervention priority, 
behavioural capacity, 
physiological state, 
recent intervention history, 
user goals, 
ongoing interventions, 
safety considerations. 
Timing decisions shall consume upstream intelligence.
They shall not recreate it.

Engineering Rule
Timing shall be derived from trusted understanding rather than raw observations.

5.4 Timing States
Every Decision Object shall possess a Timing State.
Suggested Timing States include:
Immediate
The intervention should begin without unnecessary delay.

Near-Term
The intervention should begin soon but is not urgent.

Scheduled
The intervention should occur at a defined future point.

Deferred
The intervention remains appropriate but should be postponed.

Monitoring
Continue observation before intervention.

Expired
The opportunity for intervention has passed.

Retired
The intervention is no longer appropriate.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention should know not only what it is, but when it belongs.

5.5 Readiness Assessment
Before scheduling an intervention, the Decision Intelligence Engine shall evaluate user readiness.
Factors may include:
active illness, 
recovery status, 
competing interventions, 
behavioural capacity, 
major life events, 
travel, 
sleep disruption, 
training load. 
Readiness influences timing.
It does not alter the underlying recommendation.

Founder Principle
The same recommendation may succeed or fail depending entirely on readiness.

5.6 Opportunity Windows
Certain interventions possess optimal opportunity windows.
Examples include:
Improving recovery routines during periods of increased training.
Strength training recommendations following injury rehabilitation.
Repeat blood testing after an appropriate physiological interval.
Lifestyle interventions following completion of major life events.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall recognise opportunity windows where constitutionally appropriate.

5.7 Sequencing
Interventions shall not be treated as isolated events.
The engine may determine that:
Intervention A
↓
should occur before
↓
Intervention B
Example:
Improve sleep consistency
↓
before increasing training intensity.
Or:
Reduce alcohol intake
↓
before reassessing liver biomarkers.
Sequencing shall maximise cumulative health improvement.

Constitutional Principle
The order of interventions may influence their effectiveness.

5.8 Repetition
Certain interventions require periodic reinforcement.
Examples include:
Educational refreshers.
Repeat laboratory testing.
Behavioural reviews.
Progress evaluations.
Lifestyle reassessment.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall determine whether repetition remains justified.
Repetition shall never occur solely to increase engagement.

Founder Principle
Repeat interventions only when repetition improves outcomes.

5.9 Adaptive Timing
Intervention timing shall remain dynamic.
Examples:
Recovery improves faster than expected.
↓
Exercise intervention moves forward.

Illness develops.
↓
Training intervention deferred.

Confidence decreases.
↓
Recommendation returns to monitoring.
Timing evolves alongside understanding.

5.10 Timing Conflicts
Multiple interventions may compete for the same period.
Examples:
Weight-loss programme.
↓
Marathon preparation.
Or:
Improve sleep.
↓
Increase early-morning exercise.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall resolve timing conflicts before activating interventions.
Priority alone shall not determine sequencing.

Constitutional Principle
Interventions should complement one another in time as well as purpose.

5.11 Intervention Duration
Every active intervention shall define an expected duration.
Duration may depend upon:
intervention type, 
expected physiological response, 
behavioural objective, 
review interval, 
supporting confidence.

5.12 Explainability

6.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Decision Intelligence Engine determines the appropriate strength of an intervention before it is communicated.
Not all interventions warrant the same level of certainty or urgency.
The strength with which BioSense recommends an action shall always remain proportionate to:
supporting evidence, 
confidence, 
expected health impact, 
constitutional safety, 
potential consequences of inaction. 
Intervention intensity governs the authority of the decision.
It does not govern the wording used to communicate it.

Constitutional Principle
The strength of an intervention should never exceed the strength of the evidence supporting it.

6.2 Engineering Philosophy
Decision quality consists of more than selecting the correct intervention.
It also requires determining how strongly that intervention should be expressed.
Examples include:
A newly emerging observation.
↓
Suggestion.

A well-established behavioural pattern.
↓
Recommendation.

A persistent constitutional safety concern.
↓
Strong recommendation to seek professional medical advice.
Each represents the same decision architecture operating with different intervention intensity.

Founder Principle
Confidence determines conviction.

6.3 Intervention Intensity Model
Every Decision Object shall possess an associated Intervention Intensity.
Suggested constitutional levels include:
Informational
The objective is awareness.
No behavioural expectation exists.
Example:
Educational content.

Exploratory
BioSense proposes a possible opportunity.
Evidence remains limited.
Example:
"We're beginning to observe..."

Suggested
Evidence supports potential benefit.
Behavioural change is optional.

Recommended
Evidence consistently supports intervention.
BioSense believes the recommendation is likely to improve health.

Strongly Recommended
High confidence.
High Expected Health Impact.
Significant opportunity for improvement.

Clinical Discussion Recommended
BioSense recommends discussing findings with an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.
This remains guidance rather than diagnosis.

Safety Escalation
Constitutional safety pathways are activated.
Communication follows BioSense's Safety Framework.

Engineering Rule
Intervention intensity shall be assigned independently from intervention category.

6.3a Reconciling Category, Intensity, Priority and Lifecycle
Several taxonomies describe the same Decision Object — intervention category (3.3), intensity (6.3), priority group (4.9), decision state (2.8) and lifecycle state (9.3). A single crosswalk shall state how they relate and shall remove collisions: 'Safety Escalation' and 'Clinical Discussion' currently appear as both a category and an intensity and shall carry one meaning each. Category is what kind of intervention; intensity is how strongly it is expressed; priority group is when it competes for attention; state is where it is in its lifecycle. These are distinct dimensions, not synonyms.
Engineering Rule
One crosswalk relates category, intensity, priority and lifecycle; no label appears in two taxonomies with two meanings.
6.4 Determining Intensity
Intensity shall consider:
confidence, 
Expected Health Impact, 
intervention burden, 
intervention category, 
safety considerations, 
competing hypotheses, 
remaining uncertainty, 
constitutional constraints. 
No single factor shall determine intensity independently.

Constitutional Principle
Intensity emerges from balanced judgement rather than isolated metrics.

↓
Low Expected Health Impact.
↓
Moderate recommendation.

Moderate confidence.
↓

↓

The Decision Intelligence Engine evaluates multiple considerations simultaneously.

Even strong recommendations shall preserve:

transparency, 
explainability, 
constitutional humility. 
BioSense supports decision-making.
It does not direct behaviour.

Founder Principle
The user's autonomy is never reduced by BioSense's confidence.

6.7 Escalation Limits
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall not increase intervention intensity beyond constitutional authority.
Examples:
BioSense shall not:
diagnose disease, 
prescribe medication, 
instruct emergency treatment, 
provide definitive clinical conclusions. 
Higher intensity remains bounded by BioSense's constitutional role.

Constitutional Principle
Confidence cannot expand constitutional authority.

6.8 Intensity Evolution

↓

↓
Recommended
↓
Strongly Recommended
or

↓
Suggested
↓
Monitoring
↓

The Confidence Engine and Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore maintain consistent constitutional behaviour across equivalent scenarios.
This supports:

Engineering Rule
Equivalent evidence should produce equivalent intervention strength.

6.11 Explainability

Chapter 7
Competing Interventions & Decision Conflict Resolution

7.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Decision Intelligence Engine identifies, evaluates and resolves situations in which multiple interventions compete, conflict or cannot reasonably be pursued simultaneously.
Human health is a complex system.
Interventions that are individually beneficial may become counterproductive when combined.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore optimise coordinated intervention strategies rather than independent recommendations.

Constitutional Principle
The best health strategy is not the sum of the best individual recommendations.

7.2 Engineering Philosophy
Health optimisation is inherently multi-objective.
An intervention designed to optimise one outcome may temporarily compromise another.
Examples include:
Calorie restriction improving metabolic health while limiting muscle gain. 
Increased training improving fitness while reducing recovery. 
Aggressive sleep extension reducing available training time. 
Frequent blood testing improving evidence while increasing intervention burden. 
Rather than treating these as engineering failures, BioSense shall recognise them as expected characteristics of complex health optimisation.

Founder Principle
Health decisions should optimise the whole person, not individual variables.

7.3 Conflict Detection
Every Decision Object shall undergo Conflict Assessment before activation.
Conflict detection shall identify:
competing recommendations, 
mutually exclusive interventions, 
contradictory behavioural objectives, 
excessive cumulative burden, 
timing conflicts, 
safety conflicts, 
resource conflicts. 
Conflict detection shall occur continuously as Decision Objects evolve.

Engineering Rule
No intervention shall become active before conflict assessment.

7.4 Types of Conflict
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall recognise multiple forms of conflict.
Direct Conflict
Two interventions recommend opposing actions.
Example:
Increase calorie intake.
↓
Reduce calorie intake.

Resource Conflict
Both interventions compete for limited time, effort or attention.
Example:
Increase strength training.
↓
Increase sleep duration.

Physiological Conflict
Optimising one physiological system temporarily reduces performance in another.
Example:
Weight loss.
↓
Muscle hypertrophy.

Goal Conflict
The user's goals compete with one another.
Example:
Complete an ultramarathon.
↓
Maximise muscle growth.

Temporal Conflict
Both interventions require the same implementation window.

Safety Conflict
One intervention increases the risk associated with another.
Example:
Increase exercise intensity.
↓
Acute illness.

Constitutional Principle
Different conflicts require different resolutions.

7.5 Conflict Evaluation
Once identified, conflicts shall be evaluated according to:

confidence,

intervention burden, 
user goals, 
timing, 
reversibility, 
supporting evidence. 
Conflict evaluation shall remain explainable.

7.6 Resolution Strategies
The Decision Intelligence Engine may resolve conflicts using several constitutional strategies.
Prioritisation
One intervention proceeds.
The competing intervention is deferred.

Sequencing
Both interventions remain active.
Implementation order is adjusted.

Combination
Multiple compatible interventions are merged into one coordinated strategy.

Modification
One intervention is adapted to reduce conflict.
Example:
Reduce training intensity rather than increasing it.

Monitoring
Neither intervention proceeds until additional evidence becomes available.

Retirement
One intervention is permanently withdrawn.

Founder Principle
Resolving conflict should preserve as much health benefit as possible.

7.7 Coordinated Intervention Plans

Example:

↓

↓

↓

Each phase supports the next.
The entire plan remains one constitutional Decision Strategy.

Constitutional Principle
Long-term success often depends upon coordinated sequencing rather than simultaneous optimisation.

7.8 Goal Reconciliation
User goals influence conflict resolution.
However, goals shall never override constitutional safety.
Example:
User goal:

BioSense may:
modify the goal, 
recommend a safer timeline, 
reduce intervention intensity, 
prioritise long-term health. 
The platform shall support ambition without compromising safety.

Founder Principle
Goals guide optimisation. They do not suspend constitutional principles.

7.9 Opportunity Cost

The Decision Intelligence Engine shall explicitly consider opportunity cost.
Examples include:

Opportunity cost shall be considered during prioritisation.

7.10 Dynamic Reconciliation
Conflict resolution is not permanent.
As new observations, confidence changes and Health Beliefs evolve, previously conflicting interventions may become compatible.
Similarly, previously compatible interventions may later conflict.
Conflict assessment shall therefore remain continuous.

7.11 Explainability

Why was this strategy preferred?

7.12 Engineering Constraints
The Conflict Resolution Framework shall:

support multiple conflict types, 
optimise coordinated strategies,

remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable.

Chapter 8
Outcome Evaluation, Intervention Effectiveness & Closed-Loop Learning

8.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define how the Decision Intelligence Engine evaluates the effectiveness of interventions after they have been implemented.
Recommendations should not be regarded as completed when they are delivered.
Their true value can only be determined by observing whether they produced meaningful improvements in health.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore continuously evaluate intervention outcomes while preserving the constitutional separation between observations, understanding and decision-making.

Constitutional Principle
An intervention is complete only after its outcome has been evaluated.

8.2 Engineering Philosophy
Every intervention represents a hypothesis.
For example:
Improving sleep consistency will improve recovery.
The intervention itself does not prove the hypothesis.
Only subsequent observations can do that.
The Decision Intelligence Engine therefore evaluates outcomes using newly acquired evidence rather than assuming success.
This preserves the scientific integrity of the BioSense Intelligence Platform.

Founder Principle
Recommendations are hypotheses. Outcomes are evidence.

8.3 Outcome Evaluation Inputs
Outcome evaluation shall consume:
From ENG-001
Updated Health Beliefs 
Updated Discoveries

From ENG-002

Updated confidence 
Revised Explanation Objects

From ENG-004
Original Decision Object 
Intervention history 
Expected Health Impact 
Intervention timing 
Intervention intensity 
The Outcome Evaluation process shall not modify upstream engines.

Engineering Rule
Outcome evaluation consumes updated intelligence.
It does not reinterpret historical evidence.

8.4 Outcome Categories
Every completed intervention shall receive an Outcome Classification.
Suggested categories include:
Successful
Expected improvement observed.

Partially Successful
Some intended improvements observed.
Further optimisation appropriate.

Neutral
No meaningful physiological change observed.

Inconclusive
Insufficient evidence available.
Further observation required.

Unexpected Outcome
Physiological changes differed from expectations.
Further investigation appropriate.

Adverse Outcome
The intervention appears associated with undesirable physiological or behavioural consequences.
Constitutional safety pathways may be triggered where appropriate.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention deserves an honest evaluation, including those that do not work.

8.5 Measuring Success
Success shall be evaluated according to predefined Outcome Objectives.
Examples include:
Improved sleep efficiency.
Reduced resting heart rate.
Improved HbA1c.
Reduced alcohol intake.
Improved recovery.
Improved symptom burden.
Progress towards user goals.
No single observation shall independently determine intervention success.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall consider longitudinal physiological change.

Founder Principle
Success is measured by meaningful improvement, not recommendation completion.

8.5a Outcome Objectives Are Pre-Registered
To prevent success being judged after the fact, the Outcome Objectives against which an intervention will be evaluated shall be defined at decision time and stored on the Decision Object, before the intervention is delivered. Evaluation then measures the pre-registered objectives rather than selecting a favourable outcome retrospectively.
Engineering Rule
Outcome objectives are pre-registered on the Decision Object at decision time. Success is graded against them, never chosen post hoc.
8.6 Behaviour vs Physiology
Outcome evaluation shall distinguish between:
Behavioural Success
The user adopted the intervention.
Physiological Success
The intervention produced measurable biological improvement.
These outcomes are related but independent.
Example:
The user consistently sleeps earlier.
↓
Behavioural Success.
HRV remains unchanged.
↓
Physiological Neutral.
Understanding both dimensions improves future decision-making.

Constitutional Principle
Changing behaviour and changing biology are different achievements.

8.6a Causal Attribution Limits
Attributing a physiological change to an intervention is causal inference from observational data, where regression to the mean, natural history and confounding can masquerade as effect. Outcome evaluation shall be confounder-aware, shall attach a confidence bound to any attribution of change to an intervention, and shall not treat post-intervention improvement as proven effect. This limit is what makes the longitudinal learning of 8.9 safe to use.
Engineering Rule
Post-intervention improvement is not proven effect. Attribution is confounder-aware and carries a confidence bound.
8.7 Unexpected Discoveries
Some interventions may produce outcomes unrelated to their original objective.
Example:
Sleep intervention unexpectedly improves glucose regulation.
Exercise intervention unexpectedly improves mood.
Nutrition intervention unexpectedly reduces migraine frequency.
Such observations may contribute to:
new Health Beliefs, 
new Discoveries, 
revised Biological Identity. 
The Decision Intelligence Engine records these outcomes.
The Dynamic Health Model determines whether they become understanding.

Engineering Rule
Unexpected outcomes create opportunities for learning, not automatic conclusions.

8.8 Feedback Without Contamination
This is one of the most important constitutional safeguards.
Intervention outcomes shall influence BioSense only through newly observed evidence.
The intervention itself shall never become supporting evidence.
Correct sequence:
Intervention
↓
New observations
↓
Observation Engine
↓
Confidence Engine
↓
Updated understanding
Incorrect sequence:
Intervention
↓
Assume improvement
↓
Increase confidence
This behaviour is constitutionally prohibited.

Constitutional Principle
Outcomes influence understanding only through evidence.

8.9 Longitudinal Learning
Repeated intervention outcomes may gradually improve future decision-making.
Examples include:
Certain interventions consistently succeed.
Certain interventions consistently fail.
Certain interventions perform better under specific physiological conditions.
The Decision Intelligence Engine may refine future prioritisation using validated longitudinal outcome analysis.
Historical Decision Objects remain unchanged.

Founder Principle
BioSense should learn from outcomes without rewriting history.

8.9a Governance of Longitudinal Learning
The learning that refines future prioritisation from outcomes is powerful and can quietly optimise for the wrong thing, so it is governed. It shall guard explicitly against adherence bias (easy interventions appearing successful, contrary to 4.4), selection and survivorship bias (outcomes existing only for interventions that were tried) and confounding (per 8.6a). Learned adjustments to the Expected Health Impact methodology or to prioritisation shall be validated and require clinical and data-science sign-off before they affect live decisions.
This governs the prioritisation model; it does not relax 8.8. The contamination boundary of 8.8 — an intervention never becomes evidence — remains absolute.
Engineering Rule
Outcome-driven learning is bias-controlled, calibrated and human-signed-off before it changes prioritisation. 8.8's contamination rule is never relaxed.
8.10 Intervention Retirement
Outcome evaluation may result in:
Continuation.
Modification.
Re-prioritisation.
Suspension.
Retirement.
Retired interventions remain fully auditable.
Retirement does not imply failure.
It reflects the current state of understanding.

8.11 Explainability
Every outcome evaluation shall answer:
What was the original objective? 
Was behavioural adoption achieved? 
Was physiological improvement observed? 
Which observations support this conclusion? 
Were unexpected outcomes detected? 
How will this influence future decisions? 
Does additional evidence remain necessary? 

8.12 Engineering Constraints
The Outcome Evaluation Framework shall:
evaluate interventions objectively, 
distinguish behaviour from physiology, 
preserve constitutional separation of evidence, 
support longitudinal learning, 
prevent evidence contamination, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable. 

8.13 Acceptance Criteria
This chapter shall be considered complete when:
✓ Every completed intervention receives an Outcome Classification.
✓ Behavioural and physiological outcomes are evaluated independently.
✓ Unexpected outcomes may contribute to future understanding through the proper constitutional pathway.
✓ Intervention outcomes influence future decisions only through newly acquired evidence.
✓ Intervention retirement is explicitly supported.
✓ Longitudinal outcome analysis improves future decision-making without rewriting historical decisions.
✓ Every outcome evaluation remains fully explainable and auditable.

Chapter 10
Constitutional Safety Framework & Intervention Guardrails

10.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to define the constitutional safeguards governing every intervention generated by the Decision Intelligence Engine.
Not every possible intervention should be delivered.
Even evidence-supported recommendations may become inappropriate when viewed within the wider context of constitutional safety, uncertainty, user wellbeing or BioSense's defined scope.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall therefore evaluate every intervention against explicit constitutional guardrails before activation.

Constitutional Principle
The ability to intervene includes the responsibility to recognise when not to intervene.

10.2 Engineering Philosophy
BioSense exists to improve health.
Not to maximise recommendations.
Not to maximise engagement.
Not to maximise behavioural compliance.
Every intervention shall therefore satisfy two independent questions:
Is this intervention likely to improve health? 
Is BioSense constitutionally permitted to recommend it? 
Both questions must be answered positively before activation.

Founder Principle
A good recommendation outside BioSense's authority remains an inappropriate recommendation.

10.3 Constitutional Authority
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall operate only within the authority granted by the BioSense Constitution.
It may:
educate, 
encourage healthy behaviours, 
explain biomarkers, 
recommend evidence collection, 
recommend discussion with a healthcare professional, 
support long-term health optimisation. 
It shall not:
diagnose disease, 
prescribe medication, 
replace clinical judgement, 
recommend emergency treatment, 
provide definitive medical conclusions. 
These boundaries are absolute and shall be enforced deterministically by the Deterministic Safety Rule Engine owned by ENG-010, not left to model judgement (see 10.4a).

Constitutional Principle
Decision Intelligence cannot exceed constitutional authority, regardless of confidence.

10.4 Safety Validation
Every Decision Object shall undergo Safety Validation before activation.
Validation shall consider:
constitutional authority, 
intervention category, 
confidence, 
Expected Health Impact, 
intervention burden, 
user context, 
conflicting interventions, 
known safety constraints. 
Failure of Safety Validation shall prevent activation.

Engineering Rule
No intervention shall bypass Safety Validation.

10.4a Safety Ownership — Deferral to the ENG-010 Deterministic Safety Rule Engine
Safety Validation here is not a second safety system. The deterministic safety rule engine defined in ENG-010 is the single safety floor: its rules are authored and reviewed by the medical-advisory function, and the Decision Intelligence Engine may raise caution above that floor but may never present anything below it. ENG-004's Safety Validation adds decision-specific checks on top of the ENG-010 floor, and the absolute authority boundaries of 10.3 (no diagnosis, prescription or emergency treatment) are enforced by that engine deterministically, not by model judgement.
On error or ambiguity the fail-safe default is to withhold or de-escalate the intervention and, where relevant, to escalate through the constitutional pathway — never to proceed.
Engineering Rule
There is one safety floor, owned by ENG-010's deterministic safety rule engine. ENG-004 defers to it and may only raise caution, never lower it.
10.5 High-Risk Interventions
Certain intervention categories inherently require greater caution.
Examples include:
Medical discussion.
Supplement guidance.
Major dietary change.
High-intensity exercise.
Rapid weight-loss strategies.
Repeated laboratory testing.
Such interventions shall require proportionately stronger constitutional justification.

Founder Principle
Higher consequence requires higher confidence and stronger governance.

10.6 Vulnerable Contexts
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall recognise situations in which standard interventions may require modification or suppression.
Examples include:
pregnancy, 
postpartum recovery, 
acute illness, 
recent surgery, 
childhood, 
advanced age, 
significant disability, 
major psychological stress, 
multiple concurrent medical conditions. 
Recognition of a vulnerable context shall influence decision-making.
It shall not independently generate medical advice.

Constitutional Principle
The same intervention may not be appropriate for every individual or every circumstance.

10.6a Deterministic Enforcement of High-Risk and Vulnerable Contexts
High-risk categories (10.5 — including supplement dosing such as iron, major dietary or exercise change, rapid weight-loss) and vulnerable contexts (10.6 — pregnancy, postpartum, acute illness, childhood, advanced age and others) shall be handled by deterministic guards in the safety rule engine, not by 'influence' alone. In a high-risk or vulnerable context, the affected interventions are suppressed or require stronger justification and, where relevant, clinical sign-off, per the ENG-001 clinical-governance and 1C regulatory boundaries.
Vulnerable-context signals are sensitive data with a named provenance — declared or inferred with confidence — handled within the 1C privacy boundary. Where a vulnerable context is uncertain, the engine errs toward suppression.
Engineering Rule
High-risk and vulnerable contexts are deterministic guards, not soft influences. Uncertain vulnerability errs toward suppression; sensitive signals are privacy-governed.
10.7 Uncertainty Safeguards
Where uncertainty remains substantial, the Decision Intelligence Engine shall favour:
observation, 
evidence collection, 
education, 
lower intervention intensity, 
delayed decision-making. 
It shall avoid presenting uncertain conclusions with unwarranted authority.

Founder Principle
When uncertainty is high, humility is a safety feature.

10.8 Recommendation Saturation
Excessive intervention can itself become harmful.
The Decision Intelligence Engine shall prevent:
recommendation overload, 
repeated low-value advice, 
competing interventions beyond behavioural capacity, 
excessive notification-driven behaviour. 
The objective is sustainable health improvement rather than intervention volume.

Constitutional Principle
Too many good recommendations may collectively become a poor strategy.

10.9 Commercial Independence
Decision Intelligence shall remain operationally independent from commercial objectives.
Interventions shall never be prioritised because they:
increase application usage, 
encourage subscription renewal, 
promote commercial partnerships, 
increase advertising exposure, 
favour specific products or providers. 
Commercial considerations shall never influence Decision Objects.

Founder Principle
Recommendations exist to improve health—not business metrics.

10.10 Human Oversight
Certain Decision Objects may justify optional expert review.
Examples include:
Novel intervention strategies.
Repeated intervention failure.
Persistent safety conflicts.
Unexpected physiological deterioration.
Emerging scientific uncertainty.
Human oversight informs governance.
It does not replace evidence-based decision-making.

10.10a Mandatory Handling for Adverse and High-Risk Cases
Where the discretionary language of 8.4 ('safety pathways may be triggered') and 10.10 ('optional expert review') meets a genuinely high-consequence case, handling is mandatory, not optional. An Adverse Outcome (8.4) deterministically triggers the Clinical Adverse Event Governance workflow owned by ENG-010 (§8.6a), whose clinical content is governed by the Medical Advisory Function under Constitution 1B/1C; and novel, repeatedly-failing, or deteriorating high-risk cases require clinical review before continued intervention rather than optional oversight.
Engineering Rule
Adverse outcomes deterministically trigger the adverse-event process; high-risk and adverse cases require clinical review, not optional oversight.
10.11 Escalation Pathways
Where constitutional safety thresholds are reached, the Decision Intelligence Engine shall defer to predefined escalation pathways established within the BioSense Constitution.

Where ENG-004's requested urgency and ENG-010's computed level differ, ENG-010's level governs.

10.13 Engineering Constraints
The Constitutional Safety Framework shall:

manage uncertainty, 
remain commercially independent, 
prevent intervention overload, 
remain explainable, 
remain fully auditable.

Chapter 12
End-to-End Decision Intelligence Lifecycle

12.1 Purpose
The purpose of this chapter is to demonstrate the complete operational lifecycle of the Decision Intelligence Engine within the BioSense Intelligence Platform.
Previous chapters define the architecture governing intervention classification, prioritisation, timing, intensity, conflict resolution, outcome evaluation and governance.
This chapter illustrates how those components interact during a realistic longitudinal health journey.
The objective is to demonstrate that the Decision Intelligence Engine functions as a coherent, explainable and constitutionally governed decision-making system.

Constitutional Principle
Every intervention should be traceable from evidence to outcome through one continuous and explainable decision lifecycle.

For the purposes of this example we introduce a fictional BioSense user.
Name: Alex Carter
Age: 37
Alex has used BioSense continuously for two years.
The platform currently contains:
91 active Health Beliefs 
17 validated Discoveries 
8 Behavioural Identity characteristics 
5 Biological Identity characteristics 
Recent observations indicate:
declining sleep consistency, 
increasing alcohol intake, 
elevated resting heart rate, 
stable blood biomarkers, 
increasing work-related stress. 
The Confidence Engine considers these understandings well established.
The Decision Intelligence Engine begins its evaluation.

Improve recovery.

Increase exercise capacity.
The interventions become a coordinated strategy.

Constitutional Principle
Interventions should reinforce one another rather than compete for attention.

Closing Statement
The Decision Intelligence Engine is not a recommendation generator.
It is not a notification engine.
It is not a behavioural coaching system.
It is the constitutional authority governing how BioSense converts trusted understanding into purposeful action.
Every intervention begins with evidence.
Every intervention is justified through confidence.
Every intervention competes with alternative possibilities.
Every intervention is evaluated according to its expected contribution to long-term health.
Every intervention is reviewed after implementation.
Every intervention evolves throughout its lifecycle.
Every intervention remains explainable.
Every intervention remains accountable.
Through this architecture, BioSense does not seek to maximise the number of recommendations it delivers.
It seeks to maximise the quality, relevance and effectiveness of the actions it chooses to recommend.
The Decision Intelligence Engine therefore transforms trusted understanding into meaningful health improvement while preserving the constitutional principles of scientific integrity, user autonomy and lifelong learning.
